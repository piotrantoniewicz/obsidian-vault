I am begging you to start writing online.

Especially if any of these sound familiar:

1. **You feel your ability to think getting duller.** AI has made you work faster, but your brain feels a step slower every time you work without it.
2. **You know being known for your ideas is the best career moat.** But AI lets anyone publish 1,000 words in 10 seconds, so you’re not sure how to stand out.
3. **You’ve tried writing with AI, but you hated what came out.** It sounds just like what everyone is publishing, and you’d rather publish nothing than put out slop.

### If any of those sound familiar, keep reading, because this post might change your life.

Now, writing online has changed a ton since I [published my newsletter back on January 5th, 2020](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/dickie-s-digest-first-edition-219343).

### Back then, I was 24 years old working full-time as a hedge fund trader on Wall Street.

This position was my dream job. Or at least, I thought it was for the 5 years leading into it. But 12 months in, I realized this was not the life I wanted to live forever. I looked around at the people older than me and could not see myself living their lives in my 40s. There wasn’t anything “wrong” with the path they chose, I just knew it wasn’t for me.

This was a tough position for me to be in, since I was making $170,000 per year ($85,000 salary, $85,000 annual bonus). This was an absurd amount of money to me at the time, so there was no way I was just going to quit immediately. But I knew that over the long term, I did not want my time or my income to be dependent on someone else.

So instead of quitting, I needed to find a way to expose myself to opportunities. But I was not about to go waste my time at “networking events” with people I didn’t like. So after reflecting on the people I looked up to and admired, I realized they all had one thing in common:

### They wrote on the internet.

So rather than overthink it, I made a simple commitment: I was going to write and publish a weekly newsletter for 52 weeks. I gave myself permission to stop if it wasn’t working, but only after a year. Worst case, I ended up with a better understanding of the ideas I wrote about. And best case, I ended up on a completely new life trajectory.

And now, 6 years since I started on that journey, I can point every single good thing that has happened in my life back to that simple decision to hit publish on that first newsletter.

- Writing helped me better understand my views on the world
- Writing led me to make my first $1 online selling podcast summaries
- Writing helped me meet my business partner Nicolas Cole through a mutual connection that followed me on Twitter
- Writing helped me build a following of over 700,000 people across platforms, many of whom have become my friends
- Writing helped me meet my girlfriend, who I met at a cafe while writing my weekly newsletter
- Writing helped me build a business that eventually allowed me to leave my comfortable Wall Street job to run my own business
- And most importantly, my writing journey has played a role in helping 10,000+ other writers hit publish on their first post inside Ship 30 for 30

No matter your goal, writing on the internet can help you get there faster.

### However, the world of writing online looks quite different than when I started 6 years ago.

- There are more people publishing than ever before as people woke up to the value of having a following
- There is more “slop” on the internet than ever before, as AI allows anyone to publish 1,000+ words without thinking much about it (and quite frankly, without knowing what they’re talking about)
- There are better tools for getting leverage out of your ideas than ever before, which allow you to turn 1 post into hundreds of pieces of content without any drop in quality (as long as you write the original piece)

As a result of these changes, you need to take a different approach than the one I took back in January 2020. And to be honest, you need to take a different approach than just about anyone has taken over the last 5+ years, including some of the things I’ve personally recommended during that time.

- **You need to go all-in on writing things from your own experience, rather than the experience of others.** Curation content (where you collect others’ ideas) and commodity content (where you say things that anyone can say) are completely dead.
- **You need to publish those ideas on multiple platforms, as technology allows you to be everywhere without additional effort.** Focusing on just one platform made sense when it took a ton of time and effort to cross-post. But now, you need to be distributing your ideas in multiple places, since your ideal readers hang out everywhere.
- **And you need to invest in building your Digital Brain: a living library of your own stories, opinions, and lessons.** This creates a library that AI can pull from, rather than hallucinating or making up its own stories. AI should not produce any words you haven’t personally written yourself, which requires YOU to give it enough material to work with.

### The writers who adapt their approach are the ones who will capture all of the upside over the next 5-10 years.

Now, this isn’t going to be some massive overhaul, because not *everything* about your approach needs to change.

- You still need to start writing *somewhere* (which is the hardest part for most people)
- You still need to start writing *consistently* (which means publishing every day)
- And you still need to *improve your writing over time* (which means talking to your readers & learning what’s working and what’s not working)

Those are timeless principles that will always hold true, no matter how the competition changes or the tools at your disposal improve.
