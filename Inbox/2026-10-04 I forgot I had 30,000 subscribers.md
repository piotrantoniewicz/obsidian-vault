**A few months ago, I found a forgotten gem on my LinkedIn account.**

I was cleaning up my profile, and I rediscovered my LinkedIn newsletter (yes, the one you’re reading right now) *—* which had over 30,000 subscribers.

Quick history. I started it back when I ran Gong Labs and published it as a LinkedIn newsletter that lived on my profile. When I left the company, it came with me.

First order of business: I renamed it *Content Strategy Reeder*. I figured I would repurpose my Saturday newsletter there. So I did, but only for a couple of weeks. I never really committed. I got busy with (insert any excuse here). It did… fine. Then it fizzled out.

But a few conversations with folks at LinkedIn gave me a hunch that newsletters are about to get a lot more love. I can't prove it, but I always trust my gut. Plus, the LLM visibility is a perk.

So I decided to run a test.

1. **I renamed it** ***LinkedIn Growth*****.** Boring? Yes. Very yes. But I wanted an obvious, on-the-nose SEO-friendly title. It's part of what I sell, and nobody needs convincing that they want to grow on LinkedIn.
2. **I’m not creating any new content.** I recycled every issue from my content vault and the Saturday Reeder. If it’s about LinkedIn and still relevant, it got added to the calendar.
3. **Every edition included a free offer**. I decided to refresh my [*LinkedIn Growth Guide*](https://www.thereeder.co/free-guides/linkedin-growth-guide) (you’re seeing a pattern) as a quick solution because I wanted to see if this crowd would raise their hand for a lead magnet before I ever pitched them anything paid.

## The bet

I wanted to answer three questions:

1. Does this channel actually work?
2. Do my followers want long-form content on LinkedIn?
3. Can I convert subscribers?

**I didn’t have high expectations.** But it was a low-lift experiment with no downside. So why not test it?

## How I ran it (and measured it)

Three months, June 21 to September 20. Ten issues total.

I opened with three warm-up issues every couple of weeks. Then in August I went weekly with a series documenting my own LinkedIn rebuild in public. Two more issues went out in September.

Here's what I tracked:

- Reach: impressions and views per issue
- Engagement: email open rate and comments
- Growth: net new newsletter subscribers
- Audience quality: the job titles of the people subscribing
- Conversion: new Reeder subscribers from the lead magnet

What worked best:

- **A newsletter series beat tactics.** The rebuild series averaged about 9,500 impressions per issue and peaked at 10,953 with "The New Rules of LinkedIn are here. Now I'm tearing down my 100k account to follow them." The September how-to issues fell to 8,400 and then 7,400. People followed the journey.
- **Problem-led headlines won over benefit-led headlines.** Moving forward, every headline and graphic gets built around the single biggest problem the post solves.
- **The LinkedIn Growth Guide lead magnet was successful** at converting LinkedIn attention into Reeder subscribers.
- **Faces with expressions that match the headline perform best.** Kristin's read is that my face in the header drives clicks. I agree.

## How it went

The scary part first.

Launch day, I lost 21 subscribers. The next issue, I lost 45. Every launch day was net negative. Show that chart to a non-marketing exec, and they'd pull the cord.

But I knew who was leaving. The Gong Labs crowd, basically saying, "Nah, dude, I signed up for data-backed sales insights, not your LinkedIn journey." Fair enough.

Two days after launch, +53. The tailwind always showed up after the drop, and the churn leveled out faster than I expected.

The scoreboard:

- **89,487 LinkedIn impressions**, about 9,000 per issue
- **~18,000 recipients** per send, with a 32–35% open rate every single time
- **+315 new Reeder subscribers** straight from the lead magnet
- **The right people showed up:** CMOs, founders, content strategists, fractional CMOs, an old client, even a guy who used to be on my team
- **Leads: zero.** But that wasn't the goal.

Compared to what I expected, which was basically nothing, that's a win.

But the number that blew my mind wasn't on the LinkedIn dashboard.

Roughly 17–18k of my 32,960 subscribers get every issue sent to their inbox. That's INSANE, and it's so unique. It's the only way I can email people from LinkedIn. I don't own the email addresses, but it's the next best thing. There's no other way to get your LinkedIn content into someone's inbox.

**A LinkedIn newsletter is the closest thing to an email list you can build without ever asking for an email address.**

I also realized I'm sitting on a legit marketing distribution opportunity. And now I have a new channel for sponsored ad inventory. *Win-win.*

And now that the lead magnet’s been tested, I’m going to create new LinkedIn guides and tools every few weeks. **Next up**: a LinkedIn post grading tool that gives you recommendations on how to make your content grab attention.

## What this means for you:

You probably aren’t sitting on a sleepy LinkedIn newsletter with 30,000 subs.

But it might be worth starting one.

Because historically, LinkedIn has sent a one-time invite to your connections and followers when you publish your first issue. That launch blast is your biggest subscriber moment, so make issue one count.

Before you go hunting for the next shiny channel, ask yourself three questions:

1. **What am I already sitting on?** An old newsletter. A dormant YouTube channel. A folder of webinar recordings. A backlog of posts that crushed once and never saw daylight again. Most of us have more distribution and content than we're using.
2. **What's the cheapest way to test it?** I didn't write a single new word. Pick a timeframe, choose the 2–3 questions you want answered, and decide what you'll measure before you start.
3. **How will I turn attention into something I own?** Every issue had a free offer. That's how LinkedIn impressions turned into 315 new Reeder subscribers.

Then give it time. My analytics didn’t look great when I relaunched. But two weeks later, it looked like a new channel.

This summer I thought LinkedIn had stopped working for me. Turns out there was a ton of upside right in front of me.

Chances are, yours is right in front of you too. Go find it.
