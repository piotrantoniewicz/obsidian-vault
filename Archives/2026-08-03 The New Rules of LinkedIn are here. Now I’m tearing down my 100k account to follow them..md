## Here's what actually happened, with receipts.

**LinkedIn rolled out a new algorithm starting in 2025.**

Something DID change. It’s not just us going crazy. *Yay!!*

Those two "wait, what happened to my reach?" waves everyone felt? That was this getting rolled out and refined in real time, on top of us, with no announcement.

And here's why we were all so confused: LinkedIn didn't confirm any of it until March 2026. Your feed and reach were getting quietly reshaped for over a year before anyone said a word.

Then they finally showed their hand. On March 12, 2026, a LinkedIn engineer [published the most detailed account](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed) the company has ever given of how the feed actually works — a full rebuild from a network-based system to a relevance-based one.

It all comes down to this:

"Our new system... create\[s\] a more engaging, personalized, and timely Feed experience that surfaces genuinely valuable content, **whether it comes from your immediate network or professionals you've never connected with before**."

In other words…

## LinkedIn went from a social graph to an interest graph.

In plain English: the algo stopped asking *"who follows Devin?"* and started asking *"who cares about this topic?"*

If you remember one thing today, that’s it.

**The old way**: I post, it goes to my followers and connections first. They engage, it gets put in front of their connections, and up it climbs. The more followers you had, the bigger your launch pad. Follower count was the moat.

**The new way**: I post, and before it goes anywhere, the machine reads what it's about. Then it shows it to people interested in that topic — whether they follow me or not.

For your feed…

You're not seeing content because of who you know; you're seeing it based on what you care about.

For your reach…

When your 10,000 views became 3,000, that wasn't a punishment. That was the graph saying: of those 10,000 I used to show this to, I think only 3,000 actually care about this topic.

It used to be reach first, then resonance. Now it's resonance first, then reach.

I have roughly 100,000 followers. But that doesn't help me like it used to. A chunk of those people followed me for sales takes, or leadership stuff, or they're just randos. The graph knows. Big follower count doesn’t springboard posts like it used to.

Which means the “large follower count moat” doesn’t help me like it used to.

Scary? Yea, a little. But remember what I said up top — this isn't a punishment. It's an opportunity.

Because once you understand the new game, you can play it with purpose. While everyone else is still refreshing their analytics and blaming themselves or complaining, you get to go learn the new rules.

So here they are — the ones I’m rebuilding my entire strategy around.

## The New Rules of LinkedIn

![Treść artykułu](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D5612AQFpP9CioltyAA/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/B56Z.vLRCKGQAM-/0/1785350394282?e=1789603200&v=beta&t=SAVSTewOs7PXbG-SIwlyXjQFSi5T0Xl_gco2vkiakD0)

### 1\. Followers aren't the moat. Topic is.

8,000 followers and 80,000 now have roughly the same chance of being seen on any given post. It's about the topic, not the size of your list. If you've been waiting to "build your audience first" before you really go for it, that excuse just died. Which is great news if you're starting (or restarting) today.

### 2\. Your profile is the first thing the graph reads.

Before it distributes a single post, the machine reads your headline, your About, and your work history to decide what you're about. If your profile is like my garage — dusty and all out of order… you misfired before you typed a word.

Updating your profile is the easiest fix you can make today. Do it before you start pumping out more content. Use the words people actually search — keywords, not your clever coined terms. Nobody's searching for your hashtag or coined phrase.

### 3\. Pick two or three pillars and hammer them for 90 days.

Pillars aren't branding fluff or “dead.” Topic clusters are literally how your content gets sorted and distributed. This consistency is you teaching the machine what to file you under. Get super focused on those topics for three months. *Then* you earn the right to dabble.

### 4\. Write for saves, not just reactions.

Saves, comments, and dwell time are the currency now. Likes are weighted less than ever before. LinkedIn is cracking down on AI slop and rewarding what they call “knowledge density” — real, useful, keep-this-forever stuff.

Likes are applause. Saves are trust. Applause is good for dopamine rushes, but saves compound into authority. Formats that buy dwell time: carousels, dense infographics, video — and don't sleep on plain text. Highest floor, lowest effort. Just you and your thumbs /keyboard.

### 5\. Commenting helps you grow (if you do it right).

Now comments are training data too. The new graph watches who you engage with and who engages back. So skip "great post." Leave 5-10 real comments per day on the right people's stuff, and on your own posts. Reply with an open-loop question that pulls them into the conversation (and lurkers into commenters).

A LinkedIn leader told me: if you've got 20 minutes, spend 10 thinking, 5 commenting, 5 writing. Engagement isn't the afterthought anymore. It's half the job.

### 6\. Don’t worry about post length. Provoke a response instead.

Long or short doesn't matter. Did you make someone stop and *think*? Did you make them reply? A hot take on return-to-office gets you likes, and tells the graph nothing about who you are. Say something classifiable that earns a real reaction from the right person.

### 7\. Play the 90-day game — because almost no one else will.

Getting classified takes two to three months. Trust takes another beat after that to turn into anything real. And here's the opening: with the dopamine gone, a lot of people are about to quietly quit posting. No viral hit, no reason to keep showing up.

So consistency isn't a virtue right now — it's the entire mechanism. The people who keep going while everyone else rage-quits are the ones who win the new graph.
