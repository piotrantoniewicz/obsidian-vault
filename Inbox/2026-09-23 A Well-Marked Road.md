***Clear lines and built-in guardrails help people use AI with more confidence***

### TEAMS! Newsletter | AI | Technology Edition | September 23, 2026

Think about the last time you drove a winding road at night. The painted lines showed you where to go. The guardrail on the curve told you a mistake wouldn't be the end of the story. Neither slowed you down. Because they were there, you could take the road with confidence.

That’s how I think about AI at AbilityPath. As our staff began learning these tools, what struck me most was their eagerness. They wanted to know how to apply AI to their work so they’d have more time to support people. That eagerness deserves a clear path, and building one is one of our most important responsibilities as leaders right now.

### The Idea in Brief

- ***The idea.***Governance and momentum are not opposites. Clear policy and system-level guardrails give people the confidence to use AI well.
- ***In practice.*** Clear policy, system-level guardrails, hands-on staff training, and a board that sets the tone.
- ***The payoff.***More time for the human work only people can do, supported by the trust of the community we serve.

### Tools that serve people

Our approach begins with a simple belief:

> ***AI and technology are tools that serve people, not the other way around.***

The landscape is changing fast, and with it have come real questions about trust. Watching those questions unfold hasn’t changed our philosophy. It has confirmed it.

In practice, decisions about people should be made by people. The tools can help us prepare, draft, and analyze. They don’t make decisions.

When technology serves a purpose, every decision about it has a simple test: does this help someone we serve? When it doesn’t, the tool starts setting the agenda, and an organization ends up adapting to whatever is newest. I’d rather we stay in the driver’s seat.

### Trust is what we carry

The people and families we serve entrust us with some of the most personal information in their lives. Everything we do with technology is on behalf of that community, and their trust is paramount. We treat safe use and data protection as commitments to honor, not compliance chores to complete. As we prepared for our rollout, we evaluated five platforms for HIPAA-compliant use because the standard we owe people doesn’t change with the technology.

### Painted lines and steel rails

A good policy does two things. It sets a clear line on practices, so everyone knows what’s expected. It also gives people a reference point when they face a decision the policy didn’t anticipate, which is how a policy builds confidence rather than merely limiting it. We paired ours with teaching, including a 15-day staff curriculum and an AI Prompt Playbook, so the line is something people learn to work with.

But policy depends on people remembering it on a busy day. That’s why we also build guardrails into our systems. They limit how AI can be used, and the tools can’t bypass them.

***Lines guide good choices, and rails keep a hectic day from turning costly.***

I wouldn’t want to rely on either alone. Designing protection into the system also shows respect for our staff, because it relieves them of the burden of getting every judgment right on their own.

### A board sets the tone

Confidence begins with governance, and governance begins at the top. Our board has a Board AI Governance Charter, an Ad Hoc AI Oversight Working Group, and we held AI fluency sessions to help directors ask informed questions. That tone matters to a program coordinator or a therapist deciding whether to try a new tool. When they know the board has thought carefully about the road ahead, they can drive it with confidence.

### For organizations building their own road

If I could offer one piece of advice to other nonprofit leaders, it would be to think seriously about governance and systems design and to build guardrails from the start rather than add them later. Know your obligations for transparency and data protection and treat them as part of the design.

Policy, systems, and governance are the visible structure, but they work because of something less visible: people who want to do this well. The eagerness I saw in our staff is what turns a well-marked road into one people actually travel.

A well-marked road doesn’t slow anyone down. It lets a whole team move at a confident pace because everyone knows where the lines are and trusts that the rails are in place. When staff know the boundaries, they can focus their energy on the work only people can do: listening, connecting, and supporting the people who count on us.

That is the promise of the Innovation pillar within Building to Belong, a cornerstone of our 2030 vision.

> ***Technology that streamlines work gives our staff more time for direct care and meaningful connection while honoring the trust of every valued friend we serve.***
