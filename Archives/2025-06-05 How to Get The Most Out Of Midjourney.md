## Quick Intro to Parameters

Real quick, before we dive into personalization, I do want to provide any Midjourney newbies with a 101 on the basics (feel free to skip ahead if you’ve used Midjourney a bit).

Specifically, I want to quickly explain **parameters**.

Explained simply, parameters are instructions you add to the end of your Midjourney prompts using two hyphens (--), followed by the parameter code and value.

It’s best explained with an example.

Say we want to change the aspect ratio (dimensions) of our image output. We would tag the *aspect ratio parameter* to the end of our prompt, like so: "a portrait of a man --ar 2:3"

- \-- = parameter indicator
- ar = aspect ratio parameter
- 2:3 = the value (2 width, 3 height)

This would give us an output with portrait-style dimensions 👇

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXdxQdv1m9U-v563ZfF_ybekuMfyQQO5MWzl4H600Uey-u-lZFsdXQCswzRNybLBWk22Bw3Lfk0YwPZlCh24hKzuF32Zl9axr4qj5bipmddFF7l1BkQ1vfzrPDtDj5F4chFUabAp?key=DlGuSrxQ6KcaScnZpjmNnA)

Midjourney has 23 parameters at time of writing. If you want to go down the rabbit hole, [here’s Midjourney’s guide to each and every one (and how to use them).](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32859204029709-Parameter-List?utm_source=moodboard.beehiiv.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=how-to-get-the-most-out-of-midjourney)

If I’m being honest, *I’ll only regularly leverage the same 2-3 parameters to get the outputs that I want*. And, for this edition, I’m not trying to overwhelm.

Start with aspect ratio and some of the personalization features we’re about to talk about - those two will give you a great foundation for further experimentation.

## ✨ Meta Prompting ✨

If you’ve read any other edition of this newsletter, you know I’m a sucker for a good meta-prompt.

Call it resourcefulness, call it laziness - I just want to ramble a half-baked idea into Claude or ChatGPT, and get a finely-tuned, optimized & detailed prompt that I can copy/paste into my AI tool of choice.

As you might imagine, my Midjourney workflow is no different.

Here’s a meta-prompt that you can use to turn your rambling ideas into detailed, optimized Midjourney prompts that you can try, tweak, and learn from:

You are an expert Midjourney prompt engineer. Your task is to transform casual scene descriptions into five highly detailed, optimized Midjourney prompts.

**USER INPUT:** ==\[Paste your scene description/ramblings here - describe your vision, mood, basic elements, and aspect ratio if you have a preference\]==

### Instructions:

When the user provides a description of a scene (including basic elements and aspect ratio), analyze their input and create exactly 5 distinct Midjourney prompts that:

### Core Requirements:

- **Maintain the user's intended scene and specified aspect ratio, or assign an appropriate aspect ratio if none is provided**
- **Add rich visual details** (lighting, composition, materials, atmosphere)
- **Include technical parameters** (camera settings, art styles, quality modifiers)
- **Vary approaches** while staying true to the original concept
- **Use proper Midjourney syntax** with only the aspect ratio parameter --ar

### Optimization Techniques:

- **Specific descriptors**: Replace generic terms with precise visual language
- **Artistic references**: Include relevant art movements, artists, or photographic styles
- **Technical details**: Add camera angles, focal lengths, lighting conditions
- **Material qualities**: Specify textures, surfaces, and atmospheric effects
- **Color palettes**: Define mood through color temperature and saturation
- **Composition elements**: Include framing, depth of field, and visual balance

### Output Format:

For each of the 5 prompts, provide:

1. **Prompt Number** (1-5)
2. **The complete Midjourney prompt**
3. **Brief explanation** of the approach/style focus (1-2 sentences)

### Example Structure:

**Prompt 1:** \[Detailed scene description\], \[lighting details\], \[composition\], \[style references\], \[camera/technical specs\] --ar \[ratio\]

*Focus: \[Brief explanation of this prompt's unique approach\]*

## Training Midjourney to your Taste

Alright, here’s the main course for today’s edition.

The beauty of Midjourney is that **it can learn your personal taste and apply it automatically to every image you generate.**

There are two main ways to do this:

1. **Training your “Global Profile”** - in which Midjourney learns your overall aesthetic preferences
2. **Moodboards** - in which you create specific style collections for different projects

They’re both awesome. It’s helpful, however, to start by tuning your Global Profile.

Thankfully, Midjourney’s web app makes this process very simple.

All you’ll need to do is navigate to the "Personalize" tab in Midjourney's web interface.

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXdQgIgelyGFOpKVxv8eLTRIbFLbHadjuEPsbfcPwbbJ0dI-vOhha_Z_qSK5N4txjKXctltBDQaWxq9pc_WFmCQunuh_sQjhUlx-KZrO11gJaN79UvKkRzvvEhdAZWPTwqw_xX8shw?key=DlGuSrxQ6KcaScnZpjmNnA)

From there, click "Ranked Profiles."

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXdtr4nDSJ25fWPAu_u-93Mc8ECtq0s92IpOu_WMOYhMIXa4MHds7O0E5u7trr9_wxP3ORC02ZaUs9x_NAI7-Ia7Cl03SwdWkBhRJ7Hd0M41Bi5_MCs4_vziDaV3aPi6WLrDD2zWLA?key=DlGuSrxQ6KcaScnZpjmNnA)

This should initiate an exercise where you’re shown 2 images.

To start teaching Midjourney about your aesthetic sensibility, all we’ll need to do is start choosing which image from each pair you like more.

That simple. Left, right, or skip.

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXcbbL__0glliZHFBYgoqNiIrgORe7vL4fQH4v2STuLVSKi1i5hGAmTVADXIL5gSg0Tegsaww_nU32k3HQx16ZFiI3Pmn5wqTZrEyZ3r9bp8taIsBOF75PydF7dmZnnQ62_qhwrJQw?key=DlGuSrxQ6KcaScnZpjmNnA)

Now, some of the options may be like comparing apples to oranges. Some may seem like comparing apples to bicycles.

The best advice I can give you is **don’t overthink**.

Keep it as simple as clicking the one your lizard brain gravitates toward. Midjourney will do the work on the backend to parse out your unconscious sensibilities.

The minimum floor for personalization within Midjourney’s V7 model is 200 rankings (meaning you have to make 200 of those ranked decisions before you can see what MJ has learned about you).

For me, this took about 7-8 minutes, and I had some fun in the process.

Do as many as you like, and feel free to come back to this same feature anytime you want to beef up your profile a bit more.

(You can also create additional personalization profiles if you want to start from scratch or aren’t happy with the results)

### Creating Project-Specific Moodboards

Okay, now let’s talk about building and utilizing a moodboard.

This is, in my opinion, **one of the most useful Midjourney updates ever.**

What this allows us to do is upload, link to, and/or select images that fit a particular mood/style/color palette/aesthetic/etc. and instruct MJ to reference the combined style of those images when creating outputs.

*I use the sh\*t out of this.*

I used it when styling/branding this newsletter, I use it when creating assets for clients, I use it to have fun and make cool art.

I love it, and it’s criminally simple to get started.

We’ll navigate back to the Personalize menu, but this time, we’ll click “Create+” next to “Moodboards”.

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXcDq7nUnAAOZmVbxuyzIjgrd4vVsZ1zDvhtnQ9UXf0F8JpzHCbhk76wNPhXk4Vp1STD8PLIW8m0vshJI60MGqXw4B7uClvG8xTw8ciz-LwfqvpPhCAiTwKQHLzO_ooUyIeO33xiNg?key=DlGuSrxQ6KcaScnZpjmNnA)

It gives us a few options. We can add images from a link, we can upload them directly from our computer, or we can select them from our Midjourney gallery.

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXdaYPcQ0izxmE4wdXA72BIlph2LPLH0uTqS1XK1_kBepV81uJJgkGAH3cPCpuycKgDiqKnwovK7jD1R5s-df_XOLYW8X6VALMJyXLoOsnTk7NamcE0iLx57j9dv_WfCyCsMyUcSkw?key=DlGuSrxQ6KcaScnZpjmNnA)

What I’ll typically do is start with uploads/links, try it out and experiment a bit, and then go back in and select the best outputs to reference for future iterations.

This way, I’m getting closer and closer to the aesthetic that I’m looking for with every iteration.

Once you’ve put together a collection of images that you’re happy with, run the same experiment that we did with our Global Profile: use the same simple prompt twice, once with the moodboard personalization applied, and once without.

## Mix It Up

In my opinion, **some of the best outputs occur when** ***combining*** **personalization methods.**

I’ll typically combine a moodboard with my Global Profile (by selecting both options from the “P” menu in the prompt bar), and it channels an aesthetic that’s tuned to the vibe of the moodboard but with my own unique taste applied.

You can also:

- **Reference multiple moodboards at the same time** - blending styles and inspirations to create a brand new, combined aesthetic.
- **Combine moodboards/your Global Profile with individual image references**. Simply drag/drop or upload an image to the prompt field with personalization enabled.
- **Find and try out other Midjourney users’ personalization codes.** Midjourney’s Discord community is massive, and you can use the web app to browse through other user’s creations.

The sheer depth of the customizability of Midjourney can get a little bit overwhelming if you’re diving in head first.

Start with your Global Profile and a moodboard or two, and experiment from there.

## “This Is Cool, But When Will I Use It?”

Okay, so now Midjourney has learned how to create images that we like. *So what?*

Outside of just having a good time creating cool artwork (and, honestly, I would say that about 90% of my time in Midjourney has been spent doing just that), **Midjourney is very useful for design, branding, and marketing exercises.**

Think about it. Once we’ve created a moodboard packed with images and style inspirations for our brand, we can create unified, aesthetically-aligned image assets with relatively simple prompts.

Landing page imagery, social posts, thumbnails, inspiration/starting points for AI video assets ([see my AI video guide from last week](https://moodboard.beehiiv.com/p/beginner-guide-to-veo-3), if you haven’t already, and take your Midjourney outputs to Veo 3 or [Runway](https://runwayml.com/?utm_source=moodboard.beehiiv.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=how-to-get-the-most-out-of-midjourney) to turn them to video).

***The only real limit is your own creativity.***

One note: while the aesthetic quality of the outputs and depth of personalization is much better, Midjourney is not particularly strong at text in the same way that ChatGPT 4o is.

In using MJ for work, I’ll typically create images that serve as *backgrounds* for more text-heavy assets, and bring them over to Canva to add the text that I want.
