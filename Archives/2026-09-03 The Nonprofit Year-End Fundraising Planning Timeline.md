It's that time again! Halloween costumes are on display in all the stores far too soon, but it’s not too soon at all for year-end campaign planning.

I know, I know. But the thing about year-end fundraising is that it rewards the organizations who start early.

So let's talk timeline. Where should you be, and when?

## Summer and Fall are Prime Time

I’ve written about how summer is nonprofit “prime time”, not for asking, but for warming up your audience. It’s a great time to share impact from the first two thirds of the year, celebrate earned media, educate supporters about an issue, or give them a free and easy way to take action. If you’ve done any of that over the last couple of months, kudos!

If you didn’t, there’s still time. You can still squeeze in some priming content in early fall before the ask-heavy messaging kicks in. Gratitude and credibility-building never go out of style.

## Starting NOW: High-Level Decision Making

This is where the real year-end planning season begins, and it’s exactly where my new “Done by December” cohort is starting this month.

Before anyone touches a donation page or drafts a single email, we’re nailing down four things:

- Goals. A primary fundraising goal, plus one or two secondary goals, like re-engaging lapsed donors, acquiring new ones, growing average gift size, or building your monthly giving program. Goals come first. Theme comes second. I see too many nonprofits fall in love with a catchy slogan and then try to force everything else to fit around it. Remember, the campaign is for your donors, not for you!
- Campaign theme. Once your goals are locked, brainstorm a theme and messaging that actually supports them, with your most loyal donors in mind as the recipients.
- High-level messaging and look & feel. Your case for support and visual direction. This is the DNA that every piece of content will be built from.
- A segmentation plan. This is a step people skip, and it’s the one that saves you the most time later. Map out in a spreadsheet who will get every email and text message, who might be excluded or receive a different version, and whether your existing donor list needs a different message than a cold prospect list. Do your major donors and monthly donors need something entirely separate? Hint: they often do. Plan this out now, before you’re deep in content creation, so you can write segmented versions of all your content in one swoop.

I go deeper on these initial planning steps, including goals, messaging, story collection, donation pages, and segmentation, in [my article on the first 5 steps to year-end planning](https://marketeronamission.work/the-good-stuff/year-end-planning-first-5-steps), if you want the full breakdown.

## Mid-to-Late September: Finalizing Goals, Messaging + Segmentation

Here's my recommended high-level schedule, and I say this every year because it works. Have your goals, theme, messaging, and segmentation plan fully locked by the end of September.

At least locked enough that your team isn't still debating the campaign name in the second week of October.

Why does this matter so much? Because October needs to be dedicated to building and testing your campaign donation page, setting up any new automations or integrations, and creating all of your campaign content.

If you're still workshopping messaging in October, you're eating into your production time; time needed for story collection, writing, designing, and ensuring a seamless donor journey.

## October: Donation Page Creation + Content Development

Once your foundation is set, October becomes execution month. That means:

- **Building a dedicated year-end donation page**, separate from your regular one, with campaign-specific visuals, a clear case for support, and suggested gift amounts that reflect what year-end donors typically give. (I've written a whole [checklist for a high-converting year-end donation page](https://www.idonate.com/blog/your-checklist-for-a-high-converting-year-end-donation-page) if you want the specifics.)
- **Testing that page** before it goes live: checking load speed, mobile experience, and making sure the form itself doesn't create friction.
- **Drafting your cornerstone content**: the direct mail piece(s), the big emails, the text messages, the ad copy… follow the segmentation plan you built in September so each version speaks to the right audience.

## November: Scheduling + Testing

By the time November hits, you should be refining, scheduling, and collecting those last impact stories to weave into your Giving Tuesday and December sends.

You should be scheduling all of your emails and texts and building out your ad campaigns. And you should be testing the donation experience from your campaign donation page, on both desktop and mobile, to ensure it’s easy and you receive the correct automated thank-you content directly after making a gift.

Early November is also the time to double-check that all of your tracking is correct (including UTM links when needed) so you’ll be able to see where donations are coming from when the campaign is live.

And for some organizations, your campaign might actually launch in mid or late November to get ahead of the rush!

## "Done by December" Really Boosts Revenue

I named my year-end planning cohort “Done by December” for a reason. I’ve helped dozens of nonprofits raise more in December by getting all the prep work finished and mailers sent to the printer by early or mid November so December is free for stewardship and optimization. Engaging with your supporters, celebrating gifts as they come in, tracking results, and adjusting campaigns as needed.

A year-end campaign is a living, breathing thing. You need time and space in December to pay attention to what’s happening so you can add, remove, and shift.

That might look like sending a couple of additional emails or texts. Tweaking subject lines based on what works best earlier in the month. Adjusting ad campaign audiences, creatives, or spend. Layering in some phone calls or retargeting.

If you're building your own timeline this year, my advice is simple. Use September for foundational decisions, October for content production, November for scheduling and testing, and December for relationship-building and optimizing.

Here's to a fall well spent, so your December can be less stressful and more, dare I say, fun.
