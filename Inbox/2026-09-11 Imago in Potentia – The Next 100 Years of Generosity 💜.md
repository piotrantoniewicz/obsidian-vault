### Marking Milestones

One of my favorite books is [*The Power of Moments*](https://amzn.to/3VJtjJw), by Dan and Chip Heath. The subtitle of the book is “Why Certain Experiences Have Extraordinary Impact.”  
  
Indeed.  
  
One of the most powerful insights from *The Power of Moments* is in how our memories work. People tend to assume that our brains are like hard drives, dutifully recording and cataloging each experience we have. Our memory of those experiences then becomes the average of all the things that happen to us – good, bad, and neutral.  
  
The problem is that the brain is nothing like a hard drive. The brain is flexible and fluid, and we are far more likely to remember four specific types of experiences – peaks, pits, milestones, and transitions. These remarkable moments tend to stand out in our memories.  
  
**Peaks** are the highest of highs.  
  
**Pits** are the lowest of lows.  
  
**Milestones** are significant events or markers along the journey.  
  
**Transitions** mark when one stage ends and another begins.  
  
When I speak about the power of moments, I talk about growing sustainable giving as part of a compelling journey for potential and existing recurring donors.  
  
Specifically, I encourage leaders to identify these four core moments and to invest in each:  
  
**🏔️ ELEVATE the PEAKS** – How can we take the best experiences and elevate them even higher? Make them more special and memorable?  
  
**🕳️ FLIP the PITS** – How can we not only fill in the potholes, but flip them and turn them into peak moments?  
  
**❎ MARK the MILESTONES** – How can we identify significant markers of progress and acknowledge them in a way that lasts?  
  
**🎉 CELEBRATE the TRANSITIONS** – How can we acknowledge the moments when one stage ends and another begins?  
  
**💡 Takeaway:** All elements of the journey are not created equal. To leverage the power of moments, focus on elevating peak moments, flipping pits, marking milestones, and celebrating transitions.  
  
Why does marking milestones matter so much? Because milestones don't mark themselves. If we don't stop to name them, they pass by unnoticed. And they're one of the few experiences we're actually wired to remember – the rare moments that let us look back and see how far we've come.

### Celebrating this Imagoversary

In Latin, “ *imago* ” is a word rich with meaning. Literally translated as *image*, or the root of the word *imag* ination, imago is a word picture of a future that will be, but does not yet exist.  
  
For example, a butterfly is an *imago* – it’s the final form of something that is still becoming. 🦋  
  
I believe that we are all on a journey of understanding who we were created to be – discovering our *imago*, as it were.  
  
Think about your own journey for a moment. What’s your *imago*? What’s that thing in the distance you are becoming? A friend said it to me this way this week – imagine your 80th birthday. Who’s there? What are you looking back and celebrating?  
  
I hope my 80th birthday is filled with stories of inspired generosity.  
  
At The Center for Sustainable Giving, we exist to help leaders see a future that will be, but does not yet exist – their *imago*. We do this by helping organizations grow sustainable funding by inspiring generosity.  
  
Specifically, we have developed a process to deeply understand each organization's potential for growing recurring giving and translate it into a roadmap to help them build the stable funding they need to accomplish their mission in a more reliable, sustainable way.  
  
**The Center for Sustainable Giving exists to equip leaders to grow generosity – within themselves, their organizations, and the communities they serve – fueling more sustainable and lasting impact.**

### IMAGO IN POTENTIA

At The Center, our motto is *IMAGO IN POTENTIA*. Translated literally:

> ***IMAGO IN POTENTIA* — "the image existing in potential, not yet in actuality"**

This is the essence of The Center for Sustainable Giving. The essence of our *imago* – helping leaders see a future that will be, but does not yet exist.  
  
We are all *imago in potentia*. We are all in the process of becoming. You and I individually, our organizations, and our communities are all works in progress.  
  
*IMAGO IN POTENTIA* draws a distinction between the Latin for *potentia* and *actus* – potential and action – where potentia refers to something in the future that at present exists only as a seed waiting to be realized, while actus is the fully realized reality.  
  
Before anything can come into being, someone has to see the potential. But it can’t stop there. They must move from seeing to believing, and then finally, to action. That’s what we help leaders do.
