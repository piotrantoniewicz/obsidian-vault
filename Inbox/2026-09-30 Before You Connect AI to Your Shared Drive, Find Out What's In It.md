Search your shared drive for the word "routing." Whatever comes back is roughly what an AI tool connected to that drive can find when someone asks it a question.

A colleague of mine found this out on a client project recently. Before letting an AI tool read the client's shared grant folders, they asked it to describe what the folders held without opening any files. It flagged one file as sensitive, called "wire transfer document." Every folder under that multi-year grant turned out to hold wire instructions, and the person who administered the drive did not appear to know.

Shared drives are organized around the job that created each file. For the finance person who needed them, the wire instructions made sense in that grant folder. An AI tool connected to the drive reads across every job at once. Copilot, Gemini, and ChatGPT or Claude connected to SharePoint or Google Drive all see the same files the person using them could already open, and they make those files much easier to find.

In August I shared the [policy we wrote for a real nonprofit](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-policy-we-approved-last-week-wendy-clow-pvigf/?trackingId=M9sr2YXeSQOKgU%2F2utGNRw%3D%3D), which recommended working from a separate folder. That works when you control how the tool is set up. More and more, the tool arrives already connected to the drive through a license or a feature in software you already use.

This week's article, the first one on my new website, walks through a one-hour check one person on your staff can run on the drive you already have: which five folders to start with, what to search for, how to list what is there without opening anything, how to see who can open each folder, and what to do with what you find.
