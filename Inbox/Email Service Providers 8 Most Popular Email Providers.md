## Comparing email providers: Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo Mail, and ProtonMail

When choosing an email service provider, it’s essential to consider both market share and features. Gmail leads with a significant market share, offering integration with Google Workspace and extensive language support.

Outlook, formerly Hotmail, is known for its robust business features and clean interface, making it a strong contender in the email market. Yahoo Mail, while not as dominant, provides ample free storage and integrates well with other Yahoo services.

ProtonMail stands out for its security, providing end-to-end encryption and adhering to strict Swiss privacy laws. Each provider has unique strengths, so consider what features are most important for your needs.

## Most used email service providers

If you want to know more about designing your emails to accommodate the most popular email providers, here’s a list of the biggest names.

## Gmail

Gmail by Google is one of the most popular web clients for email because it’s free and easy to use. As of 2025, Gmail has 1.8 billion users, supports 105 languages, and also offers paid plans for businesses of all sizes. [90% of startups and over 60% of mid-sized companies in the United States use Gmail.](https://www.demandsage.com/gmail-statistics/) The plan you choose determines the storage, support options, and features that are available to you. Many email marketers appreciate Gmail for its intuitive interface, email storage space, and integration with Google Workspace—connecting tools like Google Docs, Google Sheets, and Google Drive for cloud storage.

One of the best parts of Gmail is that it’s simple and works with virtually any device. Gmail is compatible with the most popular web browsers, plus it’s available on Windows and Apple computers and iOS and Android mobile devices. Gmail can also be installed on ChromeOS and other Linux-based operating systems. While Gmail offers excellent basic features, keep in mind it has an email send limit for free accounts, and maximum attachment size may vary if you use Google Drive links.

## Outlook

Outlook is an email client offered by Microsoft as part of the Microsoft Office package. In addition to this paid version of Outlook, there’s a free web version, which was previously known as Hotmail. With Outlook, users have access to a clean interface with great features, such as deleted email recovery, add-on support, and automatic email organization. Outlook is considered a secure email service for many business email service providers, offering advanced security features like virus protection, an integrated calendar, and contact lists.

Just like Gmail, Outlook works with several operating systems and devices, so it can be used across several devices and platforms. Linux operating systems are the only exception since there’s no official Outlook client for Linux. Outlook is user-friendly and can be integrated with many different apps, making it a popular choice for business and individual use. Its user-friendly interface and keyboard shortcuts can streamline everyday emailing tasks. However, advanced email marketing campaigns—like cart emails—typically require specialized marketing software, not just Outlook alone.

When it comes to [email deliverability](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-deliverability-why-it-matters-and-what-it-takes/) and widespread compatibility, Outlook and Gmail are two of the most popular options. Both can serve as an email hosting provider if you purchase custom domains or integrate them with your mail server, allowing for significant capacity and custom domain usage.

## ProtonMail

ProtonMail is renowned for its top-tier privacy and security, using advanced end-to-end encryption to protect every email. Unlike Gmail and Outlook, ProtonMail ensures that no one but the sender and recipient can access the email content.

Its zero-access encryption means even ProtonMail itself can't decrypt your messages, offering unmatched privacy. Based in Switzerland, ProtonMail adheres to strict Swiss privacy laws, making it ideal for users who prioritize confidentiality.

In addition to its security features, ProtonMail includes useful tools like Proton Calendar and Proton Drive, enhancing its utility beyond email. Although not as widespread as Gmail or Outlook, ProtonMail is user-friendly, requiring no technical knowledge to send encrypted emails.

Note that attachment size limitations may apply, depending on the plan, but its robust security offerings make it a standout choice for secure communications.

## AOL Mail

There was a time when AOL was the biggest name on the internet. While times have changed and AOL is now a division of Verizon Communications, AOL Mail is still one of the more popular email clients.

AOL Mail allows you to link to other email accounts, such as Outlook and Gmail, and provides an unlimited mailbox size. Virus and spam protection is built right in, too. AOL Mail’s address book and automatic forwarding can support very basic manual emailing, but it isn’t designed to handle advanced email automations for cart campaigns or larger-scale marketing workflows.

One thing to keep in mind with AOL Mail accounts is that they’re deactivated after 90 days of inactivity and deleted after 180 days of inactivity. You might want to check your mailing list to make sure you’re not emailing deactivated AOL accounts.

## Yahoo! Mail

Launched in 1997 and currently available in 27 languages, Yahoo! Mail is one of the oldest email clients on the web. While Yahoo! Mail began as a web client more than two decades ago, it has grown to the point where mobile apps exist for it. Yahoo email address domains include “@yahoo.com” and “@ymail.com.”

Yahoo! Mail is great for personal emails, especially for people who use other Yahoo services. Its interface may appeal to some email marketers, and Yahoo Mail typically offers ample free storage—so ‘limited storage’ isn’t usually an issue

While Yahoo! Mail isn’t quite as popular as Outlook and Gmail, the email provider has [225 million active users](https://market.us/statistics/web-search-engine/yahoo/). So, using [Inbox Preview](https://mailchimp.com/resources/making-the-most-of-inbox-preview/) to verify whether an email looks good for Yahoo! Mail is important for your marketing campaigns.

In fact, Yahoo Mail generally isn’t used for e-commerce email or cart campaigns, since it lacks the specialized marketing features and automation that these workflows require.

## Zoho

Zoho is an Indian technology company known for its suite of office applications, which is aptly called Zoho Office Suite.

While Gmail and Outlook are still popular choices for many businesses, Zoho is becoming increasingly popular—with [15 million users worldwide](https://www.zoho.com/blog/mail/heres-to-11-years-of-zoho-mail-and-our-growing-user-base.html) —which can be due to its clean [layout](https://mailchimp.com/resources/best-email-layout/), excellent security, and affordable premium plans for both personal and business use. Zoho Mail is known for a user-friendly interface, custom domains, and advanced security if you choose a paid package.

Depending on your plan, Zoho offers domain aliases, email hosting, offline access, email recall, attachments as large as 1 GB, and more. Some of Zoho’s key features include Zoho Campaigns, which can help build your email list, keep contact lists clean, and manage basic cart campaigns—though more advanced e-commerce triggers may need additional integrations.

If you’re looking for a feature-rich provider, Zoho is a solid option, but bear in mind you might encounter subscriber limits or limited storage on certain tiers.

## iCloud mail

iCloud has become increasingly popular as iPhones and iOS operating systems are big players in the tech world. As long as you have an account with Apple and an Apple device, you can connect to iCloud mail using your Gmail, Hotmail, Yahoo! Mail, or AOL Mail address. Even though iCloud is compatible with several other email providers, it still renders email differently.

While iCloud mail is a popular option because it comes preinstalled on iPhones and other Apple devices, many users may choose to download Gmail or other third-party email services. Still, iCloud mail can serve as a secure email service with strong encryption for private email if you use the built-in advanced security features.

## GMX

GMX is one of the email service providers you may have never heard of. There are two different versions of GMX email: one for German users and one for English, French, and Spanish users. The version you choose determines the features available with the free version of GMX mail.

With GMX mail, you’ll have access to features like virus and spam protection, drag-and-drop for files, and file-sharing capabilities with attachment sizes as large as 50 MB. GMX mail also supports [SMTP](https://mailchimp.com/resources/what-is-the-smtp-server/), POP3, and IMAP4. While GMX can work for small-scale emailing - though a 50MB max attachment limit may be restrictive - it’s not an all-in-one newsletter platform and doesn’t officially offer ‘unlimited emails’ or subscriber-based marketing plans. Primarily designed for personal use, GMX may suit very basic needs but isn’t geared for large-scale newsletter campaigns or advanced automation.

While designing your emails with GMX mail in mind is a good idea, remember that it isn’t quite as popular because it heavily relies on the use of pop-up ads.

Although it is not as well-known as Gmail or Outlook, Mail.com's extensive features cannot be overlooked. Mail.com is one of the most robust free email service providers available today for both personal and professional email accounts.

With Mail.com, users are not limited to one domain extension for their email address. Instead, users have the option to choose from more than 200 domain extensions for the perfect fit for their business and brand. This versatility can help address contact limits if your email marketing strategy requires multiple addresses.

While Mail.com is free of charge, allowing anyone to create an email address of their own, there is also a premium version of Mail.com available. The premium version of Mail.com eliminates ads, provides additional storage, and offers access to premium services.

Upgrading Mail.com removes ads and offers extra features, but it doesn’t include advanced automations or a form builder for lead capture—so there’s still no support for more sophisticated campaign types.

Just a few of the tools that are made available to all users who register an email account with Mail.com include:

- Organizer: Use a robust real-time calendar to manage contacts, schedule appointments, and even set reminders. You can also opt to connect and synchronize any other calendars you are using to manage your business or email account(s).
- MailCheck: MailCheck is an integrated feature from Mail.com that provides notifications whenever any of your email accounts receive new mail in real-time.
- Alias email addresses: In addition to registering a free standard email address with Mail.com, Mail.com permits users to create up to 10 email addresses within a single account.
- Storage: All users of Mail.com are permitted 65GB of storage for no cost.
- Filters: Sort emails with premade templates and filters or create your own to streamline the management of your incoming emails.

A few of the security features that are provided for all accounts (including free email addresses) from Mail.com include:

- Antivirus: Maintain peace of mind whenever you are using your email knowing that an antivirus is active and protecting your inbox at all times.
- Spam filter: The use of a spam filter goes well beyond putting a stop to annoying emails. Spam filters can also significantly reduce viruses and malware from making it to your inbox.
- 2FA: Two-factor authentication is also available and can be enabled to provide an additional layer of security to your email account when using Mail.com.

Whether you are searching for an email service provider that will allow you to create your own [email design](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-design/) templates or if you prefer safety and security when it comes to your email, Mail.com is a solid choice. Some email marketers rely on it for basic email marketing automation and the ability to handle emails per day up to certain email send limit thresholds.

### How to transfer emails from one service provider to another?

In order to transfer emails between email services or email providers, you’ll need to sign up for an email account with the provider you want to switch to. Once you’ve signed up for an account, you’ll need to repoint your email-related DNS records. After you’ve got that settled, you’ll need to set up IMAP and SMTP and check if your email address is working.

Transferring emails from one service provider can be difficult on your own, so consider using a tool that does it for you or requesting professional help.

### Does Hotmail still exist?

If you have been around since the expansion of the internet to the general public, you are likely well aware of the existence of Hotmail. However, you may be wondering if Hotmail still exists. While Hotmail in itself does not exist as a brand or as an organization any longer, it has been rebranded as one of the most popular email platforms on the planet: Outlook. Outlook today is what Hotmail once strived to be as far back as the mid-1990s.

Although Hotmail originally launched in 1996, its last official update came in 2005, when it could no longer compete with the rise of Gmail, Google's own email provider. In 2012, Microsoft officially launched Outlook, which allowed those who had a registered Hotmail account the option of upgrading their own accounts and transferring their data to Outlook.

While the original Hotmail platform no longer exists, Microsoft's Outlook is now a leading email service provider, competing directly with Google's Gmail for the top spot. Those who are interested can register a free email account with Microsoft Outlook within just a few minutes.

### What security features should I look for in an email service provider?

Choosing the best email service providers can feel overwhelming with so many diverse platforms and solutions available online. However, if security is your top concern, there are a few features to look for in your next email service provider, such as:

- 2FA: Two-factor authentication is becoming increasingly commonplace with most digital platforms today. Using two-factor authentication provides an additional layer of security to all email addresses used on a particular platform, minimizing the risk of leaving your email address vulnerable.
- Antivirus: When you are choosing an email service provider, it is recommended to find a provider that offers antivirus protection to all of its users, including those who have a free email account.
- Spam handling: Do you want to minimize the risk of receiving potentially harmful files, such as malware and viruses? If so, finding an email service provider with robust spam handling, filters, and protection is a must. With the rise of spam, it is imperative to use email service providers that understand the risk of allowing emails with harmful files to bypass any filters that are in place.
- Uptime: An email service provider with a 100% uptime record and a 99-100% guarantee rate is essential if you are searching for an email service provider that protects its users.
- Syncing/security: If you are looking to find an email service provider that offers the ability to synchronize all of your external email accounts, you should do so only with a platform you can trust. Always read reviews and testimonials of those who have used platforms for security purposes before choosing the free email service providers that are right for you.
