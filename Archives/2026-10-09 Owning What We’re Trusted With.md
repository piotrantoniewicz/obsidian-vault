### TEAMS! Newsletter | Stewardship Edition | October 9, 2026

***Why the matter of “sovereign AI” is not a concern for big companies alone, and what AbilityPath is doing to put our donor data on a firm foundation we control.***

A donor’s gift is really two things. One is the contribution itself. The other is the information they share with us: their name, their story and why they give, the programs close to them, and their history. We've never doubted how to steward the former. But in an era defined by AI, the latter demands equal, if not greater, care.

I was recently drawn to a new IDC research brief, ***The State of Sovereign AI Adoption***. Commissioned by Cohere, it put more than 500 AI decision-makers at U.S., Canadian, British, and German firms to the test, all with annual revenues exceeding a billion dollars. These are firms with deep technology budgets and AI leadership talent. The results were sobering.

### What the Big Players Are Telling Us

IDC defines sovereign AI as having actual control over the design, deployment, and governance of your AI systems and the technology that underpins them.In plain terms:

> ***It is a question of who is in charge of the data, the models, and the decisions.***

The numbers are telling. Of these large enterprises, only 13 percent feel their teams understand sovereign AI concepts well, and 12 percent say the same about the associated data risks. Only 8 percent say their roles and responsibilities are well defined. Then there is the fear of generative and agentic AI; some three-quarters of those surveyed put the threat of a privacy breach or data leakage at the top of their list of worries, *ahead of legal and compliance issues.*

This trend is picking up momentum. While 13 percent of respondents currently use prebuilt third-party agents, that figure is projected to reach 67 percent within a year! Agents do more than field questions; they act on data. If you do not know where your data is or who governs it, an agent will find out.

> ***If a company with a chief AI officer and a billion-dollar tech war chest can fly blind, a nonprofit cannot assume it is in good shape.***

### Why It Is a Nonprofit Issue

You might think “sovereignty” is a term for global banks and governments, and that most of us will not be building our own AI models. Yet the question is universal for any organization that holds personal information: Is our data scattered across spreadsheets and vendors, or do we have adequate control over it?

For us, the stakes are higher. Our donors give because they trust us, not out of obligation. Trust is the foundation of our relationships. On top of that, we hold sensitive information about the people we serve that is subject to HIPAA and state data management regulations. Our systems are often a patchwork, put together to meet an urgent need rather than built with data architecture in mind. This is how you get duplicate data, inconsistent data, and data leaks, and how an AI tool may draw on data sources it shouldn’t have access to.

The advice in the IDC report is also highly relevant to the nonprofit sector: have a clear vision with named ownership, build a shared understanding of your data and risks, and do the work to establish a solid data foundation. It is not about having a billion-dollar IT budget; it is about acting intentionally to steward and protect the data you have.

### A Unified Data Lake

AbilityPath has been built program by program and through many mergers over many decades, and our data structure reflects this history. Like many nonprofits, we store donor records in one system and case management and finance in other systems. For our development team, assembling a complete picture of our donors can sometimes take hours of manual work, made worse by duplicate or inconsistent data.

With the support of some very generous donors, we are changing that. As part of redesigning our marketing and development processes, we are creating an integrated data lake to consolidate donor and marketing information from all our programs and to build a governed data foundation. We want to respond to our supporters and community members in a more personal way without ever ceding control of the information they have shared with us.

We are approaching this with a few non-negotiables:

- **We keep control.** This is AbilityPath’s data foundation.
- **One trusted record.** A supporter should not have three partial profiles. One accurate record, with a person making the final call on any merges.
- **Sensitivity by design.** We treat protected donor information the same way, on a need-to-know basis.
- **Governance first.** We have an AI governance foundation in place, from our Board charter to staff training and documented protocols for the tools we use.

> ***We must earn the trust in the data before any automation is allowed to act on it.***

### Stewardship Runs Deeper Than Money

Data infrastructure is not a fancy “showpiece project” until the day it goes wrong. In the nonprofit space, failure is a loss of trust, and that is the currency everything we do is based on.

> ***Our aim is to have our teams spend less time looking for data and more time with the people who make our mission possible, so they can be sure we are worthy of their generosity and their information.***
