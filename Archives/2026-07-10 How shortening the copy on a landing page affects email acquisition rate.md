### Franciscan University

#### Experiment Summary

Ended On: 07/06/2026

Franciscan University has content offers that they run on Facebook on a consistent basis.

Landing pages are typically around 300-350 words, sometimes longer.

We wanted to test reducing the copy on these landing pages by 40-50% to see how it would affect the email acquisition rate.

This was tested for both desktop and mobile traffic.

#### Research Question

We believe that shortening the copy on landing pages for landing page traffic will achieve an increase in email acquisition rate because shortening the copy on the page while keeping the core message intact would increase the readability on the page, allowing the user to read through the page more quickly, encouraging the user to sign up.

#### Flux Metrics Affected

The Flux Metrics analyze the three primary metrics that affect revenue (traffic, conversion rate, and average gift). This experiment produced the following results:

0% increase in traffic  
× 13.9% decrease in conversion rate  
× 0% increase in average gift

#### Key Learnings

The key learning from this experiment is that shortening the copy on landing pages for Franciscan University's content offers led to a decrease in email acquisition rate. Despite the hypothesis suggesting that reducing the copy would increase readability and encourage more sign-ups, the results showed the opposite effect.

In future experimentation, it is important to consider the specific audience and their preferences. While concise messaging can be effective in some cases, it may not always resonate with the target audience. It would be beneficial to conduct further research, such as surveys or user testing, to understand which types of content and messaging appeal most to the audience.

Additionally, it is essential to continuously test and iterate on different strategies to optimize email acquisition rates. This could involve testing variations in copy length, design elements, CTA placement, and other factors to identify the most impactful combinations for driving conversions.

Overall, this experiment highlights the importance of data-driven decision-making and the need to carefully evaluate the potential impact of changes before implementing them at scale.
