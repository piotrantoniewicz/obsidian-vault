## Explore the real-time trends defining 2026

CONVERSION

### State of growth across global markets

Three markets grew. One stalled. The gap comes down to how donors are paying.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/2666x1500/bf628d78df/conversions-big.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Conversion rates grew in three of four markets Q1oQ1. The UK posted the strongest gain at +10.5%, followed by the US at +4.4% and Australia at +2.8%. Canada was essentially flat at −0.5%.

Conversion rates improved in three of four markets, with the UK posting the strongest Q1oQ1 gain. Mobile conversion growth outpaced desktop conversion growth across markets, narrowing the historical mobile performance gap. In the US, the mobile conversion rate moved from 20.6% to 22.7%, a +10.3% gain in Q1oQ1. The gap between mobile and desktop conversion is narrowing. That's directly connected to the payment method shift later in this report under **The Wallet Takeover.**

### Why this matters

Headline conversion rate doesn't tell the full story. The same market can show modest aggregate growth while mobile is accelerating sharply, because mobile still represents a smaller share of total traffic in some markets. Market- and device-specific comparisons get you closer to the truth than global averages do. More on that later in the report.

Canada's flat conversion rate is worth watching. It's the market with the lowest mobile donation share and the highest credit card dependence among the four (more in The Wallet Takeover below). That correlation is worth noting.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/66be8e0e48/conversion-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- If mobile conversion is underperforming on your platform, the gap is usually at the payment step, not the page design.
- Enable [express checkou](https://fundraiseup.com/features/checkout/) t as the primary option on mobile donation forms. Burying it below a card entry form keeps the friction you're trying to remove.
- Benchmark your conversion rate against your market, not a global average. UK and US trends tell different stories this quarter.

SOCIAL CHANNELS

### LinkedIn is attracting higher-intent donors

LinkedIn and Instagram are pulling ahead. YouTube is falling back. Treating them as one channel is leaving money on the table.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/2666x1500/cad3b6d172/cancellation-big.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Social conversion performance varied dramatically by platform and market in Q1.

In the US, LinkedIn and Instagram posted the strongest gains — up 21.4% and 14.8% respectively Q1oQ1. Facebook grew more modestly at +10.1%. YouTube declined −4.4%.

In the UK, Facebook surged +40.1% Q1oQ1, the strongest single-platform gain across any market in the dataset. Canada showed a similar pattern of Facebook-led growth. Australia saw a broad social conversion decline of −27.9%, consistent with broader structural headwinds in Australian giving this quarter.

LinkedIn's conversion rate growth in the US coincides with a growing share of new recurring plans sourced from LinkedIn traffic. The combination of stronger conversion and higher recurring-plan share suggests LinkedIn may be reaching donors with deeper commitment potential, especially in the US.

For broader context: Fundraise Up's Pulse of the Donor 2026 found that social traffic converts to recurring giving at approximately 2× the rate of email within the first 60 days. That aggregate figure gets more interesting when disaggregated by platform.

### Why this matters

Budgets set at the "social media" level are averaging together platforms that look nothing alike. A channel growing at +21% conversion and one declining at −4% need different strategies, not the same line item.

The recurring signal on LinkedIn is worth particular attention. If LinkedIn-sourced donors are enrolling in recurring giving at higher rates, the right metric isn't same-session conversion. It's a 60-day recurring enrollment. And from a donor experience standpoint, that likely means LinkedIn is reaching people at a point in their relationship with a cause where a deeper commitment feels right.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/e65adb2dcd/cancellations-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- Pull LinkedIn and Instagram performance separately from the social aggregate in your analytics.
- Calculate recurring plan share at 60 days for LinkedIn-sourced donors. If your current reporting doesn't support this segmentation, build it before Q3 paid social campaigns launch.
- For organizations with active sustainer programs, test LinkedIn as a higher-consideration donor channel, and measure 60-day sustainer enrollment, gift amount, and acquisition cost together.
- [AI-suggested amounts](https://dataro.io/blog/fundraise-up-ai-ask-amounts-improve-2026) calibrated by traffic source can present different default gift amounts for LinkedIn vs. Facebook vs. organic traffic, where donor propensity differs.

MAIL

### Mail is earning a second look

Mail has the highest conversion rate in the dataset — in every single market. But, the channel alone isn’t the advantage.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/2666x1500/d510ada5a7/channels-big.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Direct mail and email ("mail traffic") consistently outperformed all other traffic types on conversion rate in Q1 2026 across all four markets:

- Australia: **50.1%**
- UK: **52.1%**
- Canada: **42.7%**
- US: **36.8%**

Q1oQ1 growth in mail conversion was also strong: US +18.8%, UK +22.0%, Canada +17.5%, Australia +8.5%.

Mail and social showed the largest Q1oQ1 conversion gains of any traffic type — two channels that organizations often treat as competing budget choices were the top two performers simultaneously.

In the M+R 2026 Benchmarks Study, the findings found that email revenue was up +16% sector-wide, at $2.40 per subscriber — the highest return of any digital channel in that dataset.

### Why this matters

Mail’s advantage is not that the channel is magically better. It is that the donor has already been primed before arriving at the form. The donor has already read something, felt moved to act, and navigated to a form. The conversion gap isn't in motivation. It's at the payment step. A donor who opens a mail piece, decides to give, and then has to type a 16-digit card number on their phone is hitting friction the piece didn't deserve.

Mail conversion is disproportionately sensitive to checkout optimization, because the intent baseline is already high. Even modest improvements to the mobile payment experience should produce larger lifts for mail traffic than for cold acquisition sources. The hard work is done. Don't lose them at the last step.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/6440d7e33a/channels-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- If you're running direct mail or email campaigns, ensure the destination landing page is fully optimized for mobile — not just mobile responsive.
- Enable express checkout on forms linked from mail campaigns. The donor qualified themselves before clicking; don't add friction at the last step.
- Run mail and social media in parallel. Q1 data shows both channels in their strongest conversion position — they are not competing for the same behavior.
- AI-suggested gift amounts configured for mail donor context can increase average gift for high-intent traffic, where donors are typically more responsive to anchored ask amounts.

WALLET

### The wallet takeover

Credit card share is falling in every market. Donors are replacing it with native payments - giving just as much and converting faster.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/2666x1500/4d1ee5a782/wallets-big.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

The shift from manual card entry to wallet payments continued and accelerated in Q1 2026 across all four markets.

Mobile donation share crossed 50% in the US this quarter and grew in every market.

Canada may represent an earlier stage of the wallet-adoption curve: lower mobile share, higher credit-card dependence, and flatter conversion growth. The pattern is worth watching, but should not be read as causal on its own.

#### Two wallets, two audiences

As credit card share falls, it's being replaced by two wallets that look very different in the data. Treating them as interchangeable is the wrong read.

On mobile, Apple Pay gift amounts hold parity with credit card — and in some cases exceed it. For one-time donations, Apple Pay donors on mobile give 9% *more* than credit card donors. For recurring first installments, the gap is just -5%. That's not what you'd expect from a story about mobile donors giving less. And the trend is moving further in Apple Pay's favor: mobile Apple Pay one-time ACV (Average Conversion Value) is up 16% year-over-year. Recurring is up 6%.

Google Pay paints a different picture. On mobile, Google Pay donors give 22% less than credit card for one-time donations and 29% less for recurring plans. Mobile Google Pay recurring ACV fell 8% year-over-year. Google Pay is gaining share quickly — but the profile of those transactions is distinct from Apple Pay.

The channel data reinforces the split. Apple Pay's share is growing in mail (+28%) and organic (+25%) — channels associated with established donor relationships. Google Pay is surging in paid ads (+86%) and search (+76%) — channels where donors typically arrive with less organizational familiarity. Apple Pay's share in those paid channels actually declined year-over-year.

Taken together: the wallet that dominates your donation stream increasingly reflects how donors found you. A donor acquired through a mail piece is likely paying with Apple Pay. A donor arriving through paid search is likely bringing Google Pay. The optimal checkout configuration isn't one-size-fits-all.

#### The enablement question is settled

In the Fundraise Up dataset, 99.7% of iOS checkout sessions occur at organizations with Apple Pay enabled. 99% of Android sessions occur at organizations with Google Pay enabled. It's clear: nonprofits give donors the ability to use offer wallet payments when the option is available.

*It's important to note, according to* [*M+R Benchmarks*](https://mrbenchmarks.com/website-performance/)*, 57% of nonprofits have wallet payments enabled.*

That shifts the question. Apple Pay is iOS-exclusive; Google Pay is Android's native wallet. The device a donor uses determines which wallet they can access — which means your traffic's device composition maps directly to your wallet audience composition. The gap is no longer between organizations that have wallet payments and those that don't. It's between organizations that have optimized the experience and those that have the option buried below a credit card form.

### Why this matters

The framing matters here: donors are not "moving to mobile." They're moving to payment methods that remove the card entry step entirely. The phone is the device. Apple Pay and Google Pay are the mechanism. Screen size is just where it's happening.

Think about what that payment moment looks like from a donor's perspective. With Apple Pay: tap once, authenticate with Face ID, done. No form fields. No card number. The decision to give and the act of giving happen in the same breath. That's what a frictionless donor experience feels like. And the data shows it's becoming the norm, not the exception.

The M+R 2026 Benchmarks Study found that PayPal is available at 79% of nonprofits, Apple Pay at 57%, and Google Pay at 58%. Availability isn't the same as optimization. A wallet option buried below a card entry form offers the nominal choice while preserving the friction. Fundraise Up's Pulse of the Donor 2026 found that express checkout produces a 1.5% percentage point lift in one-time conversion. At volume, that adds up fast.

Canada's trajectory may be the clearest illustration of what the early stage of this transition looks like: high credit card dependence, low mobile share, flat conversion growth. UK and Australia are further along the same curve.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/2a92173efa/wallets-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- **Review where wallet options appear in your mobile checkout.** The data shows wallet payments are gaining share and matching or exceeding credit card gift amounts on mobile but only if donors can find and use them without friction. If Apple Pay and Google Pay are listed below a card entry form, the hierarchy is working against the payment behavior that's already happening.
- **Audit your checkout configuration against your traffic mix.** Apple Pay is growing fastest among mail and organic donors; Google Pay is surging in paid ads and search. If your traffic skews toward paid acquisition, make sure Google Pay is visible and accessible. If your program relies heavily on direct mail, Apple Pay prominence matters more. There's no single right answer. The mix depends on the channels your donors arrive.
- **Revisit your default gift amount suggestions** by payment method. Apple Pay donors on mobile gave 9% more than credit card donors in Q1 2026, and that gap is growing. If your suggested amounts are set uniformly across payment methods, you may be leaving headroom on the table with wallet donors who complete transactions in a single tap.
- **Test wallet donors as a recurring upgrade audience.** The low-friction nature of a wallet transaction is a meaningful difference from a manual card entry. Donors who just gave in one tap are a natural fit for an immediate post-donation ask.

GIFT AMOUNT

### The trend reverses

In 2025, donors gave more often but gave less per gift. Q1 2026 tells the opposite story.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/2666x1500/84abc6e003/gift-amount-big.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

One-time gift averages grew Q1oQ1 in three of four markets. Growth in Canada and the US exceeded 15%.  
  
Note: Absolute gift amounts are not published alongside prior-year benchmarks in this edition. Methodology differences between Q1 data and full-year PoTD figures make direct comparisons misleading. The Q1oQ1 directional changes are the meaningful comparison.

Sector-level data shows variation in gift amount trends. Social and Legal Initiatives organizations showed the strongest Q1oQ1 growth in recurring gift amounts, while International Affairs and Religion led in one-time giving growth.

### Why this matters

The 2025 Pulse of the Donor found a sector-wide trend toward smaller average gifts with more donors giving — a widening of the donor base at the expense of per-gift depth. Q1 2026 data shows that trend reversing in the US, UK, and Canada.

One quarter does not establish a trend. Average gift growth should be read alongside donor volume and conversion. A higher average gift is not always the same based on the health of your donor file. We’ll continue to monitor this trend in 2026.

If donors are genuinely giving more, the static suggested gift amounts on your donation form may not reflect that. Those defaults were likely set when donors were more cautious — and a suggestion calibrated to 2024 behavior is probably too conservative for a donor who's ready to give more today. It's worth running a test to see if higher suggested amounts perform better before the next major campaign.

Australia diverges from the group on both one-time and monthly gift amounts this quarter. External sector research from Australian fundraising organizations points to structural headwinds — a shrinking donor pool and increased acquisition costs — that likely explain the divergence.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/952x994/75e09d0a98/gift-amounts-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- [AI-suggested gift amounts](https://fundraiseup.com/platform/fundraising-ai/) produce a +10–15% revenue lift on average in PoTD 2026 analysis. If you're using static suggested amounts, Q1's gift reversal is a good moment to test dynamic AI-calibrated suggestions against your current defaults.
- Apply market-specific calibration. The optimal suggested amount differs materially between a US donor and a Canadian or Australian donor — global defaults underperform.
- For organizations with mobile-first acquisition, consider timing recurring upgrade prompts 30–60 days post-acquisition. Habits are established but the relationship is still early.

RECURRING GIVING

### The commitment signal

The channels converting best are also producing the most sustainers.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/2666x1500/c42d618513/giving-season-big.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Monthly gift amount growth mirrors the one-time trend: up in the US (+6.6%), UK (+8.5%), and Canada (+9.0%), down in Australia (−7.3%).

LinkedIn's growing share of new recurring plans in the US is notable alongside its conversion rate growth (Section 04). When a high-conversion channel also correlates with higher recurring intent, the acquisition economics improve materially.

Sector data identifies Social and Legal Initiatives as the strongest performer in recurring gift amount growth, suggesting that cause areas in this category are attracting donors with higher sustainer commitment — or that organizations in this sector are deploying more effective recurring ask strategies.

The M+R 2026 Benchmarks Study provides useful external context: monthly giving now represents **27% of all online nonprofit revenue**. Rights-focused organizations in the M+R dataset saw monthly revenue growth of +40% — directionally consistent with what Fundraise Up's Social and Legal Initiatives sector data shows.

### Why this matters

Recurring revenue is the stability layer under programmatic work. Acquisition spikes help. Sustainer pipelines sustain. The Q1 data suggests that the channels and sectors driving the strongest conversion growth (LinkedIn, Social and Legal Initiatives) may also be where sustainer intent is highest. For organizations in these categories, Q1 data supports a recurring-first acquisition approach.

But the deeper point is what recurring giving means from a donor's perspective. A recurring gift isn't just a better unit of revenue. It's the moment a donor moves from a transaction to a relationship. The experience of that first enrollment — how easy it is, how it's framed, what follows — shapes whether a donor sees themselves as a sustainer or just someone who gave once. That's a donor experience question as much as a product or messaging question.

From the Pulse of the Donor 2026: presenting a recurring option before the one-time gift completes converts at 2.4% in the US and grew +20% year-over-year. That's not a trivial number at scale. Most organizations haven't yet built anything into that moment at all.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/ce4d8d89a1/giving-season-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- Enable and optimize [pre-donation upsell](https://fundraiseup.com/use-cases/recurring-giving/). The friction of the recurring ask is lower before the gift completes than after. Catching donors at the pre-gift moment converts a meaningful share of intended one-time donors to sustainers without a follow-up campaign.
- Use AI-powered personalized ask amounts to reduce the recurring enrollment decision burden.
- Reduce passive cancellations through self-service tools. [Fundraise Up's donor portal](https://fundraiseup.com/features/donor-portal/) allows recurring donors to pause, modify, or reschedule — reducing churn without requiring staff intervention.
- For Social and Legal Initiatives organizations and rights-adjacent causes: Q1 data suggests donors are primed for sustainer asks. Consider leading with the recurring option rather than positioning it as an upgrade.

## Sector Snapshot: M+R vs. Fundraise Up

Two independent datasets. One direction.

#### M+R 2026 Benchmarks Study

180 nonprofits · US only · Cross-channel revenue and operations

- Online revenue: +15% year-over-year
- One-time giving: +17%
- Monthly giving: +12%
- Email revenue: +16% · $2.40 per subscriber
- Desktop: 57% of donation transactions, 72% of revenue

#### Fundraise Up Q1 2026

1,000+ organizations · US, UK, Canada, Australia · Transaction-level giving behavior

- One-time gift average, US: +15.4% Q1oQ1
- Mobile: 53% of US donations
- Apple Pay share, US: +38% Q1oQ1

#### Where they agree

One-time giving is up — two independent datasets, different methodologies, same directional finding. That agreement strengthens the signal considerably. Monthly giving is also growing in both datasets, though more slowly than one-time. Email and mail remain high-performing channels in both. Year-end continues to dominate annual revenue (M+R: 37% of annual giving in December), which underlines the importance of Q3 setup.

#### Where they diverge — a note for readers

M+R reports 57% of donation transactions on desktop. Fundraise Up Q1 data shows 47% on desktop (53% on mobile). This is not necessarily a contradiction. The two datasets represent different samples and methodologies. Fundraise Up’s data reflects transaction behavior among organizations using a platform optimized for digital checkout, while M+R reflects a broader cross-section of nonprofit digital programs. Organizations using platforms designed for mobile-first giving convert mobile traffic at higher rates, which shifts the transaction share distribution. The M+R figure reflects the sector-wide average, including organizations without optimized mobile experiences.

On TikTok: M+R reports TikTok return on ad spend at $0.04, the lowest of any paid channel. The Pulse of the Donor 2026 found TikTok has the highest recurring enrollment rate of any social platform in the US (18.7%). These findings describe different moments in the donor journey. TikTok's paid acquisition economics are weak. TikTok donors who do convert may exhibit strong sustainer behavior. Both things can be true simultaneously.

#### What this means

Used together, the two datasets produce a more complete picture than either alone. Agreement between independent sources on one-time gift growth — across different methodologies and different organizational samples — makes that finding meaningfully more reliable than either would be on its own.

### Five ways to put this data to work

The benchmarks in this report are only useful if you apply them. Here's where to start.

###### Audit your payment method hierarchy on mobile

Are Apple Pay and Google Pay the first options donors see, or are they below a manual card form? In every market, wallet payment share is growing. Credit card share is declining. Donors are telling you how they want to pay. The checkout experience should reflect that. Start with your highest-traffic mobile form.

###### Benchmark your own data against this report

Use the market-specific conversion rates, gift amount changes, and payment method benchmarks here to pressure-test your own performance. Are you above or below other organization’s conversion growth rates? Is your wallet payment share trending in line with the sector? Is your monthly gift average growing or contracting? Benchmarking your own metrics will help showcase where you can begin to make incremental improvements.

###### Build your recurring program before you need it

The best time to set up, test, and optimize your pre-donation upsell and donor portal is before your next high-volume campaign window. That's when one-time giving intent spikes and short-circuits sustainer conversion. Organizations that go into their peak season with a tested recurring flow consistently outperform those that add it mid-stream. Don't let urgency be the enemy of optimization.

### Conclusion: closing the gap between intent and impact

Donors aren't moving to mobile as a destination — they're moving to frictionless as a standard. The phone is where that happens. The tap-to-donate moment, where the gap between intent and completing a donation is closing, is what the data keeps reflecting back.

But the frictionless shift is only part of what Q1 showed.

Channel behavior is becoming more specific. Mail continues to strengthen as a channel alongside the digital shift through multi-channel efforts. High-intent donors who arrive from mail convert when there has already been engagement on social channels.

Social performance is fragmenting by platform: LinkedIn is showing early signals of higher-intent sustainer behavior in the US; TikTok's paid economics remain weak even as its recurring enrollment rate leads social.

The fundraising question is no longer just "how do we drive more traffic?" It's: are we set up to convert intent when it arrives, wherever it arrives from? That means wallet payments positioned as the primary option, not buried below a card form. It means channel-specific conversion measurement, not aggregate social benchmarks. It means recurring pathways optimized for the platforms where sustainer behavior is already emerging.

The data suggest that donors expect an experience that reduces friction, and accelerates their ability to make an impact. We’ll continue to monitor this trend throughout the year and share how nonprofits can close the gap between intent and impact.
