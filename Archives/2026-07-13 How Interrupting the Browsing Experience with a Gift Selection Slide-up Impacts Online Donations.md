### Luther Seminary

#### Experiment Summary

Timeframe: 03/19/2026 - 03/20/2026

Luther Seminary operates several high-traffic digital properties, and Enter the Bible is one of their most visited websites. Because of its popularity and return visitor traffic, the team designed an experiment to measure the effect on donor conversions from a web element that interrupted the user experience instead of relying solely on the passive solicitations of a donate button.

Historically, the site has relied on relatively passive donation opportunities, which do not actively interrupt or redirect user attention. This raises an important question: are visitors motivated to give when presented with a more prominent and immediate fundraising prompt, or does this type of interruption create friction that reduces engagement?

To explore this, the team is testing the impact of a more attention-grabbing on-site element: a gift selection slide-up. This element appears during the browsing experience and is designed to actively divert user attention toward making a donation.

The experiment includes two variations:

- Control: The existing website experience with no additional fundraising interruption
- Treatment: A site-wide slide-up featuring three predefined donation amounts and an additional “other” option

This test is being run across general website visitors to Enter the Bible, leveraging the platform’s high traffic volume to reach a large and diverse audience.

The goal of this experiment is to better understand donor motivation in a passive content environment. Specifically, whether introducing a more assertive, attention-directing prompt increases donation activity or disrupts the user experience in a way that suppresses giving.

#### Research Question

We believe that introducing a gift selection slide-up for website visitors on Enter the Bible will achieve an increase in donation conversions because interrupting passive browsing with a clear and immediate giving opportunity reduces friction and prompts action through attention capture and choice simplification because interrupting passive browsing with a clear and immediate giving opportunity reduces friction and prompts action through attention capture and choice simplification.

#### Key Learnings

This test demonstrates that a more interruptive, attention-grabbing donation prompt can drive incremental giving on a high-traffic content site.

From a behavioral perspective, the results suggest that capturing attention and simplifying the giving decision through preset gift options can prompt action, even when initial donor intent is low. However, the low overall conversion rate indicates that attention alone is not enough—stronger motivational framing will likely be required to drive meaningful scale.

Next Steps

1. Keep the Donation Experience Within a Single Domain: Future tests will prioritize directing users to donation pages within the Enter the Bible environment. Keeping the full journey within a single, first-party domain will improve tracking accuracy, reduce attribution gaps, and enable more confident decision-making.
2. Test Alternative On-Site Donation Prompts: Build on this learning by testing additional formats, such as a sticky donation slide-out, to understand how different levels of persistence and visibility impact conversion.
3. Strengthen Motivational Framing: Pair attention-grabbing elements with messaging that emphasizes urgency, impact, or donor identity to increase both conversion rate and overall revenue.
