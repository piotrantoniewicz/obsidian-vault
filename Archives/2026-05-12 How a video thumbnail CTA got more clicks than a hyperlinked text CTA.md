Experiment ID: #203651

### Bible League Canada

#### Experiment Summary

Timeframe: 04/07/2026 - 04/13/2026

Bible League Canada raises funds to provide Bibles and equip local ministry champions around the world.

As part of each campaign, a “Video Email” invites supporters to watch a short video that brings the need and impact to life before giving. Historically, this call-to-action (CTA) has been presented as a **clickable video thumbnail image**, but some recent emails have used **hyperlinked text** instead.

This raised a key question: Are donors more likely to engage with a visual video CTA or a text-based one?

To test this, we ran an A/B experiment within an Africa campaign email:

- **Control (Text CTA):** The email includes a hyperlinked text prompt to watch the video
- **Treatment (Thumbnail CTA):** The email features a clickable video thumbnail image

All other elements of the emails (story, structure, and the $10 = 2 Bibles matching offer) remained the same.

The email audience was split 50/50, and performance was measured over the 7 days following send, tracking unique email clicks to the donation page (primary metric) and donations (secondary metric).

#### Research Question

We believe that using a thumbnail image as the “Watch the Video” CTA for email subscribers and donors will achieve an increase in unique clicks to the donation page because a visual cue with a recognizable play button reduces ambiguity about the next step and captures attention more effectively than text alone, making it easier and more compelling for readers to engage.

#### Flux Metrics Affected

The Flux Metrics analyze the three primary metrics that affect revenue (traffic, conversion rate, and average gift). This experiment produced the following results:

24.3% increase in traffic  
× 0% increase in conversion rate  
× 0% increase in average gift

#### Key Learnings

We ran this test in email, then pulled results for unique clicks and directly attributable gifts in the week after the email went out. The results show that **including a video thumbnail image significantly increased engagement**. The treatment achieved a 9.5% click rate compared to 7.6% for the text CTA, representing a **24.3% lift in unique clicks, with 98% statistical confidence!** This confirms that a visual thumbnail CTA is more effective at driving traffic to the donation page in this context.

- Control: 2,497 unique opens, 190 unique clicks
- Treatment: 2,443 unique opens, 231 unique clicks
The thumbnail likely made it more immediately obvious that the link leads to a video, while also standing out visually in the email—reducing friction and making the next step easier to understand and act on. However, this increase in unique clicks **did not produce a statistically significant difference in donations**. While the treatment generated more gifts (9 vs. 6), revenue and average gift size varied, and the sample size is too small to draw conclusions about conversion or value.
- Control: 6 gifts and $907 in revenue, with an average gift of $151. Though this is skewed by a $400 gift. Removing that, the average gift was $101
- Treatment: 9 gifts and $710 in revenue, with an avg. gift of $79
**What we learned:** Visual video CTAs outperform text links for driving clicks. **What it means:** Donors are more likely to take the first step when the action is visually clear and attention-grabbing. **Implications:** Future video emails should prioritize thumbnail-based CTAs to maximize engagement, while continuing to test how to convert that increased traffic into donations.
