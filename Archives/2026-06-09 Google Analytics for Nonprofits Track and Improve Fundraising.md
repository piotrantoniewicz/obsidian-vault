Nonprofits and schools invest significant time and resources into their websites, email campaigns, and online fundraising pages. But without clear data, it can be difficult to know which efforts are truly driving donations and engagement.

That’s where Google Analytics (GA) becomes an essential tool. Google Analytics helps organizations track website visitors, understand supporter behavior, and measure the success of fundraising campaigns. With the right setup, nonprofits can see how donors arrive at their website, what content inspires them to give, and which campaigns generate the most support.

In this guide, we’ll explain how Google Analytics works for nonprofits, how to set it up, and which metrics organizations should track.

## What Is Google Analytics for Nonprofits?

Google Analytics is a free website analytics platform that tracks how people interact with your website. The current version, Google Analytics 4 (GA4), provides detailed insights into visitor behavior, including how supporters find your site and what actions they take once they arrive.

For nonprofit organizations, these actions typically include:

- Online donations
- Event registrations
- Volunteer sign-ups
- Newsletter subscriptions
- Petition signatures

Unlike for-profit companies that track purchases, nonprofits generally focus on mission-driven conversions, such as donations or supporter engagement.

By monitoring these actions, organizations can answer important questions like:

- Which marketing channels drive the most donors?
- Which fundraising pages convert the best?
- How do supporters move through the donation process?

## Why Google Analytics Is Important for Nonprofits

Many nonprofit teams operate with limited budgets and staff resources. Data-driven insights help ensure that every fundraising effort is as effective as possible.

Google Analytics removes the guesswork and helps nonprofits:

### Understand Where Supporters Come From

Analytics reveals how donors find your website. Sources may include search engines, email campaigns, social media, or paid advertising. This helps nonprofits focus their marketing on the channels that produce the best results.

### Measure Fundraising Campaign Performance

By tracking donation confirmations, organizations can see which campaigns generate the most contributions.

### Improve Website Engagement

Analytics data shows how long visitors stay on pages, what content they view, and where they leave the site. These insights can help nonprofits improve their website design and messaging.

### Justify Marketing Investments

When nonprofits can show how marketing campaigns translate into donations or event registrations, it becomes easier to justify spending time and effort on digital outreach.

### Make Better Strategic Decisions

Instead of relying on guesswork, nonprofit leaders can use data to plan future fundraising strategies.

## Setting Up Google Analytics for Your Nonprofit

Getting started with Google Analytics is relatively simple. The process involves creating an account, installing a tracking code on your website, and defining key actions you want to measure.

### Step 1: Create a Google Analytics Account

If you haven’t already, visit the [Google Analytics website](https://developers.google.com/analytics) and create a new account for your organization. You’ll then create a property, which represents your nonprofit’s website. Additional properties can be added for your fundraising microsites.

### Step 2: Install the Tracking Code

Google Analytics provides a tracking code that must be added to your website for the analytics functions to work. This code collects information about visitor activity.

Once the code is generated, you can install the code through:

- Your website plugins (WordPress or CMS platforms)
- Tag management tools like Google Tag Manager
- Direct code installation on their website

### Step 3: Configure Data Streams

A data stream collects visitor data from your website or app. For most nonprofits, the primary stream will be their website.

Once activated, analytics will begin collecting information about your visitors.

### Step 4: Enable Enhanced Measurement

Google Analytics is a powerful tool with hundreds of functions. GA4 can automatically track certain activities, such as:

- Page views
- Scroll behavior
- Outbound link clicks
- File downloads

These insights help nonprofits understand how supporters interact with their website content.

### Step 5: Set Up Conversion Events

Conversions are the most important actions you want supporters to complete on your website. Common nonprofit conversions include:

- Donation confirmations
- Volunteer form submissions
- Event registrations
- Newsletter signups

Tracking these events helps nonprofits measure campaign success.

## Key Metrics Nonprofits Should Track

Google Analytics offers hundreds of data points, and it can be overwhelming. Nonprofits should likely focus on the metrics that directly impact fundraising and engagement, at least to start. Here are some of the top metrics to track for your organization.

### Traffic Sources

Traffic source reports show where website visitors come from. Understanding traffic sources helps nonprofits identify which marketing efforts attract the most supporters.

Traffic sources include:

- Organic search
- Email campaigns
- Social media
- Paid ads
- Direct traffic

### Engagement Rate

The engagement rate measures how actively visitors interact with your website.

Higher engagement means your content resonates with supporters and encourages them to learn more about your mission. Google likes this because it means users are getting what they came for, and higher conversion rates can help boost your [nonprofit’s SEO](https://www.dojiggy.com/blog/seo-for-non-profits/).

### Conversion Rate

Conversion rate shows how many visitors complete an important action, such as making a donation or signing up for a newsletter. If your conversion rate is low (compared to industry standards), it may indicate problems with your donation page or brand messaging.

### Device Usage

Google Analytics also shows whether supporters access your site via mobile devices, desktops, or tablets. This is important because many websites receive more than half their traffic from mobile devices, making mobile-first technologies essential for fundraising success.

## Tracking the Donor Journey

One of the most valuable uses of Google Analytics for nonprofits is understanding the donor journey. The typical process includes several stages:

1. **Discovery** – A potential supporter finds your organization through online search, social media, or email.
2. **Engagement** – They explore your website and learn about your mission.
3. **Conversion** – The supporter takes a desired action: donating, registering for an event, or volunteering.
4. **Retention** – The donor returns to your website to learn more and support future campaigns.

Analytics helps organizations see where supporters drop off during this journey so they can improve the donor experience. For example, if many visitors view the donation page but fail to complete the form, the page may need to be updated.

## Advanced Analytics Strategies for Nonprofits

There is a big learning curve with Google Analytics, as there is so much to learn. Once the basics are in place and key staff have been trained, nonprofits can use more advanced analytics features to improve fundraising results.

### Create Custom Events

Custom events track specific actions on your website, such as:

- Clicking a “Donate Now” button
- Watching a campaign video
- Downloading an annual report

These events help gauge supporter interest before a donation is made.

### Track Campaign Links with UTM Parameters

UTM parameters are tags added to URLs that track marketing campaigns. Using UTMs ensures your analytics reports clearly show which marketing sources drive donations, making them very useful for organizations with limited budgets. Sources could include email newsletters, social media posts, and paid advertising campaigns (e.g., Google Ads).

For example, instead of just linking to [https://www.dojiggy.com/blog/best-peer-to-peer-fundraising-platforms](https://www.dojiggy.com/blog/best-peer-to-peer-fundraising-platforms), use a link with UTM parameters: [https://www.dojiggy.com/blog/best-peer-to-peer-fundraising-platforms?utm\_source=facebook&utm\_medium=social](https://www.dojiggy.com/blog/best-peer-to-peer-fundraising-platforms).

### Analyze Supporter Behavior

Advanced reporting tools in GA4 allow nonprofits to analyze the paths supporters take through your website and pages that lead to donations. These insights can guide website improvements and campaign planning.

## Common Google Analytics Mistakes Nonprofits Should Avoid

Many nonprofits fail to get meaningful insights from their Google Analytics campaigns due to common setup issues.

### Not Tracking Donations

If your donation confirmation page is not set as a conversion event, analytics won’t accurately measure fundraising performance.

### Missing Campaign Tags

Without UTM parameters, traffic from email or social campaigns may appear as direct traffic, making it difficult to evaluate marketing efforts.

### Ignoring Mobile Optimization

If mobile users struggle to complete donations, conversion rates will suffer. Regularly reviewing device data helps ensure your donation process works smoothly across all devices.

### Not Reviewing the Data

Analytics only provides value when organizations review reports regularly and use the insights to improve campaigns.
