It is a series of small changes to how your work already flows, made by the people who feel the friction, one task at a time. A few of you wrote back with the fair follow-up question: okay, so where do I actually start?

### What it actually is

Almost every AI tool you have tried is a chat box. You type a question, it types an answer, and then you copy that answer into wherever your work actually lives. Cowork is different in one specific way. It works inside a folder on your own computer. You point it at a folder, and it can open the files in there, read them, edit them, and create new ones. It does the multi-step task instead of describing how you might do it.

The examples are the kind of thing that eats a staff person's week alive. Turn a pile of receipt photos into a clean expense spreadsheet. Draft a report from a folder of scattered notes. Take a stack of documents that arrived in five different formats and compile them into one packet. If that last one sounds familiar, it is the exact copy-paste problem I wrote about last week, the one I watched a coordinator do by hand for every client who closed a file.

So the tool is real, and it is aimed at the right problem. But here is the part almost everyone skips, and it is the only part that decides whether any of this works for you.

### The step everyone skips

A capable assistant who knows nothing about you is not very useful. Picture a genuinely talented new hire showing up on Monday. If you hand them a task with zero context, no sense of your organization, your programs, your voice, or how you like things done, they will give you back something generic and slightly wrong, and you will spend more time fixing it than if you had just done it yourself.

AI is exactly the same. The reason most people try these tools, feel underwhelmed, and quietly stop is not that the tool is weak. It is that the tool has no idea who they are. It has never been told what the organization does, what the person is responsible for, or what "good" looks like in their world.

The fix is to spend twenty minutes, one time, teaching it. And the whole setup is built around one simple thing that trips people up more than it should: a folder.

### The setup, start to finish

This is five steps. The middle one is the part almost no one explains properly, so I am going to slow down there.

**1\. Open Cowork.** Download the Claude desktop app onto your computer. It runs on Mac, Windows, and Linux, and it needs one of the paid plans I mentioned. Open the app and find the switch near the top that flips between "Chat" and "Cowork." Click "Cowork." Chat is the familiar question-and-answer box. Cowork is the mode where Claude can actually reach into your files and do the work.

**2\. Make a folder, and understand what it is for.** This is the step I really want you to get, because the whole thing rests on it.

Cowork does not roam around your entire computer. It can only see and touch the folders you specifically hand it. So the first move is to make one folder that will be its workspace, the desk you are giving your new assistant.

On a Mac, open Finder, click Documents in the left sidebar, then go to the File menu and choose New Folder. On a Windows PC, open File Explorer, go to Documents, and click New, then Folder. Give it a plain, clear name. I would call it something like "Claude Workspace" or "AI Assistant." That is it. You have just built the room your assistant is allowed to work in.

Now put things in it. Anything you want Cowork to help with goes into this folder. If you want it to compile a packet, drop the loose documents in here. If you want it to draft your monthly board summary, put last month's numbers and an old summary in here so it can see how you do it. Think of it the way you would think about handing a capable new hire a physical folder of materials on their first day. What is in the folder is what they have to work with. What is not in the folder, they never see.

That boundary is not a limitation to work around. It is the safety feature, and it is the reason I am comfortable recommending this to you. Cowork cannot wander into your personal files, your finances, your donor database, or anything else on your machine, because you only ever gave it this one room. You decide exactly what it can touch by deciding what goes in the folder.

**3\. Point Cowork at the folder.** Back in the app, in Cowork, connect the folder you just made. There is a control to add or connect a folder, and once you choose yours, Cowork can read what is inside it, edit those files, and create new ones, all in that one place. You can also add a short note to the folder telling Claude what it is for, something like: "This is my work assistant folder. Write in a warm, plain style, and always ask me before anything gets sent externally." That note travels with the folder, so you never have to repeat it.

**4\. Let it interview you, and save the answers in the folder.** Before you ask it to do any real task, teach it who you are. Start a task in the connected folder and paste in something like this:

> Before you help me with anything else, I want you to understand who I am and how I work. Interview me. Ask one question at a time about my role, my organization, what I am responsible for, the tasks that eat the most time in my week, and how I like written work to sound. Keep going until you have a clear picture. When you are done, write it all up as a short, clearly organized notes file and save it here in this folder so we can both use it from now on.

Answer its questions in plain language. It will ask you eight or ten, one at a time. You do not have to be polished, and if typing is slow, dictate your answers out loud. ([Whispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai/) is a great app for that!) When it finishes, it writes a tidy notes file right into your folder. Now your context lives in the same room as your work. Every future task you run in that folder starts from a Claude that already knows your role, your organization, and how you like things done. You did this once. It remembers from here on.

**5\. Now give it real work, carefully.** Ask it to do one actual task using what is in the folder. Compile those documents into one clean packet. Draft the reminder email you send every week. Turn last month's numbers into a board-ready summary in your voice. You can keep Cowork in "ask before acting" mode, which pauses and shows you what it is about to do before it does it, so you stay in control while you build trust. It will always ask you before it deletes anything.

### Why this is the workflow change, not the tool

Notice what just happened. You did not run a rollout. You did not form a committee or buy a platform for the whole team. You made one folder, taught one assistant your job inside it, and now the copy-paste I described last week gets done while you do the work that actually needs you.

That is the shape of AI adoption that lasts. One person, one folder, one capable helper that knows the work, quietly handing back the parts of the week that never really needed them. Claude Cowork is a genuinely good tool. But the tool was never the point. The point is that you can now put your real work in a folder, describe it to something that will actually do it, and keep a firm hand on exactly what it can touch.

Make the folder this week. That one move, plus the twenty-minute interview, will teach you more about where AI fits in your work than any webinar will.
