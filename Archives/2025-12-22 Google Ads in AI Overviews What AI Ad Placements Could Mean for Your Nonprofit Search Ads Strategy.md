If you’ve searched for anything on Google lately, you’ve probably noticed something new at the top of your results: a neatly packaged AI-generated answer that seems to know exactly what you’re looking for. These are Google’s AI Overviews, and they’re quietly reshaping how people interact with search results.

For nonprofits leveraging Google Ads and/or [Google Ad Grants](https://wholewhale.com/university/amplify-your-impact/), understanding these new AI-driven features is crucial for maintaining and improving search ads strategy and performance. This article explores the emergence of Google Ads in AI Overviews and what it could mean for your nonprofit’s digital marketing efforts.

## Key Takeaways: AI Overviews + Nonprofit Advertising

- AI Overviews are Google’s AI-generated summaries that appear at the top of search results. **Google now places ads above, below, and within these overviews**.
- **Nonprofits don’t need to create new campaigns or opt in to advertise in AI Overviews**. Existing text ads from Performance Max and AI Max campaigns, as well as campaigns with broad match keywords, are automatically eligible if they’re relevant to both the user query and the AI Overview content, and (for the moment) suggest commercial intent.
- **AI Overviews answer questions without requiring clicks, which negatively impacts Google Ads clickthrough rates**. Nonprofits should focus on high-intent, action-oriented keywords and diversify campaign types to boost performance despite an expected decrease in clickthrough rate.
- Since users receive informational answers from AI Overviews, **nonprofit ads should emphasize taking action rather than providing more information**. The AI explains the problem; your ad offers the chance to be part of the solution.
- **Organizations should continue to publish unique, comprehensive, authoritative content to increase chances of organic citation in AI Overviews**. Do what AI can’t, emphasize your unique perspective, your organizational expertise, and your trustworthy resources.

## What are AI Overviews?

Google has introduced AI Overviews as part of its ongoing efforts to enhance the search experience. These overviews appear at the top of search results for certain queries, providing users with a comprehensive summary of the topic they’re searching for. Importantly, these AI-generated summaries can now include ads, presenting a new opportunity for advertisers to reach their target audience.

[AI Overviews](https://wholewhale.com/resources/generative-engine-optimization-adapting-seo-for-googles-ai-overviews/) are AI-generated summaries that appear at the top of Google Search results. They use Google’s large language models (LLMs) to provide quick, comprehensive answers to search queries by synthesizing information from multiple sources across the web.

When you search for something, you might see a boxed section with an AI-generated response that directly addresses your question, followed by links to sources and then the standard list of search results below.

![](https://wholewhale.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-3.png)

### How They Work

When a user enters a search query that triggers an AI-powered overview, Google’s AI analyzes vast amounts of information to create a summary. This summary aims to answer the user’s question or provide key information about the topic. Ads that are deemed relevant to the overview’s content can be displayed within or alongside this summary, offering a new placement option for advertisers.

### When They Appear

AI Overviews don’t show up for every search. They’re more likely to appear when:

- The query is complex or multi-faceted
- A comprehensive answer would be helpful
- The question is informational rather than navigational
- There’s sufficient quality content available to synthesize

They typically don’t appear for:

- Simple factual lookups (like “weather” or “time”)
- Queries where people clearly want a specific website
- Topics where the AI determines showing direct links is more helpful

## How Ads Appear in AI Overviews

Google now places ads directly within AI-generated summaries, creating entirely new advertising real estate. According to [Google’s official documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en), ads are eligible to appear above, below, or within AI Overviews on Search. Ads within the overviews themselves are currently available in English in the US on mobile and desktop.

![](https://wholewhale.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-2-1024x811.webp)

### Where Your Ads Can Show

**Ads Above and Below AI Overviews:** These placements are available everywhere AI Overviews exist (both in the US and internationally). Your existing text, shopping, local, or app ads from Search, Shopping, Performance Max, and App campaigns follow the standard auction ranking system and can appear in these positions.

**Ads Within AI Overviews:** This is the new frontier. Currently available in English in the US on mobile and desktop devices, these placements position your ad directly in the AI-generated content itself. Both text and shopping ads from existing Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns are eligible for these premium positions.

Note that Google doesn’t show ads within AI Overviews for sensitive verticals like adult content, alcohol, gambling, finance, healthcare, and politics.

### How Google Determines AI Overview Ad Placement

AI Overviews typically appear for complex queries or questions where information from multiple sources would be helpful, what Google calls “no one right answer” queries. Ads trigger within these overviews only when certain conditions are met, including detected commercial intent, and the availability of quality ads relevant to both the user query and the AI Overview content.

For nonprofits, this means your existing campaigns are automatically eligible, text and shopping ads that win the auction and address both the query and the information in the AI Overview can show within it. You don’t need to create new campaign types or opt in; if your ads are relevant, they’re in the running.

[Google’s documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en) provides a revealing example: when someone searches “why is my pool green and how do I clean it,” they might see an AI Overview with cleaning suggestions, and ads for pool vacuum cleaners appear because Google detects commercial intent in the user journey based on both the query and the AI Overview content.

For nonprofits, this means even informational queries about your cause area could trigger ads if Google identifies intent to take action. Someone searching “how to help protect whales in my area” might see an AI Overview explaining conservation approaches, with your nonprofit’s ad appearing as the clear next step to actually contribute to that work.

## What Makes AI Overview Ad Placements Different

What makes these placements fundamentally different is that your ad now competes with AI-generated answers, not just other websites. Users have just received what feels like a comprehensive answer to their question. This is where nonprofit missions have a distinct advantage: while AI can explain the problem of whale conservation, your ad offers someone the chance to be part of the solution. The AI provides information; your ad provides meaning and agency.

According to Google, internal data shows people find ads within AI Overviews helpful because they can quickly connect with relevant businesses, products, and services at the exact moment they need them. For nonprofits, this validates what we already know: when someone is exploring how to help with a cause, offering them an immediate way to take action (donate, volunteer, or sign up) meets them exactly where they are in their journey.

Google describes this benefit as “shortening the path from discovery to decision” and positioning your organization as “the clear next step.” AI Overviews decode complex user needs and emerging questions, enabling your ads to meet people in these previously inaccessible moments of high relevance.

## Optimizing for AI Overview Ad Placements

### Targeting Strategy

Google indicates that in order to receive placements in AI Overviews, advertisers must leverage [broad match](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12159290?hl=en), [AI Max](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15910187?hl=en) or [Performance Max](https://wholewhale.com/resources/performance-max-for-google-ads/). The platform matches ads based not just on the user query but also on the content of the AI Overview itself.

For Ad Grants users working within the program’s keyword restrictions, this presents both a challenge and an opportunity. You may not be able to serve display ads or access the search network inventory, but you can still optimize for these placements by:

- **Focusing on high-quality ad copy:** Your messaging needs to offer a clear next step beyond information. If someone just learned about ocean plastic pollution from an AI Overview, your ad should emphasize “Take action: support our ocean cleanup initiative” rather than “Learn about ocean plastic.” The AI already provided the learning, your ad provides the doing.
- **Optimizing landing pages:** Since Google’s systems need to confidently match your ads to these new query types, ensuring your landing pages are highly relevant and clearly structured helps the algorithm understand when your ad is the right fit.
- **Emphasizing action-oriented keywords:** While AI Overviews appear for informational queries, focusing on terms that indicate readiness to take action increases your chances of appearing when commercial intent is detected.

### Ad Extensions and Creative Elements

Ad extensions, in addition to images and logos, become even more valuable in this new landscape. They make your ads larger and more prominent, helping you compete with the visual weight of AI Overviews. Strong calls to action, emotional resonance, and clear value propositions are essential.

## Special Considerations for Google Ad Grants

If your nonprofit uses [Google Ad Grants](https://wholewhale.com/google-ad-grant/), you’re navigating an additional layer of complexity with AI Overviews presenting new challenges and opportunities.

### The Zero-Click Challenge

AI Overviews are designed to answer questions without requiring users to click anywhere. Someone searching “how to protect whales” might get a comprehensive answer right there on the search page, complete with statistics, approaches, and considerations. They never need to visit your website.

![](https://wholewhale.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-1-1024x772.webp)

This creates what digital marketers call “zero-click searches,” and they’re becoming increasingly common. For nonprofits, this means fewer people clicking through to your carefully crafted landing pages, even when your ad appears right below that AI-generated answer.

### Strategic Keyword Selectivity

The key is strategic selectivity, you need to be disciplined about [choosing keywords](https://wholewhale.com/resources/google-ads-keywords/) where you can maintain strong performance. Focus on high-intent, action-oriented terms. Make sure your ad quality scores are high by ensuring your ads are highly relevant to your keywords and landing pages.

Consider running multiple campaign types rather than putting all your Ad Grants budget into broad informational campaigns. Some budget should go toward branded searches, some toward high-intent keywords, some toward remarketing if you’re eligible. Diversification protects you if one campaign type becomes less effective.

Making use of Google’s updates to Ad Grant accounts, including [Google Map placements](https://wholewhale.com/resources/google-maps-placements-for-ad-grants/), [AI Max](https://wholewhale.com/resources/ai-max-for-google-ads/), and [Performance Max campaigns](https://wholewhale.com/resources/performance-max-for-google-ads/) where eligible, can help you access these new AI Overview placements while maintaining performance thresholds.

## FAQs: AI Search Advertising for Nonprofits

### Are Google Ad Grants eligible for ads in AI Overviews?

Yes, search ads from Ad Grant accounts are automatically eligible to advertise in AI Overviews if the campaign is leveraging Performance Max, broad match keywords, or AI Max is enabled.

### Where do ads in AI Overviews appear?

Ads can appear above, below, or directly within AI Overviews on both mobile and desktop, though placements within the overview itself are currently only available in English in the US.

### How should nonprofit ads adapt for AI Overviews?

Nonprofits should begin to shift Google Ad copy away from informational messaging to more action-oriented language, since the AI Overview already provides the explanation. Your ad should offer the immediate opportunity to donate, volunteer, or take meaningful action.

**Do I need to create new campaigns to advertise in AI Overviews?**

No. Your existing text and shopping ads from Search, Shopping, Performance Max, and AI Max campaigns are automatically eligible for AI Overview placements. If your ads are relevant to both the user query and the AI Overview content, and Google detects commercial intent, they’ll be eligible to appear within, above, or below the AI Overview.

**How can I get my nonprofit’s content cited in AI Overviews?**

Focus on creating comprehensive, authoritative content that showcases your unique expertise and perspective. Implement structured data markup to help Google better understand your content. Think less about driving clicks to individual pages and more about establishing topical authority in your cause area.
