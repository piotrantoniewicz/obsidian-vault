Experiment ID: #203889

### Word of Life Fellowship

#### Experiment Summary

Timeframe: 04/28/2026 - 05/13/2026

To increase the course sign-up rate for the Word of Life (WOL) Revelation Online Course, we tested treatment language designed to more clearly emphasize Dr. Ray Pritchard’s credibility, biblical authority, and teaching experience. Rather than focusing primarily on the course content itself, the revised messaging highlighted who Dr. Pritchard is, his background as a trusted Bible teacher, and the depth of insight he brings to the study of Revelation.

The goal of this approach was to build greater trust and confidence among prospective participants by reinforcing the value of learning from a respected and knowledgeable instructor. By positioning Dr. Pritchard as a credible guide through a complex and often intimidating biblical topic, we aimed to reduce hesitation, strengthen perceived value, and ultimately drive a higher course registration rate.

#### Research Question

We believe that clearly highlighting the professor’s credibility and authority will increase form submissions by strengthening trust and perceived value.

#### Key Learnings

The key takeaway from this experiment is that including credibility-focused language on the landing page had a meaningful impact on course sign-ups. The treatment variant generated a 16.2% lift in conversions at a statistically significant 95% confidence level, demonstrating that this audience responded positively to messaging highlighting the professors’ credibility, authority, and expertise in the biblical education space.

These results suggest that prospective "students" are looking for reassurance that the content they engage with is trustworthy, academically sound, and led by respected voices. By emphasizing the professors’ experience, theological backgrounds, and leadership in biblical studies, the landing page strengthened confidence and reduced hesitation during the decision-making process.

For future experimentation, we should continue exploring ways to communicate value through the professors themselves. This could include testing additional authority-building elements such as academic credentials, ministry experience, testimonials from former students, denominational affiliations, published works, or short video introductions from the professors. Leaning further into credibility- and trust-building messaging may help reinforce the perceived value of the courses and continue to drive stronger conversion performance.
