### TEAMS! Newsletter | Talent Edition | August 31, 2026

I’ve lost count of how many times I’ve watched a great candidate sit across from me, weighing our offer against a corporate salary that makes the math seem impossible. I won't pretend; the numbers rarely work in our favor. But after a decade of doing this work, I’ve learned something critical: the organizations that win those candidates aren't the ones making excuses for the pay gap. They're the ones who change the conversation. I know, because it moved me, after 30 years in finance and technology, to make the leap.

This isn't about ignoring compensation. It’s about leading with what most candidates applying for social services positions care about most: a meaningful role, a team, and a mission they can't find anywhere else. We’re never going to out-bid the market, but we can out-purpose it.

### What We’re Covering in Today’s Talent Edition:

- **Leading with impact, not apologies:** Rethinking how we frame and open job descriptions and initial interviews.
- **Hiring for alignment over aptitude:** Asking the right questions to spot shared values before looking at skills.
- **Offering a future:** Showing candidates clear growth paths inside our organizations.
- **Turning our mission into a promise:** Applying our core values directly to how we treat our team.

### Five Pillars

**Lead with the Mission, Not the Apology.** Stop burying the "why" beneath bullet points of daily duties and defensive disclaimers about pay. Put the real-world impact front and center. State the salary range clearly, state it once, and shift the focus to what that compensation package can't capture. The mission. The human impact. Making a difference.

**Interview for Alignment, Not Just Aptitude.** Skills are easy to teach; values aren't. Every interview should feature at least one question designed to reveal a candidate's underlying motivation. The interview framework should find someone who genuinely lights up when talking about the mission and how they would handle a tough day; it tells you far more than a resume ever could.

**Emphasize the Career, Not Just a Job.** Top talent leaves when they hit a wall. If a candidate can't see a clear trajectory from job coach to program lead or director, they’ll look for it elsewhere. Show people where they can grow from day one.

**Make Flexibility and Culture Tangible.** Vague claims like "we treat staff like family" mean nothing. Specifics do. "You won't have to miss your kid's school event, and here is how the team covers your caseload" actually means something. Put your real culture in plain English during the interview.

**Live Your Vision and Mission.** Vision and Mission should focus on the human and community impact the organization focuses on. This same standard needs to apply to how we treat our staff. Candidates immediately notice whether a mission statement is just framed wall art or a lived commitment to the community and to the team.

### An Actionable Playbook

- Rewrite open job postings to lead with purpose and community impact first.
- Add at least one core values question to every standard interview script.
- Draft a clear, one-page guide to your organization’s culture and perks.
- Clean up flexibility policies so managers can explain them clearly and confidently.
- Train hiring managers to state compensation directly, no hedging, no over-explaining.
- Weave your long-term vision into offer letters and final candidate conversations.

### Closing

We aren't going to win a bidding war, and we don't need to.

> ***The real goal is to show the right people the rewards of meaningful, mission-driven work. This is our strongest competitive advantage.***

I know, because it’s what brought me to social services, and after a decade, it is what keeps me engaged and energized every day after. Working for purpose with like-minded people.
