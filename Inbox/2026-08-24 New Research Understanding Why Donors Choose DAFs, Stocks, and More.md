Some donors are just built different.

Picture this: you get a ping at 11:47pm on December 31st, saying you’ve received a donation of $50,000 in appreciated stock, from a donor who’s ignored your small dollar email asks all year. That’s great! But also – weird?! What kind of donor behaves this way??

The truth is, this type of giving is becoming increasingly common. Non-cash giving isn’t the niche topic it used to be. It’s one of the fastest growing parts of the fundraising landscape, and most of us are still explaining it to donors like it’s 2015.

The numbers back this up. Donor-Advised Funds (DAF) revenue grew 153% year-over-year in 2025, according to [Chariot](https://www.givechariot.com/resources/toolkits/daf-fundraising-report). Stock donations increased 127% in that same period, with the average gift climbing past $51,000, per the [Giving Block](https://www.nonprofitpro.com/article/crypto-giving-hits-100m-but-stock-and-daf-giving-signal-a-broader-shift/). And donors who switch to non-cash channels like DAFs don’t just give differently — they give roughly 10x more than they did before.

So naturally, we had questions. Who are these donors? What’s actually pulling them toward stock and DAF giving instead of a credit card? And what would it take to attract more of them?

### 🪴So we started digging

This spring, we fielded two surveys: one to 400 traditional and alternative donors pulled from four participating nonprofits’ email lists, and one to 300 alternative-giving prospects — defined as active U.S. donors who manage investments, trade stocks, or use cryptocurrency online — sourced from an external panel. We asked what giving methods they’d heard of, what pushed them toward non-cash giving, and what actually drives how much they give.

A few things surprised us.

**Awareness isn’t the problem. The pitch is.**

Donors already know these giving vehicles exist. 81% are aware of planned giving, 68% know about donating stock, 66% know what a QCD is, 63% know what a DAF is. The information gap isn’t the barrier — for donors, at least. (Alternative-giving prospects, who aren’t yet donors to these organizations, are far less familiar across the board, especially with stock, QCDs, and DAFs.)

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image2.png)

Which means for existing donors, the job isn’t education. It’s activation. So if awareness isn’t what’s holding donors back, what is?

**Impact beats logistics, every time.**

When we asked donors what shapes how much they give, 68% pointed to which organizations need support most. Tax implications, market performance, advice from financial professionals — all of that ranked far lower in donors’ minds.

And yet, look at almost any DAF or stock-giving landing page, and you’ll find paragraphs explaining what a DAF is, what stock giving is, every step of the transfer process. It’s well intentioned — we want to do everything we can to demystify the process. Yet that wall of text can feel counter to what donors are telling us they want: easy. Convenient. Simple.

We have a messaging mismatch. Donors are asking for ease; our pages are answering with logistics.

**Plant these seeds:**

- Instead of *“Give through your DAF and receive tax advantages,”* try: *“This takes less time than weeding through your inbox.”*
- Instead of *“Planning your giving, sustaining impact, building generosity into your life,”* try: *“$50,000 in stock = a season’s worth of seeds, water, and shelter for a family starting over.”*
- Instead of a footer link that says *“Learn about other ways to give,”* try: *“You’ve probably heard of this. Here’s why it may take root for you.”*

**The financial advisor myth**

Here’s another assumption worth retiring: that it’s the financial advisors doing the heavy lifting. 27% of donors say a financial advisor or accountant drove them to give in a non-traditional way. But only 2% say that advisor shapes how much they give. Advisors get people in the door — they’re not the ones setting the size of the gift. That part is still on us.

It’s worth testing other “ambassadors” too: program staff, or even fellow non-cash donors, may carry more weight on the *how much* question than we assume. For one organization that participated in this study, videos or quotes from non-cash donors have been astronomically successful in recruiting other non-cash donors.

**Your current donors probably aren’t the growth opportunity here**

This one stings a little: 76% of donors already giving traditionally told us they’re happy with how they currently give — no real interest in switching. And donors already giving in a non-traditional way (DAF, stock, etc.) show almost zero appetite for adding *another* alternative method (93% not interested).

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image1-1.png)

But alternative-giving prospects — people who care about your cause but are generally unaware of all the non-cash options — are a different story. Roughly half are open to learning about QCDs. A third want to know more about stock giving or planned giving. If you’re looking to grow this slice of your donor pie, *these* are the people to start talking to.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image3.png)

### 🪴What this looks like in practice

Talking to donors is one thing. Turning it into a program is another. Here’s how the International Rescue Committee did it — moving from 21st to 9th nationally in total DAF revenue (per DAFGiving360) in a single year, taking DAF grants from $6.34M to $15.3M.

**The philosophy: audience-led, not channel-led.** The IRC knows that donors who give through DAFs, stock, or IRA/QCDs are measurably more likely to upgrade into major giving and to leave a legacy gift, so they don’t save that messaging for their mid-level or major donors alone. Instead, all donors are invited to give this way, from their very first gift onward.

**Non-cash as the default, not the exception.** DAF language shows up in stewardship messages, solicitation appeals, and organic channels. It’s not a special ask — it’s baked into the regular donor experience, all year long.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image4.png)

**A standing quarterly cadence.** Every quarter, a DAF/QCD/IRA appeal goes out across mail, email, and SMS simultaneously. Because these gifts often arrive without a trackable source code — the money comes through a third party, not a click — the team runs a matchback: cross-referencing gifts against DAF/QCD/IRA revenue in the months after the appeal, rather than relying on last-click attribution. Time consuming? Yes. But worth it to understand the path these donors take to making their gifts.

**Modeling to scale it.** IRC layers SIMIO, Blackbaud, and Windfall models to identify high potential, non-cash donors, then adjusts the ask accordingly: a general standard ask might be $50/$100/$250, while a modeled upgrade ask goes $1,000/$500/$250. Conversions get tracked back against model scores to keep refining targeting.

**The flagship moment: DAF Day.** When DAF Day was first announced, the team wasn’t sure how many donors would actually answer the call — October had historically been a pretty stable, unremarkable revenue month. So they built a coordinated push: a September postcard announcing a 2x match, followed by an October email and SMS series with a 5x match offer.

Donors showed up. In its most recent year, DAF Day drove $1.4M in October revenue across all IRC audiences — up 123% year-over-year — including $1.2M from mid-level donors alone, at an average gift of $2,659. And crucially, it hasn’t cannibalized EOY results — IRC is still meeting their revenue goals in November and December.

### 🪴Take stock of your own garden

Looking to grow more of these types of givers? Start with the place most people will actually go first: your website. Before you write another appeal, go look at your own DAF and stock-giving pages with fresh eyes and ask:

- Can someone find your DAF information in one click?
- Can they donate stock without calling someone?
- Is the process explained in clear, simple terms — or does it read like a legal disclosure?
- Does it feel simple? Does it feel fast?

If the honest answer is “kind of, but it’s buried,” that’s where to start digging.

Then, take a page from the IRC and…

- Add alt giving language to your next stewardship and cultivation mailings — for all audiences, not just your major donors.
- Get more specific about your impact. Swap one vague phrase for one concrete number tied to your mission. *Of everything the IRC has tested, concrete, tangible impact language has beaten generic messaging every single time.*
- Pick one upcoming moment — a match deadline, a giving day, a fiscal year-end — and build a short sequence around it: a mailed piece plus two or three emails/texts, all telling the same story and all pointing to an alt giving ask.
- Track alt giving revenue per donor separately, and let the multiplier make your case for more investment in alt giving marketing.

If you want to get more scientific about it, here’s where we’d start testing — fair warning, some of these will be tough to get statistically significant results given typical sample sizes, but they’re worth trying anyway to get directional intel:

- **Planning-season appeals vs. EOY urgency** — test messaging in January/February, tax season, and fiscal-year-start against the classic December scramble.
- **Steps shown vs. steps hidden** — a page that leads with “it takes 5 minutes” and buries the how-to, against one that puts every step up front.
- **Show the other gardeners** — “1,200 of our donors gave through their DAF last year” against no social proof at all.
- **Till before you plant** — send a “did you know you can donate stock to us?” email two weeks ahead of your actual ask, rather than asking cold.

Non-cash giving isn’t a niche. It’s a relationship, built over time — and increasingly, it’s where your most committed donors are already headed. The only question is whether your program is meeting them there.
