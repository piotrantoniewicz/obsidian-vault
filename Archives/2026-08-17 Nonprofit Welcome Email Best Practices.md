A [donor welcome email series](https://www.causevox.com/blog/nonprofit-email-welcome-series/) fixes that. It’s a short string of emails that greets new donors, makes them feel seen, and gently invites them deeper into your work. Here’s how to build one that actually works.

## Why New Donors Slip Away

[Donor retention](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donor-retention-strategies/) is one of the toughest parts of fundraising. Plenty of first-time donors never make a second gift, and it’s rarely because they stopped caring. It’s usually because nobody gave them a reason to stay engaged.

Some reasons donors drift away are out of your hands. People’s finances change. Their priorities shift. Life happens.

But a lot of the reasons are things you can actually do something about:

- No one acknowledged their gift
- No one told them what their gift actually did
- They felt like they were hearing from you the wrong way, or not at all
- They forgot they’d even given

Look at that list again. Every single one of those is a communication problem. And communication problems have a fix.

## How a Welcome Email Series Works

A welcome series is simply a short set of emails that new donors get automatically after their first gift, before they fold into your regular newsletter and appeal rhythm. Think of it as an extended, warm introduction to your organization.

A simple three-email version looks like this:

> **Email 1: The thank-you.** Send this right away, within a day or two of the gift. [Thank them](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donor-recognition-ideas/), and show them what their gift is going to do. A little [storytelling](https://www.causevox.com/blog/storytelling-in-fundraising-campaigns/) goes a long way here.
> 
> **Email 2: The connection.** Send this a couple of weeks later. Give them a look behind the scenes. Share the impact their gift is part of, show some photos, ask a question, or invite them to follow you somewhere else.
> 
> **Email 3: The next ask.** Send this two to three months after the first gift. Donors who are invited to give again within a few months of their first gift are far more likely to do it, and a second gift is one of the best predictors that someone sticks around for the long haul.

Every email in the series should have:

- A subject line worth opening
- Warm, donor-first language (this is about them, not you)
- One clear call to action, not five
- A story that shows real impact

Three emails. Written once. Working every single day after that for every new donor who comes through the door.

This kind of series only works if it actually gets sent, every time, without you remembering to do it. That’s where CauseVox’s automations come in.

No spreadsheet exports, no manually tagging new donors, no sticky note on your monitor.

Here’s what that looks like in practice:

- **Trigger it off the gift itself.** Set the automation to start when a donation is submitted, and add a simple check for whether it’s a donor’s first gift.
- **Space it out with wait steps.** Tell it to wait a day before the first email, a couple of weeks before the second, and a couple of months before the ask. It handles the timing so you don’t have to track it.
- **Let it branch based on what the donor does.** If someone gives again before the series finishes, they drop out of the “ask” email automatically. No one gets a request to give when they just gave.
- **Test it before it goes live.** Run yourself through the whole sequence first so you can see exactly what a new donor will see, in order.

And because every open, click, and gift writes back to the donor’s record automatically, you’ll actually be able to see whether your welcome series is working. If you want to go a step further, a quick thank-you text after that first gift is a nice complement to the email series. A little extra warmth, right when it counts.
