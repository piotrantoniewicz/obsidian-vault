AI is everywhere, and I’ve talked to hundreds of foundation and nonprofit leaders that are anxious to get their teams up and running with the latest tools and skills that are going to help them do their best work. But it’s a minefield out there – between so many different tools, ethical considerations and risks, and quickly changing AI landscapes. The thing I hear most?

**“Where and how do I even begin?” – Stressed Nonprofit Executive**

Weirdly, this got me thinking about parenting. As a mom, I learned quickly that there is more to every developmental milestone—from rolling to crawling, standing to walking—than physical development. While every parent wants their kids to succeed, you realize pretty quickly that you have to help them build the confidence, safety, and skills for independence, step by careful step. My toddler didn’t go from tummy time to sprinting across the yard overnight. And there’s a lot to learn from this skill-building process that could apply to nonprofit AI adoption.

AI adoption isn’t a light switch, it’s a learning journey. And we need to provide teams with the space, structure, and support to move through each stage with skill and intention. That’s where Whole Whale’s Crawl-Walk-Run approach comes in.

## Why Crawl-Walk-Run Works

The crawl-walk-run model is designed to help teams:

- Avoid risk and AI hype by starting with intention
- Scale responsibly in a way that supports your team, brand, and workflows
- Build and measure progress across four key adoption areas—Policy, People, Product, and Performance

Jumping straight to advanced tools without structure? That’s how you end up with AI slop, hallucinated outputs, or brand damage that breaks audience trust. Start where you are. Move with purpose. And keep your mission front and center.

## Trust Is the Currency of Mission-Driven Work

At the heart of every great nonprofit is a powerful relationship with its community. And like all relationships, that bond is built—or broken—through communication.

Your emails, campaigns, website content, and social posts don’t just inform—they build trust. They signal that your organization is credible, competent, and aligned with its values.

But here’s the hard truth: AI can erode that trust fast if used without skill and intention.

- Sloppy, mass-generated AI content can feel hollow or inauthentic.
- Hallucinated facts or AI-written copy without human editing and oversight can damage credibility.
- Unchecked AI use without transparency can raise ethical concerns with supporters, staff, and partners.

If people start to feel that your organization sounds generic, robotic, or unreliable, they’ll tune out. Trust takes time to build, but only seconds to lose. That’s why thoughtful, human-centered nonprofit AI adoption isn’t just a technical challenge, it’s a communications imperative.

## The 4 Key Areas of Nonprofit AI Adoption

To succeed at holistic AI adoption, you should be thinking across four strategic dimensions. Here’s why each one matters:

### POLICY

**How** **are you guiding and protecting AI use across your organization?**

Policy sets the guardrails. Without it, staff may default to free tools that expose your data or create inconsistent outputs. With strong policy, you create a culture of transparency, safety, and trust—internally and externally.

### PEOPLE

**Who** **is driving this work, and how are you building their confidence and capacity?**

AI isn’t a tech problem—it’s a people opportunity. Your staff need training, champions, and clear use cases to meaningfully integrate AI into your existing workflows. Empowering people is the difference between surface-level use and transformative impact.

### PRODUCT

**What tools** **are you using, and how are they connected to your organization’s knowledge, brand voice, and workflows?**

This includes the AI platforms you pay for, the data you feed them, and whether your team is using personal tools (BYOAI) or secure, branded systems. Getting product right means balancing security, utility, and brand voice.

### PERFORMANCE

**What outcomes** **are you looking at to understand the impact AI has on your mission, both internally and externally?**

As AI changes how people find and engage with information, your org’s visibility and credibility are on the line. This category is about understanding AI’s external effects—like how your content shows up in Google’s AI Overviews or ChatGPT or how you’re using AI to scale up your programmatic efforts—and measuring its impact.

## AI Considerations & Pro Tips

AI adoption isn’t just about plugging in new tools. It’s about aligning technology with your values, workflows, and community expectations. Below, we break down across each of the four areas where smart AI decision-making can set your team up for success—and where pitfalls often emerge.

---

### Policy: Set Guardrails Before You Hit “Go”

Without a clear AI policy, staff may start using whatever AI tools they can access—sometimes unknowingly putting your organization’s data, brand, and credibility at risk. A thoughtful AI policy doesn’t just say “yes” or “no”—it helps your team understand **how** to use AI safely, responsibly, and strategically.

Here are some [great examples of Top Nonprofit AI Policies](https://wholewhale.com/resources/top-nonprofit-ai-policies-2025-analysis-and-trends/), including analysis and trends in 2025.

| **Key Questions** | **Pro Tips** |
| --- | --- |
| – What are the risks if staff keep using AI without guardrails?   – What concerns are most pressing, and how do we mitigate those? | ❌ **No BYOAI:** Free, personal tools often train on your data—without your consent.   🤖 **Generic AI = generic outputs:** If you don’t guide the AI, your content will sound like everyone else’s. |

---

### People: AI Is a Team Sport

The real power of AI isn’t in the tech, it’s in your people. Staff need support to experiment safely, develop confidence, and build new skills. You don’t need everyone to be an AI expert, but you do need internal champions, clear expectations, and a shared culture of curiosity and responsibility.

| **Key Questions** | **Pro Tips** |
| --- | --- |
| – Who will champion AI adoption internally?   – What training or support do staff need to feel confident?   – Where can AI have the biggest impact on team capacity? | 🧠 **Train on responsible AI use + prompt engineering** to get better, safer results.   🤝 **“Human-in-the-loop” and “First, not final” mindset:** AI should assist, not replace.   🛠️ **Create custom tools** (like GEMs, GPTs, or prompt templates) tailored to your team’s real needs and use cases. |

---

### Product: Choose the Right Tools for the Job

From Google Workspace with Gemini to CauseWriter.AI, nonprofits have access to powerful tools—some even free through grants or existing workflows. But what matters most is how those tools are implemented. Are they secure? Are they trained on your brand’s voice and content best practices? Are they helping—not hindering—your workflow?

| **Key Questions** | **Pro Tips** |
| --- | --- |
| – What tools are we using, recommending, and why?   – What’s the opportunity cost of *not* investing in shared tools?   – How do we balance privacy, security, and utility?   – What foundation of knowledge do we want our tools to have? | ✅ **Start with Gemini (free for nonprofits)** if you’re using Google Workspace, or Copilot if you’re using Microsoft 365.   🧠 **Train tools on your org’s voice and values** using custom GPTs, Gems, Agents/Copilots, CauseWriter.AI, or other Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) databases.   🔐 **Avoid shadow AI use** —centralize and secure access. |

---

### Performance: Measure What Matters

AI isn’t just about saving time. It can shape how people *find, perceive, and trust* your organization. From SEO to AI Overviews in Google to ChatGPT citations, your **[AI Brand Footprint™](https://wholewhale.com/resources/measuring-your-ai-brand-footprint-the-hidden-visibility-challenge/)** is already forming—whether you’re steering it or not. Don’t just focus on internal efficiency—track external visibility and mission alignment.

| **Key Questions** | **Pro Tips** |
| --- | --- |
| – What does AI mean for our mission outcomes, not just productivity?   – Are we tracking how AI affects our reach and reputation?   – Are we thinking big enough? | 🌐 **Track your [AI Brand Footprint](https://wholewhale.com/resources/measuring-your-ai-brand-footprint-the-hidden-visibility-challenge/) ™**: Where and how does your org show up in generative outputs?   📊 **Audit your content** for AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) readiness.   📅 **Build in evaluation checkpoints** to revisit what’s working, what’s changing, and what’s next. |

## Important Lessons from the Field

I was talking recently with a nonprofit leader whose organization has been walking confidently along their AI journey—and is now starting to run. They’ve moved beyond experimentation into codification with custom-built internal tools, clear guardrails, and capacity training for their teams. But it wasn’t always that way.

When I asked what advice they’d give to orgs in the crawl phase, they had five grounded lessons to share for nonprofits navigating the path ahead:

### 1\. Give staff tools—with rules

What we’ve heard: “If we don’t give our team a secure, shared toolset, they use whatever’s most comfortable or convenient, which is usually whatever’s free. And that’s where the risk starts.”

Staff need permission to explore, but within safe boundaries. Setting up trusted tools shows that you’re invested in their growth and your organization’s integrity.

✅ Pro Tip: Use tools that protect your data (like Gemini for Google Workspace or Causewriter.ai). Pair them with written guardrails and examples of approved use.

### 2\. Leadership buy-in matters

What we’ve heard: “Leadership doesn’t seem to get AI or is hostile to it, so I feel unsure about using it.”  
  
People want to know that leadership is invested in team success. Having executive champions signals to the team that this isn’t just a passing trend. Responsible use starts with visibility and alignment from the top.

✅ Pro Tip: Leaders should model how they use AI, talk openly about wins and challenges, and keep strategy conversations tied to mission—not just tech.

### 3\. Start with use cases, not tools

What we’ve heard: “We wasted time early on looking into all these tools we didn’t need. Once we focused on problems, not platforms, everything clicked.”

It’s easy to get distracted by flashy new tools and AI features. But sustainable AI adoption comes from matching real problems to practical solutions.

✅ Pro Tip: Identify your team’s bottlenecks or repetitive tasks, then explore how AI can lighten the load. One strong use case is better than ten scattered experiments.

### 4\. Be transparent

What we’ve heard: “I wasn’t really sure how to use it. But hearing my manager talk about ways she was using it, and her struggles and successes with it, really helped me think about AI differently.”

AI doesn’t have to be scary, but how you’re using it should be communicated with honesty and clarity. Trust is built when you bring your community along with you, both internally and externally.

✅ Pro Tip: Openly discuss how it is or is not working for you. Create a short internal and external statement about your org’s AI use. Even a few sentences can make your values clear and reduce confusion or skepticism.

### 5\. Know what AI can and can’t do well

What we’ve heard: “AI gets us a first draft faster than ever, but we still edit everything. The human touch is what makes our content unique.”

AI can accelerate your work, but it’s not infallible. Treat it like a co-pilot or thought partner, not a human replacement, and you’ll get better, more reliable outcomes.

✅ Pro Tip: Use a “human-in-the-loop” workflow. Review and edit everything before it goes public. Train your team on how to spot hallucinations or bias in AI outputs.

## Final Thoughts

AI isn’t just a productivity boost, it’s a paradigm shift in communication, trust, and visibility. If you’re not actively shaping your org’s approach to and understanding of AI, you may be falling behind.

Like parenting, the work of team AI adoption takes patience, courage, and trust in the process. Your nonprofit doesn’t need to sprint right away. But it does need to start moving.

Start crawling. Keep walking. Aim to run. And make sure every step supports your mission, your people, and the communities you serve.
