You’re trying to reach more donors.

You send the emails. You post on social media. You ask your board to share. You remind people again.

Then the same core group gives.

That support matters. But your campaign can only go so far if every message comes from your organization alone.

[Peer-to-peer fundraising](https://www.causevox.com/peer-to-peer-fundraising/) helps you change that. Instead of asking your team to reach everyone, you invite supporters to fundraise with you. They create personal fundraising pages, share why your mission matters to them, and bring their own friends, family, coworkers, and networks into the campaign.

A [peer-to-peer campaign](https://www.causevox.com/blog/peer-to-peer-fundraising/) is more than a fundraising page. It is a way to turn supporter trust into community reach.

The best peer-to-peer campaigns do two things well:

- They make it easy for supporters to share their own connection to your mission.
- They make it easy for your team to guide, track, and follow up without extra admin work.

Here’s what to include.

## 1\. Start With A Campaign People Can Explain Quickly

Before you recruit fundraisers, make sure the campaign itself is easy to understand.

Your supporters should be able to explain it in one or two sentences:

What are we raising money for? Why does it matter now? What happens when someone gives?

If the campaign is too vague, fundraisers will struggle to share it. They do not need a complex case for support. They need a [simple story they can make personal.](https://www.causevox.com/blog/storytelling-in-fundraising-campaigns/)

For example:

“We’re raising $50,000 to provide emergency meals for 1,000 families this summer.”

That is clear. It gives fundraisers something specific to invite people into.

Your main campaign page should reinforce that same story with a clear goal, strong visuals, simple donation options, and an obvious call to give or start fundraising.

## 2\. Recruit The Right First Fundraisers

Peer-to-peer fundraising works best when you start with people who already care.

That usually means your most engaged supporters:

- Board members
- Volunteers
- Longtime donors
- Past event participants
- Program alumni
- Community ambassadors
- Staff or committee members
- Supporters who regularly open, click, or share your emails

You do not need hundreds of fundraisers to get started. A [small group of committed supporters](https://www.causevox.com/blog/recruit-peer-to-peer-fundraisers-giving-day/) can outperform a large group of people who never personalize or share their pages.

Start with people who have a real reason to say yes.

When you invite them, make the ask personal. Tell them why you thought of them, what the campaign will make possible, and what you are asking them to do.

Instead of:

“Will you create a fundraising page?”

Try:

“You have been such a consistent advocate for this work. Would you be willing to create a personal fundraising page and invite your network to help us reach 100 students this month?”

That kind of ask feels less like a task and more like an invitation to lead.

## 3\. Make Page Setup Simple

Most supporters are willing to help, but they are not looking for another complicated platform to learn.

If [creating a fundraising page](https://www.causevox.com/blog/create-a-free-fundraising-site/) feels confusing, many people will say yes and then never finish setting it up.

Your job is to make the first step feel easy.

A strong fundraiser setup flow should help supporters:

- Create a page quickly
- Add a personal photo
- Write a short “why I’m fundraising” message
- Set a personal goal
- Share the page with their network

## 4\. Give Fundraisers A Simple Sharing Toolkit

Most fundraisers are not professional fundraisers.

They may care deeply about your mission, but they still need help knowing what to say.

A simple toolkit can make a big difference.

Include:

- A short campaign summary
- 2 to 3 sample emails
- 3 to 5 social post examples
- A few images or graphics
- Suggested text message copy
- A reminder to personalize their message
- A simple timeline for when to share

The goal is not to script every word. The goal is to reduce the blank-page problem.

The best fundraiser messages usually sound personal. Encourage supporters to explain why they care, who they are honoring, what the mission means to them, or why they chose to take part.

You can provide the structure. They provide the relationship.

## 5\. Keep Momentum Visible

Peer-to-peer campaigns [need energy](https://www.causevox.com/blog/7-ideas-to-motivate-peer-to-peer-fundraisers/).

If fundraisers feel like no one is paying attention, they are less likely to keep sharing. If they see progress, encouragement, and friendly activity, they are more likely to stay involved.

Momentum can come from [simple signals](https://www.causevox.com/blog/top-9-fundraising-tools-for-your-campaign/):

- Campaign progress bars
- Personal fundraising goals
- Team totals
- Leaderboards
- Milestone updates
- Shoutouts for fundraisers
- [Matching gift announcements](https://www.causevox.com/blog/maximizing-automated-matching/)
- Countdown reminders

You do not need to overwhelm people. You just need to make progress visible enough that fundraisers feel part of something active.

For example, instead of only sending a reminder that says “Don’t forget to share your page,” try:

“We are 62% of the way to our goal, and 18 fundraisers have already received their first gift. If you have not shared your page yet, today is a great day to start.”

That gives people context, encouragement, and a clear next step.

## 6\. Follow Up While The Campaign Is Still Active

A common peer-to-peer mistake is recruiting fundraisers at the start, then going quiet until the end.

Supporters need encouragement throughout the campaign.

Send short updates. Celebrate progress. Thank people quickly. Check in with fundraisers who created a page but have not shared it yet. Congratulate fundraisers who receive their first gift. Give your top fundraisers language they can use for one more push.

Your follow-up does not need to be complicated. It just needs to be timely.

A good campaign rhythm might include:

- A launch-day email to fundraisers
- A first-week encouragement email
- A midpoint progress update
- A final-week push
- A last-day reminder
- A post-campaign thank-you

## 7\. Connect Every Gift To The Next Relationship

Peer-to-peer fundraising can introduce your organization to many new donors.

That is the opportunity.

It is also where many nonprofits lose momentum.

A new donor gives to a friend’s fundraising page. They receive a receipt. Then they never hear from the organization again, or they get added to a generic list with no context.

That misses the bigger point.

A peer-to-peer gift is not just a transaction. It is a [relationship entry point](https://www.causevox.com/blog/strengthen-donor-relationships/).

Your team should know:

- Which fundraiser brought in the donor
- Which campaign they gave to
- Whether they are new or returning
- What message they responded to
- Whether they gave once or became recurring
- What follow-up they should receive next

This is where [connected fundraising](https://www.causevox.com/blog/connected-fundraising/) matters.

In CauseVox, peer-to-peer donations connect back to your campaign, fundraiser activity, donor records, receipts, and reporting. That helps your team thank donors, recognize fundraisers, and understand the full story behind the campaign without piecing it together afterward.

The result is better stewardship, not just cleaner data.

## 8\. Celebrate The People Who Made It Happen

When your campaign ends, do not let the momentum fade away.

Your fundraisers took a social risk by asking their networks to give. Your donors responded because someone they trusted invited them in. Both groups should feel the impact of what they helped make possible.

Share the final results. Thank fundraisers by name when appropriate. Highlight teams, milestones, creative outreach, and mission outcomes. Send a wrap-up message that shows what the campaign made possible.

A strong close does more than celebrate the total raised. It makes people feel proud to have participated.

That matters for your next campaign.

People are more likely to fundraise again when they feel [appreciated, informed, and connected to the impact.](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donor-recognition-ideas/)

## Common Peer-To-Peer Fundraising Campaign Mistakes To Avoid

Even strong nonprofit teams can lose momentum when peer-to-peer campaigns are hard to explain, hard to share, or disconnected from follow-up.

Watch out for:

- Recruiting fundraisers without giving them a clear campaign story
- Making page setup too complicated
- Sending supporters off without sample messages or tools
- Waiting until the end of the campaign to check in
- Tracking fundraiser and donor data in separate systems
- Treating new peer-to-peer donors like generic list contacts

These problems usually are not caused by a lack of effort. They happen when your campaign, donor data, fundraiser activity, and follow-up are not working together.
