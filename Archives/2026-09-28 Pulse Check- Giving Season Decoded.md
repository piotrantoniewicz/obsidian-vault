Giving Season Pulse Check

## Exploring the data trends that define the Giving Season

THE OPERATING CALENDAR

### The final six days

Giving Tuesday is the first peak of the season. The final six days bring in the most generosity.

![December calendar](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/912x600/33ce8cb5f6/potd-q3-final-six-days-calendar.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Online fundraising data from the last five giving seasons shows that the final six days of the calendar year — December 26th through 31st — bring in 38% of the season's total revenue. That's more than a third of everything a median organization raises in its entire 43-day giving season.

But one day stands on its own: December 31st accounts for 16% of season revenue. Compared to the average off-season day, New Year’s Eve brings in 18.4x the revenue per visitor.

And if we go back to December 26th, we see giving per day gradually increasing, with a peak landing on December 30th at 8% of season revenue.

Compare all of that to Giving Tuesday, which draws roughly 10% of season revenue, running at 9x an off-season day average revenue per visitor, but a fraction of what the final week of the year produces.

The acquisition window between Giving Tuesday and Christmas — December 9th through 21st — brings in 35% of the season's revenue on its own, nearly matching the final six days in total share even though it spans twice as many days.

### Why it matters

This data shows that while Giving Tuesday plays an important role in launching your year-end campaign, the highest moments of donor intent will come in two key windows with two additional peak days of giving on the final days of the year.

Giving Tuesday kickstarts your fundraising momentum, and the best laid plans this season will include engaging donors all the way until the ball drops on New Years Eve.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/0270bbb8d8/potd-q3-giving-tuesday-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

The final six days reward preparation. By prioritizing support and monitoring through December 31st, not just through Giving Tuesday, you ensure your attention doesn’t drift while donor intent peaks.

- **Keep donor communications running** into the final week instead of tapering them off after Christmas: keep sending, keep asking, keep showing up in inboxes and feeds all the way to New Year's Eve.
- **Name the December 31st deadline** directly in your messaging — donors already know the tax year is closing, so let that real deadline create urgency instead of manufacturing a fake one.
- **Prioritize engagement with donors who gave last giving season.** Statistically, this segment is likely to give again this year. We’ll explore this data trend further in the report.
- **Track your giving season progress** based on the donor intent peaks to understand how you’re trending towards your goals.

Ask yourself: how easy is it for donors to give in the final 24 hours of the year?

- **Turn on native wallet payments** like Apple Pay and Google Pay
- **Reduce required fields in checkout** recognizing that a majority of donors who give are already in your database

PAYMENT METHODS

### The bank transfer gap

Bank transfers bring in 5x more revenue than credit cards. But many organizations don't accept ACH transfers.

![Gold coins](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/912x600/d5a6e789be/potd-q3-bank-transfer-coins.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Donors who give by ACH — direct bank transfer — give an average of $1,524 per gift. Donors who give by credit card give an average of $276. That's more than a 5x gap in a single payment method choice.

ACH's share of giving season revenue has nearly tripled over five years, growing from 2.4% in 2021 to 7.3% in 2025. Donors are moving toward it steadily. Even so, 39% of nonprofits do not currently accept ACH payments.

### Why it matters

Creating a better donor experience is about giving donors more flexibility in how they choose to give. By enabling ACH transfers in the online giving experience, a nonprofit provides a pathway for giving aligned to donor preference and generosity has the chance to increase.

Make the most of your donors' intentions to give by meeting them where they are and providing them with a simple, frictionless giving experience that allows them to use whatever payment method they prefer.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/0e0e3915e5/potd-q3-wallets-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

Enable and test flexible payment options today in the online donation experience. Use Giving Tuesday as an opportunity to learn more about the ways donors are interacting with your form, and make adjustments for the remainder of the year.

Payment options beyond credit cards that can help increase giving include:

- ACH/bank transfer checkout
- DAF (donor-advised fund) giving options like DAFpay
- Native wallet payment methods like Apple Pay and Google Pay
- Other digital payment methods including Venmo, PayPal, and more

CHANNELS

### Organic and search dominate Giving Season

The donors who already know about you are fueling your mission during the year-end.

![Person forming a heart](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/912x600/7257972cb1/potd-q3-channels.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Search and organic traffic together account for 78% of giving season revenue — the clear majority of how donors find their way to a donation form during the season.

What does this tell us?

The donors arriving to give to you at year-end either:

- Already know about your organization and intend to give
- Have been identified by your organization and targeted to donate

In addition, email and SMS drive 9% of revenue and earn the strongest repeat-donor rate of any channel at 23%. Paid ads bring in 4.5% while organic social sits at 1%. While organic social is low in terms of conversion rate, it shouldn’t be discounted as a strong channel for awareness and engagement as part of a strategy to stay top of mind to your audience during the high-intent season.

With the advent of AI, new channels are opening and the future of donor experience is shifting. Donors are beginning to discover nonprofits through AI assisted search. While only a small segment of new donors are acquired through AI tools like Claude or ChatGPT, it's wise to leverage SEO, AEO, website optimization, and content strategy to make it easier for new donors to find you with AI agents.

### Five donor segments; five behaviors

One segment pays this year's bills. The other pays next year's.

![Open hand, five fingers](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/912x600/11cb54ef31/potd-q3-segments-hand.png/m/1540x1392/filters:format(avif):quality(60))

### What the data shows

Five years of data show a structural shift underneath giving season:

- New-donor share of revenue fell from 54% to 42%
- Returning-donor share climbed from 46% to 58%

Organizations are increasingly seeing more gifts from the donors they already have. Within that shift, five donor segments behave in five distinct ways:

- **Returning donors who gave last giving season** are the strongest-performing segment in the dataset: 31% of all giving season revenue, the highest nine-month realized value of any segment at $478, and the segment most concentrated into the season's final stretch — 41% of this segment's revenue lands in the December 26th–31st window, compared to 25–30% for other segments. These donors are the least likely of any segment to start a recurring plan during the giving season (just 1%) and respond disproportionately well to paid ads.
- **First-time donors** account for 40% of giving season revenue and are the segment most likely to start a recurring plan during the season (5%). They're statistically acquired in the middle of the season — between Giving Tuesday and Christmas — and the data suggests this segment will become next year's high-value returning-donor base, echoing the pattern the "gave last giving season" segment above shows today.
- **Returning donors who gave** ***since*** **last giving season** make up 10% of revenue. This segment engages early — often right at Giving Tuesday — and 40% of them give again within six months, the strongest near-term repeat rate in the dataset. Email is the best-performing channel for reaching them.
- **Returning but lapsed (365+ days)** makes up 9% of revenue and is the only segment growing its share of revenue year over year. These donors respond best to a combination of paid ads and email.

*An important note about the data:* shares may not add up to 100% because each number is a median per organization.

### Why it matters

The returning and first-time donor bases are doing two different jobs. The "gave last giving season" segment is this year’s high-impact group — the one carrying the giving season over the finish line. First-timers are the investment in next year's version of that same engine.

Treating both segments identically by producing the same messaging, the same channel mix, and the same timing under-serves the high-value returning donor who's ready to give right now, and under-develops the first-time donor who could become your high-value donor next year.

*An important note about acquisition:* The data we used to build this report shows us that while the giving season sees a majority of repeat gifts, it is also an opportunity to acquire new donors. The season's structure and timing favor the organizations already built around returning-donor relationships.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/7d12c7facb/potd-q3-cancellations-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- Treat donors who gave last giving season and donors acquired after January 1st as two separate segments with different messaging.
- Segment out donors who give during the giving season from additional asks.
- Turn first-time gifts into long-term relationships. Build stewardship sequences now for first-time donors acquired during the giving season and deploy them in January and February.

New recurring plans stay a small slice of Giving Season donations

### Why it matters

The takeaway isn't "push recurring harder." The data doesn't support that as a giving season strategy. Rather, focus efforts towards the season’s proven offer: one-time gifts. Let one-time giving do the job it already does well, while using the season's traffic to plant the seeds of next year's recurring base, particularly among newly acquired donors.

It’s important to remember to continue nurturing existing relationships through the giving season, in particular those that gave throughout the year (off-season), who are the most likely to give another gift.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/994x952/870ab48883/potd-q3-upsell-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

- Segment with intention. Existing donors in your database are more likely to give during the giving season. Tailor messaging to the segments most engaged to give.
- Use AI-suggested gift amounts to make the right ask for one-time gifts for donors who are motivated to give.
- Continue to nurture donors through December in addition to gift asks and campaigns.
- In September and October, A/B test post-donation upsells that could help capture additional revenue during a season of high-motivation and intent.

### What the data shows

Mobile's share of gifts rose every year of the five-year study, and it rose fastest for the donors an organization knows least. Among first-time donors, mobile giving climbed from 35% of gifts in 2021 to 45% in 2025. Among returning donors, the climb is minimal — 27% to 30% over the same five years. The gap between the two groups didn't shrink; it grew.

In our last Pulse Check, [The Story of Mobile Giving Isn’t About Mobile,](https://fundraiseup.com/reports/pulse-check-2026-q1/) there data showed a rise in mobile giving above 50% of all gifts for the first time across the U.S., Australia, and U.K. While mobile giving has taken its majority share in the off-season, donors who give during year-end are making online donations on desktop over mobile.

In fact, peak giving days such as Giving Tuesday saw a decline in percent of mobile gifts down to 23% in 2025.

### Why it matters

A returning donor already trusts the organization enough to push through a clunky form — they've done it before, they know what's coming, and they're motivated to finish. A first-time donor may not have the same patience for friction: a slow load, a payment field that doesn't fit the screen, or a multi-step form that wasn't built for a thumb is enough reason to abandon before ever becoming a donor.

That means the segment growing fastest on mobile — first time donors — is also the segment with the least tolerance for a mobile experience that doesn't work. A donation form that's merely functional on desktop and only technically functional on mobile is quietly taxing exactly the donors who are the most expensive to acquire: the ones who haven't given yet.

### What to do

- **Test the donation form on a phone** before testing anything else. Walk through it end to end on a real device, not a resized browser window — check that payment fields don't require zooming, that the amount selector doesn't need three taps to find, and that the whole flow completes in the time it takes to wait for a crosswalk signal to change.
- **Build the Giving Tuesday–Christmas stretch for mobile.** That's when first-time donors arrive, and they're the most likely to be on mobile.
- **Prepare the final six days for desktop gifts.** Donors who gave last season carry the last week, and they're mostly on a laptop. Show higher amounts on desktop and keep bank transfers visible.
- **Set amounts by device, not one default for both.** A mobile donor in early December and a desktop donor on Dec 31 are making different gifts. Use suggested gift amounts to optimize for each device type.
- **Pull your own device split by day.** Compare your mobile share on Giving Tuesday, Dec 9–21 and Dec 31 with your off-season baseline. If your peak days look the same as an ordinary Tuesday, your checkout is probably built for only one of these donors.

### Why it matters

The instinct in fundraising is to ask again. A follow-up appeal after the first gift lands, a nudge before the calendar turns, a second campaign wave mid-season. This data says that instinct doesn't pay off at scale, for any segment, in any year measured.

Securing a second in-season gift likely won’t happen with another better appeal. It's closer to a fact about how the season works: the base rate for a second gift barely exists inside the 43-day window.

When a donor gives again they do so as next year's returning donor, not with a repeat gift this season.

![](https://a.storyblok.com/f/285823045102956/952x994/1688540409/potd-q3-gift-amounts-small.png/m/994x952/filters:format(avif):quality(85))

### What to do

Stop budgeting time, dollars, and calendar space for a second in-season ask to the same donor — it isn't converting, and chasing it pulls resources from what actually works.

Redirect that effort here instead:

- Optimize the giving experience to ensure their one gift is as large as it can be
- Re-engage giving season donors to return the following year
- Build your in-season donor communications around a single, well-timed ask per donor rather than a drip of repeat appeals — and shift any "convert them again" energy into the reactivation push that happens after the season closes, where the data says the real second gift actually lands.

### A guide on how to prepare for this Giving Season

###### Pre-Giving Tuesday

Run your QA and peak-load checks now while there's still time to make fixes on anything that breaks. Confirm tracking is in place across every channel you'll report on later. Enable and test ACH/bank transfer checkout — this is a high impact payment method that nearly half of nonprofits don’t offer. Begin warming up existing audiences, especially prior-season donors, ahead of the Giving Season push.

###### Giving Tuesday

Treat December 1 as a genuine peak day — it draws roughly 10% of season revenue at eight times an average day's revenue-per-visitor. Maximize form reliability and campaign support today and keep your team engaged through the rest of December.

###### December 9–21

This is the season's broadest acquisition window, bringing in 35% of total revenue. Run the widest acquisition push of the season here, targeting first-time donors and warm returning donors specifically. This is also the best window to acquire the first-time donors who could become next year's high-value returning donors.

###### December 22–29

Scale your investments where results are already validated rather than testing new channels this late. Begin the season's main reactivation push toward prior-season and lapsed donors — momentum is already building here, even through a quiet Christmas Day, ahead of the surge still to come.

###### December 30

Treat this as a standalone peak in its own right. At 8% of season revenue, it performs like a true peak day, a full day before the actual peak arrives.

###### December 31

This is the season's highest-impact day, drawing 16% of total revenue at nearly 18 times an average day's rate. Prioritize prior-season and lapsed donors specifically — they're the segments most likely to show up today — and make sure ACH is visible and working, since the day's largest, most spontaneous gifts are the ones most likely to move through it.

## Conclusion

Giving Season is not a December strategy. It's the result of the work you do all year.

By the time December arrives, donor intent has already been shaped by months of experience with your organization. The strength of your donor base, the visibility of your organization, the quality of the giving experience, and your ability to convert intent at the right moment all build over time.

For leadership teams, the takeaway from Pulse Check is that the Giving Season has to be managed as more than a December campaign. It’s about leveraging the relationships, experiences and visibility you’ve built all year — and converting that into generosity when donor intent is highest.

This year’s first-time donor may be your highest-value returning donor next year. And that's the longer-term opportunity.

By recognizing these opportunities and orchestrating around them, you will reach the right donors, with the right ask, through the right channels.

Year-end is a peak moment in time where the entire donor experience comes due.

Make this giving season count.

## Methodology

###### Data Sources

This report is built from a five-year study (2021–2025) of Giving Season behavior among U.S. nonprofit organizations. Every Giving Season in the dataset is defined as the 43-day window ending December 31st — a window that accounts for Thanksgiving's floating date each year, rather than a fixed calendar range.

###### Calculation Methodology

Every figure in this report is an aggregated median value calculated per organization. A "16% of revenue" statistic describes what a typical organization in the cohort experiences, not a sum across all organizations combined. Cohort sizes grew across the study period: 121 organizations in 2021, 207 in 2022, 319 in 2023, 461 in 2024, and 652 in 2025.

###### Analysis Qualifications

To qualify for the cohort, an organization had to meet four tests: consistency (at least six months with five or more donations over the trailing fourteen months), scale (500 or more donations over that same period), tenure (at least one month with a donation between April and September of the prior year), and recency (at least one month with a donation since January 1st of the season year).

###### Geographic Coverage

This is a U.S.-only study. The cohort contains no UK, Canadian, or Australian organizations, and none of the figures in this report should be read as applicable outside the U.S. market.
