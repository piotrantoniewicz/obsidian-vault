Ahhh National Harbor in July. A time when hundreds of the nonprofit industry’s finest mix and mingle in the ~~heat and humidity~~ unusually breezy weather on the Potomac! Now that we’re back and caught up on email and Slack after a week at Bridge, I wanted to share some takeaways from our conference contingent. Some stuff was really exciting and interesting! Some stuff was kind of a dud! Here’s what we’ve got:

### First up, we had a lot to say

M+R teamed up with some of the smartest people we know to present fun, informative sessions.

- ***AI and organic search:*** [The rise of AI is transforming search, which has a huge impact on how our audiences find out about us.](https://www.mrss.com/lab/what-googling-will-mean-in-2027/) **Our own Diego Ruiz and Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center’s Asia Coladner and Hannah Belisle presented about how they’ve adapted website, earned and social media and paid search to the new realities of AI search with an Answer Engine Optimization strategy.**
- ***Holistic measurement:***Everyone knows that ads have a halo effect on other channels – but how do you quantify that effect for leadership and hold your program accountable? **Sarah Coughlon from M+R and Rosa Del Angel from NRDC gave a practical clinic on multi-touch attribution and marketing mix modeling (MMM), with insights on how these methods have enabled NRDC to prove impact and spur long-term growth.**
- ***Alternative giving is hot right now:*** Did you know that Donor-Advised Fund (DAF) revenue was up [44% YoY in our Benchmarks Study](https://mrbenchmarks.com/fundraising/)? Nonprofits are responding by building long-term value (LTV) (optimizing to acquire more monthly donors, year-round engagement for DAF donors and prospects) and investing in brand awareness (including mid-funnel tactics like running brand display ads to direct mail acquisition audiences). **Our very own Gwen McGarry shared research we conducted on alternative giving methods, with actionable takeaways for your program.**

If you’re feeling FOMO about these sessions, don’t! We’re planning to share a lot of this same information in upcoming webinars on MMM and AEO, plus a forthcoming post on six tests you should run on your DAF giving page. As the kids say: watch this space 👀

And if you just can’t wait for more… you can reach our AEO and MMM teams at [*aeo@mrss.com*](mailto:aeo@mrss.com) and [*mmm@mrss.com*](mailto:mmm@mrss.com)!

### Oh my, AI

Not surprisingly, AI was the hot topic this year for sure! BridgeTECH was overwhelmingly AI-focused, and even more sessions later in the week examined generative and predictive AI applications in nonprofit spaces.

This one won’t be surprising to many of you: **for the individuals and organizations leading on AI tools adoption, it’s freeing up huge gobs of time on specifically admin tasks** — notetaking, updating your project-management tools, summarizing complex conversations. In terms of individual productivity, I came away with plenty of ideas on how to work smarter with AI.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/image1.png)

*Pictured: me post-Bridge blowing up the DMs of our in-house Claude gurus*

**On a broader strategic level, we’re also seeing a lot of interest in predictive AI, which is quickly becoming a powerful tool for building audience segments and customizing creative.** Our own Gwen McGarry [wrote about this recently](https://www.mrss.com/lab/creative-is-becoming-the-new-performance-driver-heres-what-you-need-to-know-and-what-to-do-next/): AI-powered targeting is helping organizations shift from a *channel* mindset to an audience-meets creative mindset, and that shift is driving big performance gains.

Last thing on AI: the human element.  
  
I was particularly struck during a session when designer Johnathan Gibson from Americans United for Separation of Church & State said: **“AI is only backward-looking, it can only remix what we have fed into it. Only humans can innovate.”** That was a really helpful reminder of why we’re all here (and why these type of in-person connections matter for fostering creativity and innovation. More about that below!).

### Hot tip for presenters: mess with your session format

If you’ve been to enough conferences, you know the format of your median session: two or three people presenting slides (of varying quality…) for about 45 minutes, followed by 15 minutes of questions. And no shade, we do this too! It’s a classic for a reason.

But across three days of conference and preconference, it can all start to blur 😵💫

**Some of our favorite sessions this year broke that format!** Cara Holland from the International Rescue Committee joined Peter Byrnes from FundraiseUp for a split-format session on agentic giving: the first half was a fireside chat, followed by a more technical presentation on how nonprofits can prepare for AI-driven donation flows. Meanwhile, Stratovation Partners brought together a crew at BridgeTech for an Oxford-style debate on AI usage in our sector.

Both sessions gave us a lot to noodle on — both as practitioners and presenters. Hold us accountable: a year from now, expect to see some new session formats in our 2027 conference circuit.

### Bridge is about people — prioritize those connections

The staff at the Illy Cafe down the street and the Potomac District Cafe in the Gaylord really are the unsung heroes of Bridge, huh? Going into the conference, we were excited for catching up with old friends, former coworkers, and current strategic partners – and those conversations truly were the best part of our week in National Harbor.

For a company where most of us work remotely, it was a great reminder that we’re not doing this work in silos. We are connected to other humans — not just through our screens — and those connections keep us going even when the work gets tough.
