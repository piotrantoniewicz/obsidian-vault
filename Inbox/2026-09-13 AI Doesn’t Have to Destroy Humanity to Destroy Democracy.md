Some of the people who helped build artificial intelligence have warned that it could threaten humanity’s survival. Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio and Dario Amodei were among those who signed a statement calling for the risk of extinction from AI to be treated as a global priority alongside pandemics and nuclear war. I take those warnings seriously. ([Safe AI](https://safe.ai/work/press-release-ai-risk))

###### We should take seriously the possibility that AI could threaten humanity. We should be just as serious about defending democracy while we still have the opportunity. (Yes, AI created this image)

**But I believe the biggest immediate threat from AI is what it can do to democracy - especially by making disinformation easier to manufacture, distribute and mistake for truth.** We do not have to choose between addressing catastrophic risks and confronting the danger already in front of us.

This is not hypothetical. Before New Hampshire’s 2024 presidential primary, an AI-generated imitation of Joe Biden’s voice was used in robocalls urging people not to vote. No superintelligence required. Just people using available technology to deceive voters. ([AP News](https://apnews.com/article/f3469ceb6dd613079092287994663db5?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**AI search compounds the problem.** A 2025 study led by the BBC and coordinated by the European Broadcasting Union found significant problems in **45% of the AI-generated news answers tested**, including problems with accuracy and sourcing. Those errors are not all deliberate disinformation. But they demonstrate why a confident answer is not necessarily a reliable one. ([EBU](https://www.ebu.ch/news/2025/10/ai-s-systemic-distortion-of-news-is-consistent-across-languages-and-territories-international-study-by-public-service-broadcaste))

As [Tim Chambers](https://open.substack.com/users/627266-tim-chambers?utm_source=mentions) and I argued in *[You Can’t Debunk What You Never See](https://willrobinson.substack.com/p/you-cant-debunk-what-you-never-see)*, false claims can reach people before campaigns or fact-checkers even know they exist. AI tools can then give those claims a second life, repeating them as apparently authoritative answers. **Chasing the last lie cannot be our only strategy.** ([Will Robinson’s Newsletter](https://willrobinson.substack.com/p/you-cant-debunk-what-you-never-see))

The new research review, *[Countering Disinformation: Strategies for 2026 and Beyond](https://www.psgconsulting.com/research-publications/countering-disinformation)*, offers a practical response: **prebunking—helping people recognize manipulation before they encounter it.** Commissioned by Innovating for the Public Good and written by the Dewey Square Group team, the report emphasizes sustained efforts and repeated reinforcement, not one video and crossed fingers. ([PSG Consulting](https://www.psgconsulting.com/research-publications/countering-disinformation))

We need to put that work into practice now. Give it a budget. Make somebody responsible. Train trusted messengers. Combine monitoring and fact-checking with ongoing public education. Test what works. Keep reinforcing it. And stop building programs that disappear the morning after the election.
