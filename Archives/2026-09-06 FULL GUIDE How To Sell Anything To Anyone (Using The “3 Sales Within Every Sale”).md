## The 3 Sales Within Every Sale

To introduce this framework, we must answer two questions:

1. What do people buy?
2. Why do people buy?

Let’s start with inversion: what do people not buy?

They do not buy:

- You
- Your story
- Your offer
- Your product
- Your company
- Your testimonials
- Your guarantee

**People buy vehicles for change.**

Put simply, every purchase is a vehicle that takes someone from where they are to where they want to be.

It looks like this:

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!woUc!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9d61bdcb-9edc-4751-85a4-7b21f2f1aeb0_755x453.png)

They want something - this is their desired situation. The path they are currently on is not leading them there. Instead, it is leading them to a fearful future situation. To avoid that, they buy the new path.

Simple.

For example, let’s look at someone with joint pain.

- **(Current situation)** They are currently in pain. No fun.
- **(Fearful future)** They are not doing anything to fix their pain, so they will be in more pain in the future. Also no fun.
- **(Desired situation)** They want to be out of pain, but their current path is not leading there.
- **(Vehicle for change)** They buy some Advil. It removes their pain, so they end up in their desired situation.

Now - on the surface this looks like a relatively simple sale. However, there are actually 3 elements within this sale that a prospect must go through to make a purchasing decision.

Those 3 sales are:

1. **The sale on making a change**
2. **The sale on a certain method being the solution**
3. **The sale on the specific brand to deliver that method**

Applying this framework to someone in pain:

1. **The sale on making a change.** Before they purchase anything, they need to want to get out of pain. This sounds obvious, but it must happen before anything else. They must understand they are in pain, which they might be unaware of or ignoring. They must understand that it’s possible to build a better life with no pain (creating a desired situation). They must understand that if they don’t make a change, they will end up in more pain over time, which sucks (creating a fearful future situation). Once they understand 1) they are in pain and 2) there is a world where they can get out of pain, they will start to explore solutions.
2. **The sale on a certain method being the solution.** Once they’re exploring solutions, they will find many options. They could start cold plunging. They could get a massage. They could get surgery. They could take Ibuprofen. They could take Acetaminophen. They could do dry needling. There are TONS of potential solutions out there that they could explore. So the second sale is on this particular solution being the one that is best for them. Best for them will be relative to their situation & preferences, so the job of a sales process is to help elevate this solution over the others. We will talk below about how to do that.
3. **The sale on a specific brand to deliver that solution.** Once they’re bought in on Ibuprofen (the generic anti-inflammatory drug) being the best solution to get out of pain, they will research the different *brands*. So the final sale is on Advil (the brand) being the best option to deliver the solution. And again, we will talk through different ways to make this third sale down below.

Or applying it to someone making a much bigger purchase: hiring a personal trainer:

1. **The sale on making a change.** They must believe they need to get in better shape.
2. **The sale on a certain method being the solution.** They must believe that lifting weights with a personal trainer is the best way to get there, rather than trying it on their own or doing some other health fad.
3. **The sale on the specific brand to deliver that method.** They must believe this specific personal trainer is the one they should work with.

Importantly, these sales MUST HAPPEN IN ORDER. If you try to sell the specific solution to someone who doesn’t even know they’re in pain, it’s not going to work. If you try to sell your brand before someone is bought in on that solution, it’s not going to work.

Now, this does not mean that *you* have to be the one to make each of these sale. Some people will enter your world very ready to make a change and fully bought in on your method. Your job is to meet each potential customer where they’re at and give them the right amount of information they need to progress through the next sale. Sometimes that means starting at the very beginning. Other times it means jumping to the very end.

So when selling 1:1, you need to diagnose where someone is currently at, then sell them on the next belief.

When you’re selling 1 to many, you will need to understand where the majority of your audience is entering your world and structure your marketing accordingly.

If you are marketing to a cold audience who has never considered making this change, you will need to spend significant time on Sale #1 before moving forward.

If you are marketing to a problem-aware audience already searching for solutions, you can spend less time on Sale #1 and more time making Sale #2.

And if you are marketing to people who already believe in your method, then most of your work will happen in Sale #3.

The colder and less aware the audience, the earlier in the sequence you need to start.

The warmer and more sophisticated the audience, the further into the sequence you can begin.

If in doubt, it’s typically more effective to err on the side of emphasizing an earlier sale, because it never hurts to remind someone of the importance of making a change.

So summarizing each of these 3 sales:

- **Sale #1: The sale on making a change**
	- **Question:** “Do I need to do something about this?”
		- **Belief:** My current path is not good enough, and making a change is worth it.
- **Sale #2: The sale on a certain method being the solution**
	- **Question:** “What is the best way to make that change?”
		- **Belief:** This particular method is the best vehicle for getting where I want to go.
- **Sale #3: The sale on the specific brand to deliver that method**
	- **Question:** “Who should I trust to help me implement that method?”
		- **Belief:** This specific product, service, or company is the best option for delivering that method

Everything we’re about to cover simply breaks these 3 sales down into the smaller necessary beliefs someone must hold before they confidently move to the next one.

So now let’s talk about how to engineer each of these.

## Sale #1: The sale on making a change

To get someone to want to make a change, we must get extremely clear & specific on 3 “situations.”

1. Their current situation
2. Their desired situation
3. Their future situation, if nothing changes

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!q8Xn!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4b010686-a534-4b4a-a483-a7e06ca62c15_656x376.png)

For each of these situations, we want to clearly define:

1. Their surface level state (what is happening)
2. Their emotional state (how they feel)

Then, to engineer a change, we want to:

1. Paint a vivid & clear picture of the pain in their current situation **(Pain)**
2. Paint a vivid & clear picture of their desired situation **(Desire)**
3. Paint a vivid and clear picture of where they will be in a certain amount of time if they don’t change **(Cost)**

When painting this picture, we want to get a SPECIFIC and TANGIBLE as possible.

Pain & Desire happens in moments. Specific moments. Visceral moments.

Depending on the type of funnel you are running, you will either need to describe this *for them* (like if you’re writing emails or driving traffic to a landing page) or have *them describe it* (if you are selling via the phone).

For an example of what this looks like when you get someone to describe their current situation:

- **V1:** “My job sucks.”
- **V2:** “I feel like I’m wasting my potential. I’m not growing, I’m not paid what I’m worth, and I dread logging in every day.”
- **V3:** “Every week I pour in effort and get nothing back. No recognition, no progress, just more busywork. I’m constantly overlooked while people who care less than me keep moving up.”
- **V4:** “Last month I hit my targets, took on extra projects, and stayed late almost every night, only to watch my manager hand the promotion to someone who does half the work and plays politics. That was the moment I realized no matter how much I give here, I’ll never get what I deserve.”

This is the same person describing their situation. V1 is very surface level and does not elicit any emotions of change. V4 is so visceral and tangible you can feel the exhaustion and desperation for change from every word.

And this is what it looks like to get specific on a desired situation:

- **V1:** “I want to make a bit more money and work remotely while traveling.”
- **V2:** “I want enough income to not stress about bills, while having the flexibility to work from anywhere instead of being chained to an office.”
- **V3:** “I want to be in control of my schedule—earning multiple five figures a month, choosing projects I actually enjoy, and being able to spend weeks abroad without asking for permission.”
- **V4:** “I want to hit $10K+ a month consistently, running my ghostwriting business entirely from my laptop. I want to wake up in Barcelona, spend the morning writing for two clients I actually respect, then close the laptop by 2 PM and explore the city with my partner. I want the freedom to pick projects that excite me, skip the ones that drain me, and know my income is predictable, enough to cover rent, travel, savings, and still have margin. I want the confidence that I never need to ask a boss for vacation days again, and that I can design my weeks around what matters most to me.”

Can you see how someone having clarity over V4 is *significantly* more likely to make the necessary change to get there, compared to V1?

That clarity is what your sales process needs to engineer.

### 12 Ways To Get More Specific With Your Sales Messaging

Depending on what you are selling, the elements you want to paint vividly will differ.

1. Income → specific number per month
2. Location Freedom → specific places they would go
3. Time Freedom → specific things they would do on a random Tuesday
4. Dislike for job → specific moments they dislike the most
5. Stress → specific moments they experience stress
6. Pain → specific moments they experience pain
7. Experience Freedom → specific things they want to be able to book on a whim
8. Care for Others → specific things they want to do for their friends & family
9. Impact → specific causes they care about and how they can influence them
10. Feelings → what specifically would that success feel like? Or that failure feel like?
11. Relationships → specific people they want to spend more time with or stop being around
12. Health → specific physical feelings they want to wake up with every morning

Importantly, each of these is a “spectrum.”

It’s not whether or not they are in pain, it’s *how much pain are they in.*

It’s not whether or not they have a goal, it’s *how badly do they want that goal.*

It’s not whether or not they see the cost of inaction, it’s *how badly do they want to avoid that fearful future situation.*

Each of these work together to get someone above a “change threshold” — and your job as is to help someone get above that threshold by making each state more specific.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!eBA1!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F90e2ee59-01d0-4916-a3aa-0cdff3d51363_560x531.png)

If you genuinely believe in your product or service, it is your moral responsibility to help people rise above this change threshold. The reason most people stay the same for their entire life is because THEY NEVER DO THIS.

- They never take a tough look at their current situation and realize it’s not what it should be.
- They never take the time to clearly define their desired situation and explore paths to get there.
- They never look at the long-term implications of their actions and see that they don’t want to end up there

And this is the beautiful opportunity you have in your sales process.

You are helping people, for potentially the first time in their life, actually reflect on what they want, what they don’t want, and what it looks like to get to where they want to go.

Once someone has clarity over where they are, where they want to go, and where they are headed if nothing changes, they will experience an internal tension to find a solution.

And that perfectly leads us to the second sale.

## Sale #2: The sale on this particular solution

When someone is sold on making a change, they will start to search for solutions.

In evaluating solutions, people will compare along the following lines:

1. **Effort & Sacrifice.** What are all the things they’re going to have to *start* doing that they aren’t currently doing and what are all the things they’re going to have to *stop* doing that they are currently doing.
2. **Likelihood of achievement FOR THEM.** How likely is it that if they put forth effort & make the sacrifices, that they will end up achieving their goal. Importantly, it must be likely to work *for their individual circumstances*. Everyone thinks they are special, so we must assure them that their specialness is not something that will derail their success.
3. **Investment.** How much money does it take to get started on this path and to continue on this path in the future?

So in pitching your solution, you want to make it:

- As easy as possible, without having to stop doing things they like doing
- Inexpensive relative to its likelihood of success
- With an exceptionally high likelihood of success

And at the same time, you want to make all other solutions look:

- As hard as possible + having to stop doing a ton of things they like
- As expensive as possible relative to its likelihood of success
- With little likelihood of it actually working out

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0M0o!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2bf9243e-9d78-46fa-90a2-ddc66353b1e8_791x648.png)

Building on our Advil example, we would position Ibuprofen as:

- **Easy.** Just one super small pill, easy to swallow, sold in a big bottle of 500 so you rarely have to go to the store. And you get to keep doing all the things you like to do. No side effects to work through.
- **Extremely likely to work.** Results happen in under an hour. You’ll feel like your joints are brand new again.
- **Inexpensive.** Just a couple bucks per bottle of pills.

And in comparing it to say, cold plunge therapy, you would position that as:

- **Difficult & sacrifice heavy.** Extremely painful, not a fun experience. Also out of the way, you have to drive all the way to the cold plunge place and hope there’s a spot available And after your cold plunge you shouldn’t exercise for a few hours. Plus you might need someone to drive you home.
- **Unlikely to work.** The benefits of cold plunging are mostly mental, without any long-term proof that your joints will stop hurting. And it might take many months of consistent cold plunging to feel the effects. And there’s a chance you go through all of that and still ache every day.
- **Expensive.** Roughly ~$50 per session, so about ~$500 per month if you go 2x per week.

Then you would repeat the process for all of the other things someone is considering instead of Ibuprofen. Once you’ve thrown daggers into all of them and they’re fully bought in on this solution, THEN you present the final sale which is on this specific brand.

Importantly, this is why many of the best marketers in the world market the CATEGORY, then let customers do their own research on the best brand within that category.

- Tesla doesn’t market Tesla, they market Electric Cars
- Starbucks doesn’t market Starbucks, they market Coffee

At a certain point, a brand cannot grow unless more people overall get interested in this particular type of solution. They have already converted all of the people bought in on this solution who were already comparing brands. Once that happens, they must shift their marketing and messaging “upstream” to get more people interested overall.

## Sale #3: The sale on the brand to deliver that solution

Once someone is bought in on 1) making a change and 2) the specific solution to deliver that change, they now will compare options *within* that solution.

To clarify the difference between Sale #2 (method) and Sale #3 (brand):

- **The method** is the category, mechanism, or approach that creates the change.
- **The brand** is the specific product, service, or company that delivers that method.

For example:

- Get out of joint pain
	- **Method:** Ibuprofen
		- **Brand:** Advil
- Lose weight
	- **Method:** Calorie tracking
		- **Brand:** MyFitnessPal
- Build strength
	- **Method:** Barbell training
		- **Brand:** Starting Strength
- Get around a city
	- **Method:** Ridesharing
		- **Brand:** Uber
- Learn a new language
	- **Method:** App-based language learning
		- **Brand:** Duolingo

A simple test:

**Could someone fully believe in this method and still choose a competitor instead of us?**

If yes, you’re still talking about the method.

If no, you’re probably talking about the brand.

Now, once a prospect is at this stage, they will actually use the same criteria when they were evaluating the different solutions. But now they will be comparing options within the same solution, so there will be more minor changes/differences.

Your goal during this stage is to help them understand that our solution is *unique* relative to the other options out there, rather than saying we’re better/faster/cheaper.

This is extremely important. You do not try to differentiate with a relative comparison. You do not not acknowledge competition.

You instead want to explain our solution as the simultaneous explanation for why 1) what we do is different and 2) why everything else they’ve tried in the past hasn’t worked.

Finishing out the example for Advil, we now position our version of Ibuprofen as the only option by emphasizing:

- **Credibility of our track record.** Advil has been the leading ibuprofen drug for the last 20 years with over 10 million people currently taking it daily.
- **Highest likelihood of achievement.** Studies have shown that Ibuprofen’s unique *slow release* methodology leads to the longest lasting pain relief per dose. We are the only provider on the market who is able to use this patented technology.

Importantly, this is where intangible “brand affinity” comes into play. Some people buy from brands that are more expensive, or more niche, or more “like them” even if there could potentially be a better deal elsewhere.

And this is also where a new competitor will pop up: “doing it myself.”

If you sell an educational product or service, you must first sell them on making a change and using your particular method for implementing that change. However, at that point their main decision will come down to either working with you, working with someone else, or doing it themselves. Which means part of Sale #3 is building doubt by explaining all the things someone would have to do if they tried to implement their method on their own.

Now, once someone is fully bought in on making a change, using our method to implement the change, and working with us to implement that method, closing the sale will come down to one thing: logistics.

### Logistics

Depending on what you sell, logistics will either be a minimal factor or a huge factor.

Generally speaking, the more change required from someone or the larger the investment, the more likely logistics are to play a role in the final decision.

There are three logistics that must also be “handled” before someone will buy:

- **Time** → They must feel they have enough time in the day in the “season” to get started using your product or service
- **Support** → They must have the approval of their significant other or business partner to pursue this path
- **Money** → They must have the minimum amount of money it takes to get started in their possession

An important realization about logistics is they are easy to overcome when someone is *fully bought in on all 3 sales.* When someone is dead-set on making a change working with you to implement your method:

- They will find the time to make it work
- They will win the support of their significant other
- They will find the money to make it happen

And the reverse is also true. When someone is *not* dead-set on making a change, or on your method, or on working with you, *they will rarely, if ever, say that explicitly.* Instead, they will express their uncertainty by blaming a logistic.

- They will say they are too busy
- They will say others are stopping them from doing it
- They will say they are not financially able to make it happen

But the example I always share with people I work with: if someone came to you and offered you a brand new Ferrari for $1,000 - do you think logistics would get in the way of buying that?

Absolutely not. I don’t care how busy you are, how much your wife hates cars, or how few dollars are in your bank account. ***You would find a way to buy the $300,000 car for $1,000.***

And that is why trying to “objection handle” these types of logistics is typically a losing game. They are not bought in on making a change, your method, or your brand. And so it’s better to spend time increasing certainty on the first three sales before spending more time on these.

Now - there are certainly legitimate logistical objections that can prevent someone who is fully bought in from buying. But I use this test to illustrate that more often than not, these objections provide cover for an earlier sale that has been missed. Your job is to recognize which of these you’re dealing with and either strengthen the necessary belief, or accept it might be a legitimate logistical objection.

## Putting all of this together into a set of “Necessary Beliefs”

*Again credit to [Cole Gordon for coining the term “Buying Beliefs”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VVxmHLBv5mw) and [Todd Brown for coining the term “Necessary Beliefs.](https://toddbrown.me/the-most-important-question-when-marketing/)” Highly recommend watching both of those videos.*

---

Putting this all together, a great sales process involves building all of the following beliefs in some way:

1. Sale on making a change of some kind
	- **Pain** → they must want to change their current situation
		- **Desire** → they must have something drawing them forward
		- **Cost of Inaction** → they must believe there is a cost of not taking action today
2. Sale on the solution being the best way to deliver that change
	- **Superiority** → Belief in this solution being better than other potential solutions
		- **Fit** → Belief they can have success with this solution in their unique situation
3. Sale on us being the best option to deliver that solution
	- **Doubt** → Doubt they can just do it on their own
		- **Method** → Trust in your method for delivering the solution
		- **Credibility** → Trust we will deliver the thing we say we will deliver
4. Logistics that enable them to get started:
	- **Time** → Enough time in the day & in this season to get started
		- **Support** → Spouse/partner will approve of going down this path
		- **Money** → They have the minimum amount of money it takes to get started in their possession or access to get it

With this list in mind, let’s look at some examples of how we do this inside our business. And then we’ll take a look at how you can use this framework to improve your content, emails, landing pages, and sales calls.

## The 3 Sales Applied To Our Portfolio of Businesses

Over the last 6 years, we’ve operated 8 major types of monetization methods ([full breakdown here](https://aioperatornewsletter.substack.com/p/full-guide-digital-monetization-methods)):

- Done-for-you agency service
- 1:1 coaching/consulting
- Evergreen coaching program (1:1 + group)
- Cohort-based experiences
- Digital products
- Paid communities
- Paid newsletters
- Software/SaaS

Each of these uses a different funnel, different content, and a different sales process. But the 3 sales are always the same.

Before you think about *how* to sell something (what emails to write, what type of content to make, what to put on your landing page, etc.) you need to start with what beliefs you need to build. Once you have that chain set up, then you can figure out how to use each touchpoint to strengthen it.

Most readers of this newsletter will fall into one of the 4 following monetization methods:

- Evergreen coaching program
- Done for you agency service
- Digital product/cohort-based experience
- Paid community

So let’s look at our versions of each of these verticals so you can see how we engineer beliefs across each of them.

### Evergreen Coaching Program (Premium Ghostwriting Academy)

PGA is our 16-week program helping freelance writers & 9-5ers landing premium ghostwriting clients. Sold for $5,000+ spread out over 16 weeks.

Looking at the 3 sales we must engineer:

- Sale #1: Making a change of some kind
	- **Pain.** They must recognize they are stuck in a job that caps their income and controls their time
		- **Desire.** They must want a different lifestyle than the one their job is providing
		- **Cost of inaction.** They must recognize that nothing will change if they stay on their current path, with things likely to feel worse over time.
- Sale #2: Ghostwriting being the best solution
	- **Superiority.** They must believe for those with basic writing skills & willingness to put in significant effort, this is the best online business model. There is no audience needed, no product to build, and no startup cost. Positioned correctly, clients will pay 3-5k/month retainers for you to run their email & social media.
		- **Fit.** They must believe that they have the skillset required to execute this model.
- Sale #3: PGA being the place to learn it
	- **Doubt.** They must believe trying to do this alone, or with just free content, is likely to lead to months, if not years of trial and error.
		- **Method.** They must believe that a 16-week, guided 1:1 coaching system is the best way to make progress.
		- **Credibility.** They must believe that we provide the accountability, educational materials, and mentorship we advertise.
- Logistics:
	- **Time.** They have 10-15 hours per week to dedicate to this alongside their full-time job
		- **Support.** Their spouse or significant other will encourage them to take this path.
		- **Money.** They have access to the amount needed to get started on our lowest payment option.

As you can see, this is a relatively complex sale. And generally speaking, the more complex the sale, the better it is to have a 1:1 salesperson involved in it, which is exactly how our sales process works.

Now let’s compare this to a simpler sale, our Ship 30 for 30 cohort-based writing course.

### Cohort-Based Experience (Ship 30 for 30)

Ship 30 for 30 is our 30-day writing experience that helps beginners publish their first 30 pieces of writing online.

Ship 30’s mission is to help 1 million people start writing online. So to do that, we have to start “broader” and get more people *interested in writing at all*. We cannot only market to people who are already thinking about writing online. We need to make them aware that they should start building an online presence somehow. Then we help them understand writing online is the best way to build that presence. Then, and only then, do we help them understand that Ship 30 is the best way to start writing online.

- **Sale #1: Making a change to build a presence online**
	- **Pain.** They feel they have something worth saying but haven’t been able to do so. They see other people building audiences & online presences and reaping the rewards as a result. They keep getting passed over by other people who are more well-known.
		- **Desire.** They want people to know them for something. They want inbound opportunities from people who stumble across them. They want status and reputation that opens doors for them in the future. And they want to connect with likeminded people who see the world the same was them.
		- **Cost of Inaction.** They must recognize that another year staying invisible means another year of watching less skilled people with more attention get promotions, more opportunities, and more clients.
- **Sale #2: Writing online is the best way to build that presence**
	- **Superiority.** They must believe writing is the fastest & lowest-barrier way to start. Better than videos or podcasting because both require hours of filming, editing, and equipment. They must believe writing is quick to produce, easy to repurpose, and has the secondary benefit of forcing them to think clearly about something. And they must see the creation of a written library as something that compounds in the future.
		- **Fit.** They must believe they don’t need to be a “writer” to start writing. They must understand that if they can explain what they do in conversation, they can write it online.
- **Sale #3: Ship 30 is the best way to start writing online**
