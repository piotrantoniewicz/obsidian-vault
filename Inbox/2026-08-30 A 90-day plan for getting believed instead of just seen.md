You've probably heard (or said)...

*LinkedIn's becoming Facebook!*

And that's lookin' pretty damn true from my seat.

I'm seeing more glam shots in Greece from someone insisting ChatGPT made their vacation amazing because they automated the decision-making out of their travel planning.

**Because they get the most engagement and views.**

Then people see that type of content performs best, and publish more of it. That’s life for some. An annoying cycle to others.

The "old LinkedIn" made us better at our jobs. "Knowledge posts" from our favorite professionals were like free mentorship to help us excel.

If you feel like knowledge posts are fading as LinkedIn continues its [rapid shift from the professional network into a true social media platform](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/5-new-tools-change-how-you-post-partner-get-paid-devin-reed-sze0c/?trackingId=EW4ksPlQS8%2Bfk4AUddkbAQ%3D%3D) like IG, TikTok, and Facebook — you're not wrong.

There's tension growing between how people use the platform:

**You're either chasing attention or building authority.**

So what should I do? Post what gets the most views? Or the stuff that builds credibility?

It depends on your goals.

**If your goal (and business) relies on getting eyeballs, you need more attention.**

*A lot of it.*

**If you want to sell software or services, then you need more authority.**

Luckily, it's not binary. If you want maximum growth, your strategy needs both.

This applies to me, too. I'm both a business owner who sells services and a creator who sells brand deals.

**My reputation and pipeline depend on building my credibility.**

But my brand deals depend on volume of reactions, views, and engagement rates.

I tell my team and hired guns: I want to create the best content possible. But I'm not giving marketing tips during a cold plunge for views.

I got a reputation to protect.

So as I rebuild my LinkedIn strategy, I decided to test this hunch against my own content. I analyzed my content and found something interesting:

![Treść artykułu](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D5612AQHRPnYhZoz35A/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/B56aA0kzePIAAQ-/0/1787588458778?e=1789603200&v=beta&t=49D3hzpbq0EKs8S74QAvaBEnSluYyjRM1P-hcY9ECk8)

Here’s what I see:

- **Knowledge posts have less mass appeal, so they'll have less reach**. And that’s okay. Plan for it, don’t deny it. I’m going to set a new baseline for these types of posts then try to beat it instead of comparing them to personal posts.
- **Personal posts are important, but they need focus**. I've been posting whatever's in front of me. Going forward it's golf, sneakers, milestones, and behind-the-scenes stories. That’s the lane.
- **Sponsored posts carry links, and LinkedIn suppresses them**. That explains the 9%. Not much I can do there. It's the cost of doing that business. Plus reach isn't always the goal on those anyway. Conversions are, and this chart doesn't show them.
- **Distribution posts are being neglected.** That's where I launch gated guides, tools, and YouTube videos, but I’ve only posted twice in 90 days. Time to step it up.
- **I only published one proof post in 90 days**. I have dozens of clients, so this one stings. I want to build the habit of posting one named client 2-3/month.

That's the analysis. Here's my action plan for the next 90 days.

## Cadence: 20 posts per month, 5x a week

I'm posting more, not because volume fixes anything, but because I added two standing commitments and I'm not robbing my best content to pay for them.

If I hadn’t just hired someone full time, this would be way more daunting.

- 3x Monday through Friday
- [*LinkedIn Growth*](https://www.linkedin.com/newsletters/linkedin-growth-6742676758434328576/) newsletter every Sunday
- One repurposed video a week
- Everything drops between 7–9am PST

## My content mix gets more disciplined

Last quarter I posted whatever the week handed me. Now every slot has a job before I sit down to write.

- **Authority: 8/month.** Two of those are proof posts — a named client with a real outcome. That's the only floor I'm holding myself to.
- **Personal**: **4/month**. Golf, sneakers, milestones, BTS.
- **Sponsored**: **4/month.** Hard cap. Money pressures a calendar, and I won't notice until it's six.
- **Distribution: 4/month.** Gated guides, tools, YouTube videos. This one's been starving.

Remember that I’m working towards reclassification.

So breaking outside of authority posts is fine. I did it on purpose last quarter for Cannes, LinkedIn's private event, and sitting down with Ty Heath at LinkedIn HQ. Opportunistic, and worth every post. Now it's back to balance.

## I’m running two experiments

Two bets I'm running that I'm not certain about yet.

1. **Repurposing my best YouTube Shorts and IG Reels to LinkedIn.** One a week, mostly golf one-shots. These already won on another platform, so I want to see if that travels.
2. **The LinkedIn Growth newsletter.** Already in flight, already outperforming what I expected. That one's earning its own edition soon.

## How I'll know any of this worked

I'm changing what I measure, because the news rules value saves and comments over followers and likes.

- New baselines per bucket. Knowledge posts stop getting graded against sneakers.
- Saves and sends per 10K, not raw reach. A save means someone plans to come back to it.
- Same report, same window, 90 days from now — and I'll publish it either way.
