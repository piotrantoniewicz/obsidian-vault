Stock donations offer a valuable way for donors to support your cause while avoiding capital gains tax, making them an appealing alternative to cash-based giving.

Incorporating stock giving into your year-end campaign can therefore welcome larger gifts, enabling your organization to fully benefit from the increased generosity of the [Giving Season](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/giving-season).

Below, I’ll explain why supporters may lean towards stock giving this year-end, and what you can do to prepare for this shift in giving behavior.

---

## Key Takeaways 💡

- **Educate supporters and enable stock giving on your year-end campaign.** This allows your organization to capture larger gifts from supporters who prefer non-cash alternatives.
- **Show donors the impact stock donations can have.** Clearly explain the difference a stock gift can have by using concise messaging that connects the gift to the outcome.
- **Prepare your team ahead of the year-end rush.** Make sure fundraising and development teams are aligned on messaging and know how to answer any questions related to stock gifts.

## Why More Donors May Give Stock This Year-End

Due to recent market growth and increased tax benefits for donors, non-cash donations like [stock and cryptocurrency](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/capture-more-donor-wealth-with-crypto-and-stock) are quickly becoming a mainstream giving solution for supporters who want to elevate their impact.

The Giving Block’s [2026 Annual Report](https://thegivingblock.com/annual-report/) shows that online stock donation volume **increased** **by** **127%** in 2025, with the average gift climbing past **$51,000**. Plus, supporters who give non-cash donations give roughly [10X more](https://www.mrss.com/lab/new-research-understanding-why-donors-choose-dafs-stocks-and-more/) than they did in the past.

See how the average stock donation size has scaled rapidly year over year from 2022 to 2025:

![stock donation analysis year-over-year](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/how-to-prepare-for-year-end-stock-giving_stock-donation-analysis-year-over-year.png)

**Source**: [2026 Annual Report on Crypto Philanthropy and Digital Fundraising Innovation](https://thegivingblock.com/annual-report/)

New research from M+R also shows that [68%](https://www.mrss.com/lab/new-research-understanding-why-donors-choose-dafs-stocks-and-more/) of surveyed donors say the biggest influence on how much they give is the organizations who need their support most. Tax implications, market performance, and financial advice matter far less to donors.

### 2\. Guide prospective donors

M+R’s study shows that [68%](https://www.mrss.com/lab/new-research-understanding-why-donors-choose-dafs-stocks-and-more) of existing supporters already know about donating in stock.

However, prospective donors are far less familiar with alternative giving options, which is why it’s important for your organization to [educate supporters](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/explain-stock-crypto) about stock donations.

![Awareness of Alternative Giving Methods](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/how-to-prepare-for-year-end-stock-giving_guide-prospective-donors.png)

**Source**: [M+R](https://www.mrss.com/lab/new-research-understanding-why-donors-choose-dafs-stocks-and-more/)

Include relevant information about stock and crypto giving on your end-of year campaign page, in emails and newsletters, and in other communications.

Donors value simplicity, so keep things straightforward by making the option visible and redirecting supporters to the information they need.

### 3\. Align your team on messaging

Ensure everyone in your organization – including fundraising and finance teams, and [development staff](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/tackling-the-nonprofit-development-staffing-dilemma) – is aware that you accept stock donations and knows how to answer any questions new prospects may have.

Use clear, concise messaging in your appeal to communicate the impact a stock donation can have.

> For example, instead of *“Make a stock donation for increased tax benefits,”* consider something like, *“A $\_\_\_\_\_ gift in stock provides a year’s supply of food and shelter to 10 homeless families.”*

Here are some basic points to align on:

- How should staff explain stock giving to [prospects](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/prospect-research) and donors?
- Where can supporters make a gift (e.g., your donation form)?
- Who handles questions about the transfer of stock?
- Who should be notified when a stock gift comes through?

In addition, consider how donors will be acknowledged for end-of-year stock donations. To minimize your workload and streamline your year-end fundraising, consider sending automated receipts so no donation goes unacknowledged.
