In many low-resource regions of the world, there is just one ophthalmologist for every 100,000 people. There aren't enough humans to do the work. Yet most blindness is preventable or treatable if patients are screened and connected to care in time. [Visilant](https://www.linkedin.com/company/visilant/) is an AI-powered nonprofit making that possible.

Their team built a low-cost smartphone adapter for community health workers to intervene. They capture clinical-grade eye images. AI then analyzes those images and patient history to diagnose conditions like cataracts and infections with near-clinical accuracy. Research-backed solutions changing people’s lives. Real people doing the work. Powered by AI.

Visilant has already screened more than 30,000 patients and expects to reach more than one million within three years. For them to do it well and at scale, funders need to fund the tech.

I know funders want to get on board. But many have questions about how to review grant proposals when new, AI-powered organizations like Visilant come across their desk. My philosophy is that the best way to learn is to play.

That’s why [Fast Forward](https://www.linkedin.com/company/fast_forward/) built the [**AI Proposal Assessment Tool**](https://www.ffwd.org/ai-proposal-assessment). It’s designed to help funders evaluate AI solutions submitted in grantee proposals. You can run any nonprofit AI proposal through a series of ready-to-use AI prompts. It works on any native AI platform.

We want to keep humans in the loop, not take them out of it. The AI Proposal Assessment Tool centers grantmakers, supporting the decision-making phase with externally validated research. So you fund AI with confidence.

I’m sure you’ve got questions. I have answers.

### Why would I consider an AI proposal if tech is not a part of my grantmaking?

Tech is a huge unlock for nonprofits to scale their impact. It lets organizations do more with less. Expand high-quality healthcare in rural communities, reach more students with personalized learning, connect families with public benefits that are otherwise complicated to access — and so much more. The AI Proposal Assessment Tool determines how the use of tech can affect change. You’re still the issue-area expert.

### I’m not sure if AI aligns with my foundation’s values. How will the tool help me make that determination?

First, the tool itself is meant to be used in *your* native environment. Not ours. (That’s why it’s a prompt library — not a web app.) You copy and paste each prompt into your preferred (or required) AI platform. And since it's your native environment, the chatbot should already have knowledge about your foundation’s values, mission, and strategic plan. If it doesn’t, you can share that context privately and securely. Once you’re set up, the tool can help you look for alignment between your mission and approach and the nonprofit’s solution.

### How do I know if AI is the right solution to the problem?

AI is not a silver bullet. The AI Proposal Assessment Tool does not assume that AI can solve the problem. Or that an AI solution is worth the investment or the risk. The first prompt is, “Write questions to ask the applicant to better understand the evidence behind the problem and why AI is the right intervention.” Start there. The rest of the prompts will help you dig deeper on the nonprofit’s capacity, resources, and approach to responsible AI.

### What if I disagree with the AI chatbot’s grantmaking recommendations?

The AI Proposal Assessment Tool doesn't make recommendations. That’s not the point. It prompts you to ask the next best question of your potential grantees. You maintain the decision-making power.

I want this tool to be as helpful as possible. So I’m open to feedback. Next time you receive a nonprofit AI proposal, run it through the AI Proposal Assessment Tool. If you’re really feeling inspired, ask AI to write a proposal for you — then critique the AI!

There’s a feedback form built directly into the tool. Let me know what you think. Happy playing!
