Public media stations are rarely short on good ideas for digital revenue. Teams are constantly throwing tactics at the wall to see what sticks. But without a structured system to test, measure, and scale those efforts, even the most innovative ideas can stall out. This was the challenge facing WFAE in Charlotte when they joined the Knight-Funded Digital Revenue Project, a partnership with Greater Public and News Revenue Hub. They were already building successful digital connections with their audience, but they lacked structure and a cohesive plan to implement and achieve the growth in engagement and revenue they knew they were capable of.

They had tried different things like using tip jars with online articles, and varying calls to action (CTAs) on pages of the site and in newsletters, but hadn’t conducted much testing or planning around developing new efforts. One of the first projects they looked at with News Revenue Hub was examining and updating CTAs across all channels to ensure consistent messaging, especially following the federal funding changes.

## Moving From Guesswork to "Sprints"

As their skillset grew, the WFAE team began to conduct A/B testing. For instance, they found that a pop-up or fly-in call to action generated the most revenue, regardless of the visitor’s entry page.

![](https://greaterpublic.org/app/uploads/2026/06/Screenshot-2026-05-29-171413.png)

This WFAE pop-up generated $20,807 in revenue.

They also learned that placing donation request modules at the top of an article page was far more effective than placing them at the bottom. To manage this rapid experimentation, the team adopted a tech-industry practice popularized by Google: “sprints.” Instead of getting bogged down by massive, months-long digital overhauls, the station began working in short, highly focused one- or two-week blocks dedicated to hitting specific, bite-sized goals.

This structural shift brought a vital new level of accountability to the organization. As WFAE President & CEO Ju-Don Marshall explained, the framework provided a clear roadmap for tracking performance: “News Revenue Hub brought to us the project management skillset to be able to establish and set sprints and then the discipline around the marketing elements and KPIs, like how do you measure what your baselines are and how do you know if you’re seeing an ROI based on that.”

## Defining Accountability Across Departments

This new level of data discipline required greater clarity on who owned which task. To achieve this across different station departments, WFAE implemented the RACI framework (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed). Used as a blueprint for keeping the team on task and meeting milestones, the framework helps WFAE map stakeholders and identify bottlenecks.

### Quick Tool Breakdown: What Is a RACI Matrix?

To keep projects moving without endless consensus-seeking, the RACI model explicitly defines roles for every digital milestone:

- **Responsible:** The person who actually does the work.
- **Accountable:** The one person with final approval power and ownership over the outcome.
- **Consulted:** The subject matter experts whose input is needed before work is finalized.
- **Informed:** The stakeholders who are kept up-to-date on progress but don’t vote on decisions.

Digital Solutions Manager Demitrius Suggs explained, “We look at who’s responsible, who’s accountable, who’s a collaborator, and who’s informed. By mapping that out and seeing some bottlenecks, that is helping us a ton.”

In public media, projects can stall when too many people feel they need to consult on every decision, or conversely, because everyone assumes someone else is handling it. By clearly defining roles, the WFAE team reduced the endless email chains and ambiguity that typically slow down digital campaigns. Decisions that used to take weeks of cross-departmental debate could now be made in days. After successfully using the RACI framework to plan their digital sprints, the WFAE team is now applying it to other cross-departmental projects across the organization. “We’ve also become much more intentional about assigning responsibilities based on each team’s strengths and areas of expertise,” Suggs added. “Today, we’re typically finalizing plans weeks in advance, whereas in previous drives we were often still working through key details as the campaign launched.”

## The Payoff: Exceeding the Budget

The results of this newfound organizational agility have been, in the words of Marshall, “remarkable.” In FY25, they budgeted for $25,000 in digital revenue and achieved $38,000—53% over budget. As FY26 approached, the loss of federal funding became a strong case message, and WFAE was ready for even greater success. In FY26 (through December), they budgeted for $50,000 in digital revenue and raised $133,000, representing a 166% increase above their full-year goal and a massive 248% increase year-over-year digital performance.

## Scaling with Automation and Infrastructure

Looking ahead, WFAE’s work with News Revenue Hub focuses on scaling these successes through automation and infrastructure. The team is currently developing an automated welcome series for new newsletter subscribers—complete with an audience survey to better understand user expectations—and crafting high-impact messaging for the station’s upcoming 45th-year celebration. “We’ve talked to \[News Revenue Hub\] about messaging with impact to prompt action. We have so much opportunity,” Marshall reflected.

Capitalizing on that opportunity will also require evaluating WFAE’s backend technology, including Customer Relationship Management (CRM) tools and Customer Data Platforms (CDPs). For Marshall, optimizing those systems goes hand-in-hand with supporting her staff’s evolving roles. “Demetrius \[Suggs\] has to be the product expert, the event platform expert, \[and\] the registration expert,” Marshall noted. “As we think about what could really strengthen our organization, we have to look at where we have deficits and how can we start to provide additional support in some of those areas?”

## A Superpower of Flexibility

When asked what they have learned about their strengths as a team through the last year and a half, Marshall highlighted their agility. “One of the strengths is our flexibility because we’re a small team. We’re used to being nimble, shifting gears and directions. For the most part, you have a team that’s willing to try new things, experiment, and reach beyond what we think we’re capable of doing. I think that’s one of the superpowers of the team that we have.”

Demetrius Suggs agreed, expanding, “We’ll be in a meeting and report out what we’re doing and people get excited. I definitely think people are open; everything is connected to digital somehow. Everyone is willing to jump in and experiment and try different things.”

What started as a project to optimize digital fundraising has fundamentally transformed how WFAE operates as an entire organization. By breaking down departmental walls, defining clear accountability through RACI, and embracing the rapid pace of digital sprints, the station didn’t just beat its revenue goals—it built a culture of continuous learning and experimentation.

WFAE’s journey proves that the path to financial sustainability in the digital age isn’t about finding a single magic tool. It’s about building an agile, collaborative team that isn’t afraid to step outside its comfort zone. As the public media landscape continues to shift, WFAE has created a repeatable blueprint for success, proving that when stations have the courage to try things they’ve never done, they can reach heights they never thought possible.
