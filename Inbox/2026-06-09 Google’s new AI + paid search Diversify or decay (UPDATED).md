With Google’s [move to embed AI into every search](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/google-marketing-live-2026-collection/), our [predictions last year](https://www.mrss.com/lab/four-scary-predictions-for-digital-advertising-in-2026/) have come true (for better or worse). If your nonprofit has a paid search program, things are going to change *very* quickly — expect that your search ad program will likely look drastically different a few months from now.

Take a nice, deep breath. Then read on for how to keep up.

As users shift to getting answers from Google’s AI Overviews or AI Mode — or increasingly using LLMs instead of search engines altogether — the way they interact with organic and paid search is evolving. Users are shifting from searching a handful of keywords that result in a long page of blue links to receiving a full answer to their query in an AI overview with no need to click to a website, or searching in paragraphs and having back-and-forth “discussions” with their search tools.

#### Google Ads: Where Are We Now?

For many users, AI search has become a primary discovery and answer tool. They don’t click to a website, look at a primary source, or scroll down to see the non-AI search results. They start and end their journey all within one AI conversation.

Along with Google’s new “AI Intelligent Search box” comes **a new generation of ad formats built specifically for AI Mode, including conversational discovery ads, highlighted answers, and lead ads.** These new features are designed to live inside AI-generated conversations rather than the traditional results page. This update means users are even more likely to stay exclusively within the Google environment.

Here’s what all of this means for paid search.

#### Hard truth #1: Your search ads are being seen less and less, and to get them seen, Google wants you to use its AI tools more and more.

Traditional search ads are being pushed further down the page, by the AI Overview box. Here’s what that looks like in practice:

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AEO-search-blog-images-03.png)

**How to adapt:** Make sure you’re using broad match, AI Max, or Performance Max. These bidding strategies are the only way to make your ads appear within AI Overviews. *Note that this is not currently available for political advertisers.*

- *Another note: If you are currently just running only brand-focused campaigns, opening up to these bidding strategies will expand the queries you appear on to non-brand terms as well. So there will likely be a tradeoff between appearing in AI Overviews vs potential performance implications for expanding to non-brand terms.*

#### Hard truth #2: Searches are becoming conversations, not a few keywords in a box.

As users adapt to the new search conversation style, they are likely to search transactional keywords (like concise donate terms and brand terms) less and less over time. We anticipate a shift to more informational queries (ex. “I want to sign up for a marathon where I can support cancer research. Where do I start?”) in LLM’s and AIO/AI Mode.

The new conversational ads available in AI Mode are dynamic, with Google generating the ad copy directly based on its analysis of the user’s intent, tailoring the creative specifically to that user’s conversation. [This is just another example of the trend in the ads industry toward personalizing content for the user experience.](https://www.mrss.com/lab/everything-everywhere-take-your-ads-to-the-multiverse/)

- *Once again, these new AI ad formats are not available for political ads. Google’s official policy on what qualifies an ad as* [*political is here*](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6014595?sjid=14213186703237869114-NA#zippy=%2Ctaiwan%2Csouth-korea%2Cunited-states-us-election-ads%2Ctroubleshooter%2Cstate-and-local-election-ads-in-the-united-states)*, but we’ve found that several ads that follow these guidelines have still been flagged. It’s worth appealing/talking to your Google rep if this is a persistent issue!* Google’s AI Conversation Ad Example:

**How to adapt:** In your paid search campaigns, continue to maximize brand-focused keywords/campaigns as much as you can while the volume is still there.

In a parallel path, start building a foundation of AI-driven campaigns, including AI Max and Performance Max, to ensure your ads are appearing in the new Google AI search experience, and taking advantage of AI-powered ad formats like conversation discovery ads.

And don’t forget to make sure your brand is appearing how you want it to in AI Overviews when people search for your organization. This is a key part of what’s known as Answer Engine Optimization (AEO, also referred to as Generative Engine Optimization or GEO) — the new toolset for ensuring visibility in an AI-first search landscape. We talked about Google’s most recent changes and what it means for organic search [here](https://www.mrss.com/lab/the-impact-of-ai-search-during-end-of-year-the-news-isnt-good/), highlighting the need to implement core AEO practices, including:

- Monitoring what appears in AI overviews and on LLM chatbots for key brand and non-brand searches, and what first-party and third-party sources are being cited.
- Optimize your website content for AI Search, and implement earned and social media strategies to gain more third party content for answer engines to pull from.

- If your organization isn’t showing up in competitive non-brand prompts, identify which queries your brand is not appearing in and ensure that you have a paid search ads campaign serving to those queries/keywords.
	- To use the International Relief Organizations prompt from above as an example, if you are an international relief organization and the AI Overview did not list your company in their response, ensure that you are bidding on “International Relief” broad match/AI max keywords to help ensure your brand appears near your competitors.

#### Ads Within ChatGPT and Other LLMs: Where Are We Now?

[We predicted this one, as well](https://www.mrss.com/lab/four-scary-predictions-for-digital-advertising-in-2026/) — ChatGPT rolled out an ads offering early this year. While they started with a high commitment of $200K and an outrageous $60 CPM, they are quickly working on lowering the barrier to entry and adapting their offering to a more direct response product (similar to Google search). They’ve already introduced a self-serve ads manager, partnered with third parties like Stackadapt and Criteo, launched pixels for tracking, and lowered the minimum spend. All these changes indicate that OpenAI is gunning for Google’s business and trying to stake their claim in the AI search ad space. How well they perform remains to be seen.

In terms of the other major players:

- Claude has confirmed they are not planning to roll out an ad product (and ironically are running an [ad campaign](http://youtube.com/watch?v=3sVD3aG_azw&list=PLf2m23nhTg1OW258b3XBiJME7tgrRk-KI&index=4) trolling ChatGPT about it).
- Perplexity piloted an ad offering last year but has since abandoned it.

**Final Thoughts:** Search is shifting to an AI-driven, personalized ad experience. This has big implications for political advertisers who aren’t allowed to participate in this new AI paid search world. For those advertisers, a big shift in strategy and channel diversification is necessary.

For other nonprofits, the future belongs to those who invest early in bridging the gap between paid visibility (AI Max/Performance Max) and organic authority (Answer Engine Optimization).

The later you start monitoring how your brand and issues appear to your donors and advocates reaching you through AI – and adjusting your strategy accordingly – the more likely it is you’ll be left behind.
