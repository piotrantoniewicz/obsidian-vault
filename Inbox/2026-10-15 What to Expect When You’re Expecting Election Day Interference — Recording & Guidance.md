### What to Expect After Election Day

We teamed up with experts from Nonprofit VOTE, Protect Democracy, and the National Council of Nonprofits to walk through how 2026 is different from previous elections, how to plan for Election Day, and — critically — for the 61 days between Election Day and when the new Congress is sworn in.

Many organizations are thinking carefully about how to ensure that every vote is counted. We’ve learned from past elections that there is a playbook for election interference — it begins with disinformation and can escalate to threats not to certify. The good news is that together we can interrupt this kind of escalation. If you mitigate the first step (disinformation that destroys trust in elections), you decrease the likelihood of bigger events happening later.

### How Nonprofits Should Prepare

Nonprofits have a unique role to play — by helping to reduce the escalation of threats, nonprofits can help reduce the chance of interference EARLY. These are things we’re thinking about and planning for, and we encourage you to do the same.

**1\. Be Ready to Combat Disinformation.** IF we see false claims of fraud or delay about the count begin to swirl (often online), we debunk and don’t amplify.

- We share the facts from trusted sources, like our local election officials.
- We don’t repeat the lie, even to argue with it.
- We lead with shared values, name the bad actor, intent, or motivation, and pivot to what we are fighting FOR.

**2\. Be Ready to Respond to Attacks on Officials and the Results.** IF claims of fraud grow into demands for investigations or attacks on election officials counting our votes, we vocally have election officials’ backs.

- We thank our election officials publicly and stand with them if they are attacked.
- We remind our communities that election officials are [following an established process](https://protectdemocracy.org/work/election-certification-explained/) with checks and balances that account for every ballot cast.

**3\. Be Ready to Condemn Threats Not to Certify Results.** IF we see any efforts to delay certification or refusal to certify, we immediately condemn and say count every vote.

- We condemn threats to certification right away, before they can build.
- We say plainly that certification is a required duty, while our legal partners work to make sure officials do their duty.
- We amplify the call to count every vote in the weeks after Election Day.

We also recommend you get connected to expert guidance so you can be informed in case we see interference. The Election Safeguards Response Network (ESRN) is a nonpartisan network of local, state, and national partners working to make sure that people can vote and that every vote is counted. They work closely with election protection networks and coordinate messaging and litigation strategies.
