Fall is one of the best seasons to be a fundraiser. The air gets crisp, people get generous, and there’s a natural energy around community and gratitude that makes donors more receptive than almost any other time of year. And yet, most nonprofits funnel everything into GivingTuesday and a year-end push, leaving a lot of opportunity untouched between September and December.

If you’re looking for fresh ways to engage your community this fall, you’re in the right place. These 19 fall fundraising ideas range from low-lift campaigns you can launch in a week to community events that build real relationships with donors. Some are in-person, some are virtual, and most can flex to fit whatever your bandwidth looks like right now.

## 1\. Fall Recipe Contest

People are already cooking in October. Sourdough, soup, apple crisp, chili, whatever your community loves to make when the weather turns. A fall recipe contest taps into something people are genuinely excited about and gives you great content in the process.

[Sell tickets](https://www.causevox.com/event-ticketing-forms/) for participants to submit their recipes, and invite your broader community to vote for a small donation. You can keep it fully virtual by collecting submissions online, or make it in-person with a tasting event where attendees pay to sample and vote.

Either way, it’s the kind of fundraiser that feels fun rather than obligatory. Share the winning recipes in a [post-event email](https://www.causevox.com/blog/connect-event-giving-auctions-follow-up/) to keep the momentum going and give donors something to actually use.

## 2\. Farm-to-Table Donor Dinner

A farm-to-table dinner is one of the most effective ways to [deepen relationships](https://www.causevox.com/blog/strengthen-donor-relationships/) with your [mid-level](https://www.causevox.com/blog/mid-level-donor-strategy/) and [major donors](https://www.causevox.com/blog/major-donors-how-to-attract-and-keep-them/). It’s intimate, it’s memorable, and it gives people a reason to show up that goes beyond a standard ask.

Partner with a local restaurant or chef who’s willing to donate their time or offer a discounted rate for a cause they believe in. Keep the guest list small enough that every person in the room feels seen. The evening should feel like a genuine thank-you, not a pitch. Share an [impact story](https://www.causevox.com/blog/storytelling-in-fundraising-campaigns/) over dinner, let your executive director speak personally about the work, and make the ask feel like a natural next step rather than the whole point. Set up a simple [giving page](https://www.causevox.com/fundraising-sites-pages/) so guests can give or [pledge](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donation-pledges-nonprofit/) right from their [phones](https://www.causevox.com/blog/mobile-wallets-and-nonprofit-fundraising/) before they leave.

## 3\. Hot Cocoa or Cider Bar at a Community Event

This one is less of a standalone fundraiser and more of a smart presence-building move. If there’s a fall community event already happening in your area, like a parade, school fair, or neighborhood market, set up a hot cocoa or cider bar and show up as a community partner.

Charge a small fee per cup or set up a donation jar with a QR code that links to your [giving page](https://www.causevox.com/blog/create-a-donation-page-for-free/). It’s low-cost, low-lift, and puts your organization in front of people who might not know you yet. Bring a volunteer or two who can actually talk about your work, and have a simple sign-up sheet or digital form ready for people who want to stay connected. Sometimes the best fundraising starts with just being present.

## 4\. Fall Farmers Market Fundraising Day

Partner with your local farmers market to host a dedicated [fundraising day](https://www.causevox.com/use-case-giving-days/) in the fall. This could look like a percentage of vendor sales going to your cause, a donation booth with activities for kids, or a raffle with items donated by market vendors.

The key is making it feel like a natural part of the market experience rather than an interruption. Have volunteers stationed throughout who can share your story in a genuine, conversational way. Set up a [donation page](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donation-pages-that-convert-7-must-dos/) in advance so you can promote it ahead of time and capture donations from people who visit your booth. Markets already attract community-minded people, your job is just to connect them to your mission while they’re there.

## 5\. Corn Maze Partnership with a Local Farm

Reach out to a farm near you that runs a fall corn maze and ask if they’d be willing to do a fundraiser night or weekend where a portion of ticket proceeds benefit your organization. Many farms are looking for ways to give back to the community and this kind of partnership is a natural fit.

Your role is mostly promotion, get your supporters to show up, share the event on [social media](https://www.causevox.com/blog/giving-tuesday-social-media-tips/), and make sure your organization’s name is visible at the event. You can also set up a [peer-to-peer campaign](https://www.causevox.com/peer-to-peer-fundraising/) where supporters raise additional donations from their networks before the day of the event. It’s a fun outing that practically sells itself, especially for families.

## 6\. Harvest Run or Turkey Trot

[Running events](https://www.causevox.com/use-case-runs-walks-races/) have a proven fundraising track record because they combine [peer-to-peer fundraising](https://www.causevox.com/blog/peer-to-peer-fundraising/) with something people were probably going to do anyway. A harvest-themed 5K in October or a turkey trot in November gives your supporters a reason to rally their friends and family around your cause.

Give yourself at least two to three months of lead time. You’ll need to secure a route, pull any necessary permits, line up volunteers, and get T-shirts ordered. Ask local businesses to [sponsor](https://www.causevox.com/blog/how-to-get-sponsors-for-nonprofit/) the event in exchange for logo placement on shirts and signage (this can offset a significant chunk of your costs).

Set up a [peer-to-peer campaign](https://www.causevox.com/blog/launching-a-peer-to-peer-campaign/) so each participant can share their page with their own network and raise donations leading up to race day. A virtual option where participants complete the distance on their own is worth offering too, since it opens the event up to people who can’t be there in person.

## 7\. Friendsgiving Dinner for Donors

A Friendsgiving dinner is a warm, personal way to say [thank you](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donor-recognition-ideas/) to the people who show up for your organization year after year. Think of it less as a fundraising event and more as a relationship investment that pays off over time.

Keep it intimate, around 20 to 40 people is a sweet spot. Potluck style works well and makes it feel communal rather than formal. Share a genuine update on your work over dinner, introduce donors to each other, and let the conversation happen naturally. You don’t have to make a big ask at the event itself. The goal is to make your most loyal supporters feel genuinely valued, which is what keeps them giving year after year. A [heartfelt follow-up email](https://www.causevox.com/blog/fundraising-campaign-communication-templates-for-email-social-media-and-sms-marketing/) a few days later with a giving link is more than enough.

## 8\. Board Game or Trivia Night at a Local Coffee Shop

This is a great option when you want something social and low-key that doesn’t require a lot of planning. Partner with a local coffee shop to host a board game or trivia night, charge a small entry fee, and let people show up, play, and connect.

The coffee shop benefits from the foot traffic, your supporters get a fun evening out, and your organization raises money without a ton of overhead. Set up a giving page ahead of time and share it in your event promotions so people who can’t attend can still contribute. Add a short mission moment mid-evening, nothing heavy, just a two-minute update on what your organization is working on, and you’ve turned a casual night out into a meaningful touchpoint.

## 9\. Cozy Movie Night Under the Stars

An outdoor movie night in early fall, before it gets too cold, is the kind of event people genuinely look forward to. Pick a film that connects to your mission or just something universally beloved, charge admission, and sell concessions to boost your revenue.

If outdoor isn’t an option, a rented community space works just as well, lean into the cozy factor with blankets, string lights, and warm drinks. Set up a [giving page](https://www.causevox.com/blog/complete-guide-donation-page/) and share it in your event confirmation emails so donors can give beyond the ticket price if they want to. You can also use the event as a soft launch for a [year-end campaign](https://www.causevox.com/blog/end-of-year-giving-campaign/), sharing a brief [impact video](https://www.causevox.com/blog/10-powerful-nonprofit-videos-to-inspire-you/) before the film starts and giving people a concrete ask to respond to.

## 10\. “Harvest Your Impact” Year-End Pledge Launch

Fall is the perfect time to launch a [pledge campaign,](https://www.causevox.com/blog/6-ways-to-use-pledge-now-pay-later-in-your-fundraising-campaigns/) especially as donors start thinking about [year-end giving](https://www.causevox.com/blog/transition-your-givingtuesday-campaign-into-year-end/). Pledges let supporters commit to a larger gift paid out over several months, which makes it easier for them to give generously while giving your organization more predictable revenue heading into the new year. Frame it around impact: what will these pledges make possible? Give donors a clear, specific answer.

## 11\. Local Business “Round Up for a Cause” Partnership

Reach out to local businesses, coffee shops, boutiques, restaurants, and ask if they’d be willing to run a “round up for a cause” campaign in October or November, where customers can round up their purchase to the nearest dollar and donate the difference to your organization.

It’s a low-ask for businesses because it doesn’t require them to donate out of pocket, and customers tend to opt in readily when they trust the cause.

Make sure your organization is visible in the store during the campaign with a small sign or table card explaining who you are goes a long way. Follow up with a genuine thank-you to the business and share the results publicly. It’s good for them and it introduces your mission to an entirely new audience.

## 12\. DIY Peer-to-Peer: Fall Challenge Edition

[Peer-to-peer fundraising](https://www.causevox.com/blog/peer-to-peer-fundraising-ebook/) works especially well in the fall because there are so many natural hooks to build a challenge around. Ask your supporters to commit to something for the season, like cooking one new recipe a week, taking a daily walk, or going screen-free. Have them raise donations from their networks in support.

Each participant sets up their own [fundraising page](https://www.causevox.com/blog/online-fundraising-website/) and shares it with friends and family. The campaign runs itself once people are signed up, because participants are motivated by their personal commitment. Give it a clear start and end date, share updates and highlights along the way, and celebrate your top fundraisers publicly. It’s one of the most scalable formats available because your supporters are doing the outreach for you.

## 13\. Harvest Auction

A harvest [auction](https://www.causevox.com/auction-management/) is a natural fit for fall and gives your donors something to get genuinely excited about. You can run it [live at an event or entirely online](https://www.causevox.com/blog/product-update-auctions-on-causevox/) over several days, which dramatically expands who can participate.

For items, think local and experiential, dinner with a community figure, a behind-the-scenes tour of something interesting, a cooking class with a local chef, or a basket of goods from local businesses.

Donated items keep your costs low and give vendors visibility in return. If you’re running an online [silent auction](https://www.causevox.com/blog/silent-auction-ideas/), give it a clear window (five to seven days tends to work well) and send reminder emails as the deadline approaches. A final-hours push on the last day typically drives a meaningful spike in bids.

## 14\. Autumn Paint and Sip Night

Paint and sip events are reliably popular because they require no artistic ability, feel like a night out rather than a fundraiser, and fall gives you the most beautiful subject matter to work with like golden leaves, harvest scenes, cozy cabins.

Partner with a local studio to host a fundraiser night and split proceeds, or go the DIY route with a local artist and a rented venue. Either way, set a ticket price, sell some concessions, and watch people surprise themselves with what they can create.

## 15\. No-Shave November (Or Your Own Version)

No-Shave November has been around long enough that most people already get the concept, participants skip shaving for the month and donate what they’d otherwise spend on grooming products. It’s lighthearted, it’s social, and it generates great content throughout the month.

But here’s the thing: facial hair is optional. The real magic is the challenge mechanic. Think about what your community could commit to giving up or doing for 30 days that ties back to your mission. A sugar-free month, a daily walk, a screen-free morning routine, whatever resonates.

It’s [peer-to-peer fundraising](https://www.causevox.com/blog/recruit-peer-to-peer-fundraisers-giving-day/) baked right into the concept, and it lives entirely online.

## 16\. Host a Fall Festival

[Fall festivals](https://www.causevox.com/blog/best-fall-festival-fundraising-ideas/) are community anchors. They’re warm, welcoming, and give your organization a chance to be seen and celebrated in a way that a one-night event just can’t match.

Mix in live music, hayrides, apple bobbing, pie-eating contests, face painting, and all the fall food your volunteers can manage.

[Sell activity tickets](https://www.causevox.com/blog/nonprofit-event-management/), create sponsor packages for local businesses, and set up a few QR codes around the grounds so anyone can donate right from their phone.

## 17\. Back-to-School Carnival

Early fall is a great window for a family-friendly outdoor event, and carnivals give you multiple revenue streams in one: entry fees, activity tickets, food, and merchandise. The format is familiar and easy to promote because people already know what to expect.

Set up a campaign page and sell tickets online in advance. It jumpstarts your fundraising before the event opens and gives you a headcount to plan around. Having registration and donations in one place makes [follow-up straightforward](https://www.causevox.com/blog/nonprofit-event-management/) and helps you connect with first-time attendees well after the event wrapped.

## 18\. Oktoberfest Event

Late September through early October is Oktoberfest season, and communities everywhere are already celebrating. Lean in. Brats, brews, live music, and a good cause make for a genuinely great night.

Host your own event or partner with a local brewery, restaurant, or community group already running an Oktoberfest party and negotiate a portion of proceeds going to your organization. [Sell tickets](https://www.causevox.com/blog/fundraising-tickets-how-to-plan-a-ticketed-event-for-your-nonprofit/) in advance and activate peer-to-peer fundraising to expand your reach well beyond your own list.

## 19\. GivingTuesday

No fall fundraising list is complete without [GivingTuesday](https://www.causevox.com/blog/givingtuesday-2025-campaign-planning/). But the organizations that do it well aren’t just joining the noise, they’re telling a [specific story](https://www.causevox.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-storytelling-for-nonprofits/) that makes someone want to give to them, on that day, for a clear reason.

Pick one person, one outcome, one concrete thing that donations will make possible. Start building toward it the week before rather than treating it as a one-day campaign. Secure a [matching gift](https://www.causevox.com/use-case-matching-gifts/) if you can, donors give significantly more when they know their gift will be doubled.

A personal, specific thank-you after [GivingTuesday](https://www.givingtuesday.org/) is often what turns a one-time donor into someone who [gives again](https://www.causevox.com/blog/convert-more-recurring-donors/) in December and beyond.
