From AI Ambition to Customer Experience at Scale: Why Success Still Depends on People, Platforms, and Pragmatism

Image Credit: Black Salmon/BigStockPhoto.com

While AI usage may be in its nascent stages in the telecommunications industry, we are witnessing one area with significant potential for AI transformation – the customer experience.

As the market becomes more saturated, customer expectations have grown exponentially. To scale, operators are deploying advanced, AI-driven use cases ranging from customer care (e.g. contact center and support), customer acquisition and marketing, and monetization and offer management.

However, the path to fruition is not necessarily linear. According to recent research commissioned from Analysys Mason, current AI and automation deployments are delivering inconsistent or limited results. More than half of operators surveyed (60%) are experiencing Proof of Concept failures at a rate of 80%, with only a small subset (3%) reporting a success rate greater than 40%.

So how can operators succeed with AI and achieve a stronger customer experience at scale?

## Human skills are still critical to AI success

However ironic it may seem, the data revealed that the success of AI implementations is hugely reliant on people skills including proactive and effective communication, and clearly defined and measurable KPIs identified and tracked from day one.

The main success factor defined by the research was ‘proactive communications and training’ followed by ‘strong executive and business support.’ Equally important to strong executive support, however, was the use of a core application with built-in AI capabilities that simplified deployment.

## The multivendor dilemma: innovation vs. complexity

Beyond people and process, operators must also navigate increasingly complex technology decisions that can either accelerate or hinder AI success. While there is growing interest in simplified, single-vendor models, most operators continue to favor multivendor strategies to access best-of-breed innovation.

In theory, this approach enables flexibility and faster innovation. In practice, it often introduces significant integration challenges. Disparate systems, inconsistent data models, and competing vendor roadmaps can slow progress and make it difficult to scale AI across the customer lifecycle. Nearly two-thirds of operators report higher development and operational costs as a result, and 93% agree that managing multiple vendors increases total cost of ownership.

This creates a clear tension. Operators want the flexibility to innovate, but also need the simplicity and cohesion required to scale.

## Why hybrid is emerging as the practical path forward

As a result, many operators are moving toward a more pragmatic, hybrid approach. Rather than choosing between build or buy, they are combining in-house development with strategic vendor partnerships. This allows them to retain control over critical capabilities while accelerating time to value and reducing risk.

This shift also reflects a broader evolution in how AI is being viewed. It is no longer a standalone initiative, but part of a larger ecosystem that must integrate across applications, data, and infrastructure. Success increasingly depends on how well these elements work together.

## Data and deployment realities cannot be ignored

At the same time, foundational challenges remain. Data fragmentation continues to limit the effectiveness of AI, with many operators struggling to unify customer, product, and operational data. Without this foundation, even the most advanced AI models will struggle to deliver meaningful outcomes.

Deployment strategy is also playing a critical role. The vast majority of operators are turning to the cloud, with 86% primarily deploying AI and automation solutions in public cloud environments, reflecting the need for scalability and flexibility. Meanwhile, IT organizations are leading the charge, acting as the primary driver of customer lifecycle automation initiatives in 90% of cases.

## From ambition to execution

There is no question that AI is seen as essential to the future of the industry. Nearly all operators agree that AI-powered automation will be critical for survival and growth over the next five years.

Yet despite this near-universal agreement, many organizations are still struggling to translate ambition into measurable results. The gap between proof of concept and production remains one of the biggest barriers to realizing value.

Closing this gap requires more than technology. It demands a shift in mindset, from isolated experimentation to end-to-end transformation. Operators must align business and IT objectives, embed AI into core processes, and prioritize scalable architectures from the outset.

## Turning AI into a competitive advantage

Ultimately, the operators that succeed will be those that recognize AI is not just a tool, but a transformation of how customer experience is delivered. That means investing not only in technology, but also in people, data, and organizational readiness.

The opportunity is clear. While the journey may not be linear, those who take a pragmatic, integrated, and human-centric approach will be best positioned to turn AI ambition into real business impact at scale.
