A student asked me a great question: in the age of AI, is a script or template even valuable anymore? Can't you just ask AI to generate it?  
  
I'm always going to argue: yes, information is still valuable. But the value shifted. Here's how to think about it.  
  
When someone says "AI can do that," they're making three assumptions. Assumption 1: everyone knows how to use AI. (Only about 1% of the world is actually using AI in 2026.) Assumption 2: even if they know AI, they know what to ask for. (AI is a blank chat window — it only gives you what you know to ask.) Assumption 3: even if they know what to ask, they know how to troubleshoot when things go wrong. (They don't have your trial and error.) Most of the "AI killed this" objections collapse the moment you unpack them.  
  
Here's where information value actually sits now. The information itself is becoming commoditized — yes, everything you write about can be found elsewhere. That's not what someone subscribes for. What they pay for (in time or money) is two things: time saved, because you organized the information so they don't have to scour the internet. And certainty, because they trust you to tell them what's right, what's wrong, and what to focus on.  
  
But the bigger shift is the asset layer. The most valuable products now pair information with an asset that increases the likelihood of success. Take "how to write a book." All that info is free. But if I give you a Claude skill that outlines, drafts, and proofreads your book, the asset isn't just a giveaway — it's the thing that makes you actually finish the book.  
  
The trap most people fall into is dropping "viral Claude skills" or AI prompt bundles on X/LinkedIn without the information layer. The asset alone isn't valuable. What makes an asset valuable is when it's paired with information explaining what to do, AND the asset increases the likelihood that you'll do it successfully.  
  
If you're worried about whether information is still valuable in the age of AI, this is the answer: organization + certainty + asset. The information is the map. The asset is the vehicle. Together, they're worth paying for.
