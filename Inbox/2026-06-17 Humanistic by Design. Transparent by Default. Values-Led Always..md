### TEAMS! Newsletter | AI & Technology Edition | June 17, 2026 | A Five-Day Series Celebrating 150 Editions of TEAMS!

### The Fastest-Moving Force in the Room.

We've spent two days on the human side of the TEAMS! framework, who you choose, and how you treat them once they're there. Today, we turn to the force reshaping every assumption underlying both of those questions.

Of all the macro forces pressing in on organizations right now, none is moving faster or demanding more thoughtful leadership than Artificial Intelligence. And I want to be direct about something: our response to this moment cannot merely be operational. It must be profoundly humanistic and values-based. Because the organizations that get this wrong won't just adopt bad tools. They'll quietly drift from who they say they are.

### Technology is Not Neutral

Let's start with the premise most technology conversations skip. Technology is never neutral. Every system carries the values and blind spots of the people who built, funded, regulated, and deployed it. AI is not an exception to that rule. It is the sharpest example of it.

Which means that introducing AI into a mission-driven environment isn't a procurement decision. It's a values decision. And it requires the same rigor, transparency, and ethical scrutiny we bring to any other decision that affects the people we serve.

> ***No computational system, however sophisticated, can create a heart that gives itself, or a conscience that discerns good from evil.*** **— Pope Leo XIV, Magnifica Humanitas**

That quote stopped me when I first read it. Because it names exactly the line that mission-driven leaders need to hold. Our core asset, at AbilityPath and in any relationship-centered organization, is human connection. The sacred interaction between a clinician and an individual. Between a support professional and a family. AI can be a powerful tool in the service of that connection. It cannot be a substitute for it.

### What AI Should and Shouldn't Do

The framing I come back to is this: AI should alleviate administrative burdens and free up human energy for deeper connection. Documentation. Scheduling. Data synthesis. Compliance tracking. These are areas where AI can genuinely give people back time to do the work that matters most.

What AI must never do is mediate or cold-render the care we provide. The moment a vulnerable person's experience is reduced to an algorithm's output, without a human in the loop who is accountable, present, and thinking critically, we've crossed a line that an efficiency gain can't justify.

### Our Standard at AbilityPath

> ***We don't adopt technology merely because it is fast. We shape it to be fair, transparent, and aligned with our purpose. If a tool can't pass the human dignity test, it doesn't come through the door.***

We must also be vigilant about what I call the *Human-in-the-Loop paradox*: the risk that humans become rubber stamps for AI outputs they don't fully understand, or don't have time to interrogate. A human signature on an AI recommendation isn't the same as human judgment. The governance frameworks we build must honestly account for that gap.

### Our Rule of Three for Technology Governance

To anchor this work responsibly, our board and executive team actively manage AI adoption through three governing principles. These aren't aspirational statements. They're operational filters that every new tool has to pass before it touches our staff, our clients, or our data.

- **Human Dignity:** The primary filter for any new software or AI tool adoption. If it compromises dignity, it doesn't move forward.
- **Algorithmic Transparency:** Our data architecture and vendor relationships must be understandable. We won't deploy black boxes against vulnerable populations.
- **Values-Based Governance:** Our tools are held to the same ethical standards we demand from our people. No exception for technology.

These three filters are embodied in our AI Governance Working Group, an oversight structure that ensures nothing touches our clients, our data, or our staff workflows without human review. It's not a committee that slows things down. It's a mechanism that makes sure we're still ourselves when we come out the other side of adoption.

### Culture Projects, Not Technology Projects

Here's the reframe I keep coming back to: our 15-day AI literacy curriculum, our board AI education sessions, our LLM platform evaluations for HIPAA and BAA compliance; none of these are technology projects. They are cultural projects. Deliberate investments in making sure that every person in our organization can engage with these tools confidently, critically, and with a clear ethical compass.

An organization where only the IT department understands how AI is used has already abdicated its governance responsibility. Literacy has to be broad. Judgment has to be distributed. The people closest to the work need to be able to ask good questions about the tools that touch their workflows and feel safe doing so.

> ***The greatest risk in this era is not that AI will do too much. It is that leaders will abdicate their responsibility to think critically about what it does.***

### First Principle, Not Analogy

Managing by analogy, doing what worked for another organization in a previous era, copying a strategy because it looked good in a conference presentation, is no longer sufficient. The pace of change in AI is fast enough that yesterday's playbook may already be wrong.

*First Principles Thinking* demands that we break every complex challenge down to its most foundational truths and build our solutions upward from there. That applies to AI adoption as much as to program design or financial modeling. Don't ask what other organizations are doing with AI. Ask what your mission requires, what your values permit, and what your people need in order to engage with these tools with dignity and confidence. Then build from there.

That's the standard we're holding ourselves to. And at 150 editions of TEAMS!, and it's a standard I believe more in now than when I wrote the first one last year.

### For Reflection

> ***Where in your workflow is AI already operating, seen or unseen? Is it transparent to the people it affects? Does it pass the human dignity test? And who in your organization is empowered to ask those questions out loud?***
