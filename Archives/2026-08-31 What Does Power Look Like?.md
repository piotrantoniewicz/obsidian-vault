What does power look like? Sometimes, we don’t even recognize it.

During the organizing education program with **[Sustainable Georgia Futures](https://sustainablegafutures.org/)**, we did a role play. Half the participants played members of an environmental justice organization; the other half played members of the utility regulatory commission. In this fictional scenario (ripped from the headlines), leaders and activists have requested this meeting to convince the commissioners to reject a rate increase request from the investor-owned utilities.

One of the women who played a commissioner turned in an Oscar-worthy performance. She gave a perfect display of power before anyone even knew it.

## Data Doesn’t Win

Members of the environmental justice organization came to the meeting with stories of hardships because of rate increases. They presented evidence of inequity, showing the lop-sided burden that low-income neighborhoods and communities of color face compared to middle- and high-income and white neighborhoods. All the facts are on their side.

What did the Commission do?

Nothing.

They smiled. They thanked the activists for coming. They put on a semblance of paying attention. The Oscar winner greeted everyone individually and shook their hands with a beaming smile. She even asked some surface-level questions.

## “Don’t Worry Your Pretty Little Head.”

The EJ organization members knew exactly what was happening. They got what I like to call the “Don’t worry your pretty little head” treatment. It’s like they were being pat on the head and told to let the grown ups talk.

That’s what power looks like.

We are so accustomed to seeing power wielded in obvious ways - police violence, hateful executive orders, regressive laws, coercion, toxic workplaces - that we don’t always recognize that those in positions of power wield it by simply doing nothing. Because they can.

## Equal and Opposite Power

The equal and opposite force is when we build power. When social justice leaders build power, we can make the head-patters take action. We make it uncomfortable or untenable to do nothing.

When leaders and activists build the power of organized people, they can fight on equal terms with those in positions of power.

That’s also what power looks like.

When have you seen community members build power?

## What to Watch: “Deaf President NOW!”

“ [Deaf President NOW](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt34965967) ” tells the story of [Gallaudet University](https://gallaudet.edu/) students who built power to demand a deaf university president. (Gallaudet is the US’s only university for the deaf.)

A promotional image from the film "Deaf President NOW." A white man seen from the back, with one fist raised, pointing at the US Capitol building in front of him. There are protestors and signs filling the area in front of the Capitol.

In 1988, the board of the university appointed a new hearing president. And this one couldn’t even sign! After 124 years of hearing presidents, students had enough.

This movie is different from the other movement films you’ve seen. Some of the narration is signed with subtitles. Some of the protest scenes are silent, to invoke the scene participants may have experienced.

I love this organizing story because it has so many elements of a strategic campaign. Students (and faculty) came together to build power. They built a strategy. They chose focus people that had the power to make the decision they wanted. They had consensus on clear and compelling demands. Their message was about values and emotion.

## More than Winning

No spoilers, but they won more than a deaf president.

If you’re looking for some organizing inspiration before the election cycle gets deep, this is the movie you need right now.
