## Why Fall Is the Perfect Season for Fundraising

Fall gives nonprofits a built-in calendar of traditions people already look forward to: back-to-school routines, Halloween, and Thanksgiving. A fundraiser that taps into one of those traditions doesn’t have to compete for attention. Instead, it creates an opportunity for something people already want to do.

Fall is also a strategic runway into the biggest fundraising season of the year. The relationships you build and the donor data you collect this season set the stage for year-end giving. A supporter who shows up for your harvest festival in October is a warm prospect for your year-end appeal in December, especially if you follow up with intention.

## 10 Fall Fundraiser Ideas for Nonprofits

### 1\. Fall Harvest Festival

**Why it works:** A fall harvest festival is a classic fundraiser idea that naturally becomes a community tradition. It combines multiple revenue streams — food, games, rides, vendor booths, and even a corn maze or hayride if you have the space — into a single event.

**Best for:** Organizations with the staff and volunteer capacity to coordinate a large, multi-activity, and possibly multi-day event.

**Tips for success:** Recruit local vendors and businesses as sponsors to offset costs, and include reminders of your mission throughout so attendees understand where their support is going.

### 2\. Fall Scavenger Hunt

**Why it works:** Picture teams racing around town snapping photos at the local coffee shop, hunting for a specific leaf color at the park, or tracking down a clue hidden in the town square. A scavenger hunt invites your whole community to get involved — families, groups of friends, and corporate groups can all take part in the fun.

**Best for:** Organizations with a defined local footprint and a story that connects to specific places or landmarks.

**Tips for success:** Charge a team registration fee, and weave your mission into the locations and clues.

### 3\. Pumpkin Carving or Decorating Contest

**Why it works:** Pumpkin carving is a low-cost, high-participation activity that people are already planning to do this time of year, so you’re simply giving them an opportunity to do it for a cause.

**Best for:** Family-focused organizations and community groups looking for an accessible entry point.

**Tips for success:** Charge an entry fee per pumpkin, recruit local judges, and use social media voting to extend engagement beyond event day.

### 4\. Fall Bonfire Concert

**Why it works:** What’s cozier than a fall bonfire? An outdoor autumn concert is a warm, inviting event that brings together people of all ages. Local music acts can promote your event to their own audiences, while vendors, food trucks, and merchandise can add extra revenue streams. You could even make it a fall concert series with a variety of musical styles to attract different crowds.

**Best for:** Organizations with access to an outdoor space and connections to local musicians or bands.

**Tips for success:** Smaller teams can simplify setup and equipment needs by sticking to acoustic acts. Whatever the size of your bonfire concert, be sure to get the proper permits well in advance.

### 5\. Walk-a-Thon or Fun Run

**Why it works:** Walk-a-thons and fun runs are naturally suited to [peer-to-peer (P2P) fundraising](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/maximizing-peer-to-peer-fundraising/), since participants can rally their own networks of friends and family to sponsor their miles. Fall also comes with a built-in occasion for this kind of event: The Thanksgiving Turkey Trot is a beloved tradition for many families.

**Best for:** Organizations of any size looking for a scalable, repeatable event.

**Tips for success:** Engaging Networks makes it easy to set up individual and team fundraising pages, complete with leaderboards to spark friendly competition.

### 6\. Pumpkin Patch or Orchard Partnership

**Why it works:** The community will already be flocking to local pumpkin patches or apple orchards, so partnering with those organizations is an easy way to raise funds without building an event from scratch.

**Best for:** Smaller organizations or those new to building community partnerships.

**Tips for success:** Negotiate a donation table or percentage-of-sales agreement, and bring volunteers to engage visitors directly instead of relying on signage alone.

### 7\. Costume Contest or Parade

**Why it works:** A costume contest or parade gives people a chance to show off their creativity, which drives organic sharing and word-of-mouth promotion well beyond your usual donor base. You can charge a small entry fee or partner with local sponsors.

**Best for:** Organizations with a strong local or neighborhood presence, including schools and community centers.

**Tips for success:** Offer categories for different age groups, and consider adding a pet costume category to widen participation.

### 8\. Pie Bake-Off Fundraiser

**Why it works:** A fall pie bake-off is inexpensive to run and taps into the season’s comfort food traditions, giving supporters a chance to show off a family recipe while contributing to your cause. Judges with local name recognition can promote your event to their own followers, while the audience can pay for tasting tickets to elect a “People’s Choice” winner.

**Best for:** Smaller or volunteer-led organizations looking for a budget-friendly event.

**Tips for success:** Give your participants a social media or email template so they can easily share your event (and your cause) with their networks.

### 9\. Fall Raffle or Silent Auction

**Why it works:** The fall season offers plenty of opportunities for themed raffles or silent auctions. Prizes and auction items could include gift cards for a local ghost tour, a basket of seasonal treats, a weekend stay at a log cabin retreat, or local artwork featuring fall scenery. Since prizes are typically donated, this is a low-cost fundraiser that even the smallest teams can pull off.

**Best for:** Organizations of any size with access to local business donations, board member networks, or engaged supporters.

**Tips for success:** Pair the raffle or auction with an existing fall event to boost participation, and consider offering both in-person and online bidding to reach supporters who can’t attend.

### 10\. Fall Photo Contest

**Why it works:** A photo contest generates social media content and word-of-mouth driven by your supporters, with minimal work from your team. It gives the community two ways to engage with your organization: sharing their own photos, and voting for the winner.

**Best for:** Organizations looking for a low-lift, digital-first fundraiser.

**Tips for success:** A fall photo contest can be especially helpful if you need to boost your social media engagement prior to Giving Tuesday and year-end fundraising. New audiences will be visiting your social media profiles, so make sure your mission and donation link are prominently featured.

## How to Choose the Best Fall Fundraiser

The right fall fundraiser depends on your organization’s capacity, audience, and goals. A small team with limited volunteer support might do better with a low-lift option like a pie bake-off or digital photo contest, while an organization with strong community ties and more staff bandwidth might be ready to put together a multi-day harvest festival or bonfire concert series.

It’s also worth considering your audience. Family-oriented events like a costume parade or pumpkin carving contest tend to draw people of all ages, while team-based events like a walk-a-thon or scavenger hunt can activate your most engaged supporters through peer-to-peer fundraising.

## Tips for a Successful Fall Fundraiser

- **Start planning early.** Fall calendars fill up quickly with community events, school activities, and busy holiday weekends, so lock in your date and venue well in advance.
- **Plan for the weather.** Outdoor fall events can mean anything from crisp sunshine to freezing rain, so have a contingency plan ready in case of bad weather.
- **Build in mission moments.** Whether it’s through a story shared on stage, signage throughout the event, or branded merchandise, make sure attendees understand the impact of their participation.
- **Promote across multiple channels.** Email, social media, [text reminders](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/5-tips-for-nonprofit-sms-campaign-success/), community flyers, and local press each reach supporters differently, so use them together rather than relying on just one.
- **Follow up with supporters.** The fall season leads straight into Giving Tuesday and the year-end giving push, which can account for a significant portion of an organization’s annual revenue. Keep your supporters engaged by following up donations with a warm, [personalized thank-you](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/the-power-of-thanks-and-gratitude/) and a story about their impact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Some of the best fall fundraiser ideas include harvest festivals, walk-a-thons, pumpkin carving contests, costume contests or parades, and bonfire concert fundraisers. The best choice depends on your organization’s size, audience, and available volunteer support.

Easy fall fundraising ideas include a pie bake-off, a fall photo contest, a seasonal raffle or silent auction, and partnering with a local pumpkin patch or apple orchard to collect donations. These options can raise meaningful revenue even with a small budget or small team.

To organize a fall festival fundraiser, you’ll need to:

- Secure a **venue** and **date**
- Obtain any required **permits** and **insurance**
- **Recruit** vendors, entertainment, sponsors, and volunteers
- **Promote** the event across [email](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/solutions/nonprofit-email-marketing/), social media, and local partners well in advance to build attendance
- Give vendors, entertainers, and sponsors a **social media template** so they can easily promote your fall festival fundraiser to their own audiences
- Create a list of **all required materials**. If vendors need to bring their own materials, make sure they know the requirements well in advance
- Create a detailed **day-of planning document** and schedule so everyone knows exactly what they need to do and when
- Use event management tools to **handle ticketing** and **check-in**
- Build in **multiple fundraising opportunities**, including entry tickets, a percentage of food and vendor sales, signage around the event, and at least one appeal from the stage

Most nonprofits should begin planning a fall fundraiser at least three to four months in advance, especially for larger events like a harvest festival or walk-a-thon that require venue bookings, vendor coordination, and volunteer recruitment.

Even smaller, more budget-friendly fall fundraisers may need time to coordinate park permits, local sponsors, and donated prizes.

A successful fall fundraiser combines the right event format for your audience, early and thorough planning, clear volunteer roles, and consistent promotion across multiple channels.

In addition to raising funds, “success” might mean high community participation, growing your audience, or connecting supporters to your mission and impact in a memorable way.
