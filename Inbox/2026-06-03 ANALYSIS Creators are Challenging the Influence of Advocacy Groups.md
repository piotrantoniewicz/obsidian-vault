Last year marked a dramatic shift: For the first time in history, social media became the most popular mode of news consumption in America. According to a [Reuters Institute analysis](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary), social media overtook both TV news and online news (like news websites and apps) as the most common medium people use to get their news.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image8.png)

Unfortunately, advocacy groups are currently just a blip on social media, with their perspectives on public policy getting crowded out by social media content creators who produce videos, graphics, and written content for social platforms like Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, X, Bluesky, and Substack.  
  
M+R analyzed [Magnitude Media’s dataset](https://dashboards.cluvio.com/dashboards/qxny-9e5q-k65v/shared?filters=%7B%22platform_filter%22%3A%5B%22Bluesky%22%2C%22Facebook%22%2C%22Instagram%22%2C%22Rumble%22%2C%22Threads%22%2C%22TikTok%22%2C%22Truth%20Social%22%2C%22Twitter%22%2C%22YouTube%22%5D%2C%22political_content%22%3A%5B%22political%22%5D%2C%22political_lean%22%3A%5B%22Neutral%22%2C%22Left-Leaning%22%5D%7D&sharingToken=78eb1196-5766-429d-acf8-edcfd96b7067&timerange=1767225600~1775001599) of the top 500 social media accounts covering politics and policy during the first quarter of 2026 and found just 17 advocacy groups\* made the list, 3% of all accounts. Meanwhile, 270 creator accounts made up more than half the list of the top 500 most influential accounts. We defined creator accounts as including both influencer-style accounts where individuals talk directly to camera (think [Aaron Parnas](https://www.tiktok.com/@aaronparnas1?lang=en)) as well as graphic or text-based accounts (think [The Other 98%](https://www.facebook.com/TheOther98/)).

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image10.png)

*Methodology: We used* [*Magnitude Media’s dataset*](https://dashboards.cluvio.com/dashboards/qxny-9e5q-k65v/shared?filters=%7B%22platform_filter%22%3A%5B%22Bluesky%22%2C%22Facebook%22%2C%22Instagram%22%2C%22Rumble%22%2C%22Threads%22%2C%22TikTok%22%2C%22Truth%20Social%22%2C%22Twitter%22%2C%22YouTube%22%5D%2C%22political_content%22%3A%5B%22political%22%5D%2C%22political_lean%22%3A%5B%22Neutral%22%2C%22Left-Leaning%22%5D%7D&sharingToken=78eb1196-5766-429d-acf8-edcfd96b7067&timerange=1767225600~1775001599) *to analyze the 500 top social media accounts covering politics and policy from a neutral or progressive lens ranked by combined number of engagements across 9 platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Threads, YouTube, TikTok, X, Bluesky, Truth Social, and Rumble) during the first quarter of 2026. Creator Accounts are run by an individual or a few people who post news and analysis directly on social media. Media Outlet Accounts are run by news organizations, ranging from legacy media like the* New York Times *to newer online outlets like* Zeteo*. Politician/Campaign Accounts are run by candidates, elected officials, and political parties. Advocacy Group Accounts are run by organizations like the ACLU, Human Rights Campaign, and the National Organization for Women (NOW).*

In the last five years, the advent of free or low-cost video production and graphic design software available on everyone’s smartphone has allowed individuals to provide instant analysis and content in formats that once only well-resourced institutions could, creating increased competition to both media outlets and advocacy groups trying to shape the public discourse.

Just as it’s become easier for individuals to compete with big institutions at creating content, it’s also become easier for individuals to compete for audiences. Social media algorithms have shifted away from serving people content from the accounts they follow and instead toward content that is most likely to keep you on the platform the longest.

People in the industry [describe this trend](https://garyvee.substack.com/p/social-media-is-dead-interest-media) as the demise of “social media”and the rise of “interest media.”Media outlets and advocacy organizations that built up significant social media followings in prior years no longer have the same level of access to them as they did in the past.

As creators gain a larger share of attention on social media, they are also chipping away at advocacy groups’ market share in traditional news media coverage. Media outlets are increasingly featuring creators because they are popular thought leaders who bring along engaged audiences that media outlets also desperately need.

But creators don’t only present a communications threat to advocacy groups who have spent decades trying to shape public policy and build their supporter base. Changes in technology have not only allowed individual creators to **reach** millions of people on social media, they are also empowering them to **organize** millions of people. Creators are generating political engagement from social media in ways only organizations with lots of staff and resources could do previously.

Ricky Longoria, a 24-year-old creator known as [The Gay Liberal,](https://www.instagram.com/thegayliberal/) used [Sway to get out the vote](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DYqMch2JNcr/) in the recent Texas primary and got dozens of voters to the polls. Top campaign finance influencer [Nico Agosta](https://www.instagram.com/stocking_the_capitol/?hl=en) launched a new advocacy organization, the [Political Integrity Project](https://www.instagram.com/politicalintegrity.us/), with an Instagram [post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DR27CcHDm3B/) garnering 1.4 million views and a wave of fundraising. Creator Tommy Marcus raised $30 million for advocacy causes between his 25th and 30th birthdays from the [meme account](https://www.instagram.com/quentin.quarantino/?hl=en) he started while in quarantine in 2020.

While political candidates and elected officials historically participated in forums held by advocacy groups in order to reach the highly engaged grassroots base, they can now choose to bypass organizations and still reach these activists by appearing on creators’ [podcasts and social media](https://www.mrss.com/lab/how-to-engage-podcasts-8-lessons-advocates-can-take-from-zohran-mamdanis-podcast-blitz/). Creators are increasingly providing [voter guides](https://www.instagram.com/p/DY8mbKMN6sV/?hl=en), which have been the purview of advocacy groups for years.

### So what should groups do? How can they stay relevant?

The first solution is obvious:Creators should be a key piece in every advocacy group’s communications strategy. For creators who act more like media outlets, this means you should prioritize them in media relations outreach, offering exclusives and priority access to newsworthy interviews and information. For creators who act more like advocates and spokespeople, this means paying them for access to the audience they’ve spent years building. M+R partners with [influencers](https://www.mrss.com/what-we-do/issues-and-services/?service=influencers) to help nonprofits combine powerful content with targeted digital advertising.

But advocacy groups shouldn’t be completely dependent on creators to reach social media audiences at scale. They still need to be prominent players in the online discourse themselves to remain key players in the democratic debate. To do so, nonprofits should embrace the social media playbook that creators are using.

### Here are four creator strategies advocacy groups should emulate:

**1\. Shape public opinion before it forms (before it’s too late).**

Breaking news draws the attention of massive audiences, but there is a limited amount of time to capitalize on interest and shape opinion. Provide perspective on these stories within minutes or hours, since attention will move on to other stories.

**Example: Aaron Parnas**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/aaron.parnas), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/aaronparnas), [Threads](https://www.threads.net/@aaronparnas), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@aaronparnas1), [TikTok](https://tiktok.com/@aaronparnas1), [Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/aaronparnas.bsky.social), [X](https://twitter.com/AaronParnas)

Creator Aaron Parnas generated 209 million engagements on his content across social platforms in the first three months of 2026, second only to the legacy media outlet the BBC in our analysis above.

Parnas provides nearly instantaneous analysis of breaking news with a progressive perspective. As the below screenshot shows, he consistently posts simple direct-to-camera videos of himself presenting a news story under a breaking news headline.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image4.png)

**2\. Prioritize creating the sharpest and fastest argument over perfect aesthetics.**

Creating a sharp, memorable argument in a timely fashion is the most important aspect of your content. That might involve a smart comparison, an interesting historical reference, or a compelling use of data. Because quick, critical thinking is the driving force behind whether content is widely shared, using basic production techniques makes it easier to post sharp analysis at speed.

**Example: Feminist News**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/feministnews.us), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/feminist_news_now), [Threads](https://www.threads.net/@feminist_news_now)

Feminist News drove 23 million engagements from January through March and was the 56th overall account across platforms in our analysis, running just behind *The Washington Post* and just ahead of NBC New York. The account is run by Lacey Dickinson, who pairs poignant perspectives on breaking news with basic graphics and curates high-performing tweets coupled with her own analysis. As Lacey [says](https://www.instagram.com/ole_lace/), she makes “the memes your grandmas and aunties share on Facebook.”

Below are examples of Feminist News’ sharp and timely commentary on issues like student debt and public spending:

**3\. Collaborate with others so your content reaches larger audiences.**

When it comes to reaching mass audiences and driving public opinion, your distribution strategy matters just as much as your content. “Publish and pray” is not a great strategy, especially when opponents are utilizing all sorts of unsavory tactics (like bot farms) to juice their performance. This means you should approach social media like a team sport and partner with other accounts to share your sharpest content with their audiences. Collective action is critical on social media.

**Example: Saadia Mirza**  
[Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/Saadia___m), [Threads](https://www.threads.net/@saadia___m), [TikTok](https://tiktok.com/@saadia___m)

Saadia Mirza drove 6.6 million engagements from January through March and was the 176th overall account in our analysis, just behind *The Wall Street Journal* and just ahead of CBS New York.

When Saadia [addressed](https://www.instagram.com/p/DYlSo3cMOE5/) Mark Cuban’s support of TrumpRx, she didn’t go it alone. She joined forces with fellow creators 92% News, Quentin Quarantino, Alexis Rose RN, Josh Greene, and Jordan Uhl to get the word out, generating 226k views with the collaborative effort. Saadia’s collaborations aren’t just a one-way-street: she also looks to uplift strong content from others, like this [video](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DY5ZyHAyAmT/) by Uhl about the Los Angeles mayoral race that was also shared by Dear White Staffers, Good Morning, Bad News, and Josh Greene, garnering 168k views.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Screenshot-2026-06-03-at-3.08.22-PM.png)

**4\. Don’t sleep on YouTube.**

YouTube is the [most popular social media platform](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/) and the second-most popular search engine *and* second-most visited website after Google. YouTube is now also one of the [most common sources](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/) for answer engines like Chat GPT, Gemini, and Claude. Creators use YouTube with great success, but according to [M+R’s 2025 Benchmark study](https://mrbenchmarks.com/), 26% of nonprofits don’t even have a YouTube account.

**Example: Carlos Eduardo Espina**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/CarlosEduardoEspina1998), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/Carlos_Eduardo_Espina), [Threads](https://www.threads.net/@carlos_eduardo_espina), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@CarlosEduardoEspina), [TikTok](https://tiktok.com/@carlos_eduardo_espina), [X](https://twitter.com/cespina1998)

Carlos Eduardo Espina drove 150 million engagements in the first three months of 2026, coming in as the sixth account overall in our analysis, just behind CBS News and just ahead of NBC News. He is driving those massive numbers in part from his [YouTube page](https://www.youtube.com/@CarlosEduardoEspina), where he has posted over 6,900 videos and amassed over half a million followers. He is engaging heavily in YouTube shorts, which allows people to post the vertical video content they are making for other social platforms on YouTube, too.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image7.png)

### Organizations Can Emulate Creators

You might think it would be impossible for your organization to operate like these creators: Staff time is spent in back-to-back meetings, sharp public-facing content is then word-smithed by several internal teams to ensure organizational alignment, partnering with others makes already lengthy approval processes even slower, and producing video optimized for YouTube takes too much time.

***But you can, and you must.*** Because if you are not shaping online discourse, it is becoming harder and harder to shape public opinion and political outcomes. Here are a few examples of advocacy organizations among the 500 accounts in our analysis that are applying the above strategies.

**Act Fast: The Sunrise Movement**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/sunrisemvmt), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/sunrisemvmt), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@sunrisemovement4046), [TikTok](https://tiktok.com/@sunrisemvmt), [Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/sunrisemvmt.bsky.social), [X](https://www.twitter.com/sunrisemvmt)

The Sunrise Movement drove 1.5 million engagements across their social media in the first quarter of 2026, coming in at 471st in our analysis, just behind CBS Phoenix and just ahead of Nancy Pelosi. Executive Director and Minneapolis resident Aru Shiney-Ajay posted her [raw first reaction](https://www.tiktok.com/@sunrisemvmt/video/7598968303578615070?_r=1&_t=ZP-96ndixDBYCM) to the shooting of Alex Pretti on TikTok, which resulted in a powerful, widely viewed video that immediately galvanized people’s anger into community action

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image2.png)

**Prioritize Sharp, Timely Ideas Over Aesthetics: Stand Up America**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/StandUpAmerica), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/standup_america), [Threads](https://www.threads.net/@standup_america), [YouTube](https://youtube.com/@standupamerica6524), [TikTok](https://tiktok.com/@standup.america), [Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/standupamerica.bsky.social), [X](https://www.twitter.com/StandUpAmerica)

Stand Up America drove 4.4 million engagements in the first three months of 2026, coming in at 257th in our analysis, just behind *Mother Jones* and just ahead of *The Financial Times.* This [simple te](https://www.facebook.com/StandUpAmerica/posts/pfbid02964x23yf1b6nPZDPUuHuUuF7LcXM49greJV9RG5H7fyRguSeHecWwrk2yiFgn8jUl) [xt](https://www.facebook.com/StandUpAmerica/posts/pfbid02964x23yf1b6nPZDPUuHuUuF7LcXM49greJV9RG5H7fyRguSeHecWwrk2yiFgn8jUl) box that generated 14k likes shows how they prioritize ideas over aesthetics.

**Collaborate with Others: The NAACP**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/naacp), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/naacp/), [Threads](https://www.threads.com/@naacp), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@NAACP), [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@thenaacp), [Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/naacp.bsky.social), [X](https://x.com/naacp)

TheNAACPdrove 2.6 million engagements from January to March, coming in at 357th in our analysis, just behind Yahoo News and just ahead of CBS Philadelphia. They came together to collaborate with Kristen Clarke, Meidas Touch, The Tennessee Holler, and Dear White Staffers [to announce](https://www.instagram.com/p/DYS5vZdDMY0/?hl=en) they were filing a lawsuit challenging Tennessee’s new congressional map, generating 19.7k likes and showing the importance of taking the extra time to partner with allies.

**Don’t Sleep on YouTube: MoveOn**  
[Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/moveon), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/moveon), [Threads](https://www.threads.net/@moveon), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@MoveOnVideo), [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@moveonaction), [Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/moveon.org), [X](https://www.twitter.com/moveon)

MoveOn drove 2.8 million engagements in Q1, coming in 339th in our analysis, just behind CBS LA and just ahead of the Senate Judiciary Committee. MoveOn is crushing it on [YouTube shorts](https://www.youtube.com/@MoveOnVideo/shorts) by identifying break-out moments in Congressional hearings and posting using best practices on the platform around thumbnails, titles, and descriptions. These videos take very little production time but generate millions of views.

![](https://www.mrss.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/image9.png)

These examples show how organizations can successfully emulate the social media strategies used by creators to have an outsized impact on the online conversation. In an era where people are shifting allegiances away from institutions and toward individuals, it’s important for advocacy groups to adapt the successful strategies of creators to maintain their relevance.
