I've spent years inside nonprofit fundraising, and the year-end appeal is where I watch the most money get left on the table. Here is the AI workflow I would run in July to fix it before November.

Most nonprofits I know write one year-end letter. Then they send it to everybody on the list, from the person who gave $25 for the first time in October to the donor who has given $5,000 every December for nine years running.

Development directors know better. Of course they do. But segmenting the list by hand, then writing a different version of the appeal for each group, is a week of work, and most shops are running on one or two people who are already underwater in November. So the single letter goes out, and it speaks to no one in particular.

This is the year that excuse runs out. The sorting and the drafting, the two jobs that made real segmentation feel impossible, are now something you can do in an afternoon with an AI tool you probably already pay for. Here is how I would do it, starting now in July, so the whole thing is calm by the time year-end arrives.

**Step 1: Pull your list with real giving history.**

Export your donor list from your CRM with the last two to three years of giving attached. You want name, total given per year, first gift date, last gift date, and gift frequency. Do not overthink the fields. If you can get who gave, how much, and when, you have enough. Strip out anything sensitive you do not need for this, and keep the file on your own machine.

**Step 2: Let AI sort donors into groups.**

Hand that file to your AI tool and ask it to group donors by giving behavior, not by gift size alone. You are looking for four or five buckets: first-time givers from the past year, lapsed donors who used to give and stopped, steady recurring supporters, mid-level donors who look ready to move up, and your major donors. The AI does in two minutes what would take you an afternoon of sorting and filtering. You review the groups and adjust the edges, because you know your people better than any tool does.

**Step 3: Name the one thing each group needs to hear.**

This is the strategy part, and it is yours, not the machine's. A first-time giver needs a warm welcome and a reason to give again. A lapsed donor needs to be missed, honestly, without guilt. A steady supporter needs to hear that they are the reason the work happens. A major donor needs a specific, confident, bigger ask. Write one sentence per group describing the message. The AI is about to turn each of those sentences into a letter.

**Step 4: Draft a version per segment, in your voice.**

Give the AI your best base appeal, the one you would have sent to everyone, plus the one-sentence message for each group. Ask it to rewrite the base letter for each segment, keeping your voice and your story, changing the framing and the ask to fit that group. You get five drafts back instead of one. They will not be finished. They will be a very good start.

**Step 5: You edit for heart, then send.**

Here is the part no AI touches. You read every version out loud. You fix the lines that sound a little too smooth. You verify every number, every program stat, every claim, because the AI will sound completely confident even when it is wrong, and it is your name on that letter. You add the one detail that makes it yours. Then you send.

**A few guardrails, because this is donor trust we are talking about.**

The AI does the sorting and the first drafts. You keep the relationship, the voice, and the facts. Never let it invent a donor's giving history, and never let a generated number reach a donor without checking it against your actual records. Segmentation is just respect at scale. It means finally having the time to say the true, specific thing to each group instead of one flattened message to all of them.

Start this in July and none of it is a scramble. You build the groups once, you draft once, and you spend the fall doing the part that actually raises money, which is talking to people.
