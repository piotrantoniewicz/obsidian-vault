We’ve all been there. Staring at a blank screen, trying to come up with social content that actually resonates, while keeping up with whatever the algorithm wants this month.

It’s a lot. But it’s worth the effort. Over [5.6 billion people worldwide](https://wearesocial.com/us/blog/2025/10/digital-2026-global-overview-report/) are active on social media today, and for a lot of your supporters, it’s where they’ll first hear your name, watch your story, or decide to give. Look at how a single Humans of New York post can turn one person’s story into a campaign that raises millions in days. Or how Movember turns something as simple as growing a mustache into a global conversation about men’s health every single year. Even a small shelter’s rescue video taking off on TikTok can turn into an overnight wave of donations.

When the right story catches on, it can build momentum far beyond what any single post could do on its own. So how do you actually harness that, without it eating your whole week? That’s where having a real social media strategy for nonprofits comes in. Let’s walk through how to plan smarter, tell better stories, and build a content approach that holds up campaign after campaign.

### Key takeaways

- **Storytelling beats posting updates.** Shift from “here’s what happened” to “here’s what it felt like” and people stop scrolling and start paying attention.
- **A little planning goes a long way.** Map your year, flag key dates like GivingTuesday, and batch your posts to keep your feed consistent without eating up your week.
- **Video is your highest-converting content.** It builds trust faster than any caption, so when you’re deciding what to post, ask if it can be a video first.

## The Importance of Storytelling

A [good story](https://www.causevox.com/blog/storytelling-in-fundraising-campaigns/) moves people. When you shift your mindset from “posting updates” to “telling stories,” your content stops feeling like an announcement and starts feeling like an invitation to be part of something.

- **Increasing donor engagement.** [Building a following](https://www.causevox.com/blog/donor-engagement-cycle-inspire-learn-engage-ask-thank-show-impact/) that actually talks back, comments, shares, and shows up.
- **Expanding your donor base.** [Reaching new people](https://www.causevox.com/blog/6-ways-to-find-new-donors/) who align with your mission but haven’t found you yet.

These goals aren’t mutually exclusive. Most nonprofits are working toward a mix of them at once. The key is picking clear, measurable objectives so you know what “working” actually looks like.

### Know Who You’re Talking To

Once your goals are set, get specific about who you’re trying to reach. Understanding your audience shapes everything from your tone to your platform choice.

A quick look at where people spend their time these days:

- **Facebook** still has the largest reach of any platform, with more than 3 billion monthly users. It tends to skew toward an older audience, making it a strong fit for reaching established donors.
- **Instagram** has around 3 billion monthly users as well, with the largest age group landing in the 25 to 34 range. It’s a strong fit if you’re trying to reach younger donors and rely heavily on visuals.
- **LinkedIn** has grown past 1.3 billion members worldwide. It’s the place for reaching corporate donors, professionals, and potential partners who want to see your organization’s credibility and impact.

These numbers shift every year, so it’s worth a quick gut check on your own platform performance every so often rather than assuming last year’s plan still fits.

## Building Out Your Content Calendar

Consistency is what makes a social strategy actually work. Planning your content in advance keeps your presence steady and helps you tie your messaging to key moments, like [GivingTuesday](https://www.causevox.com/blog/givingtuesday-campaign/) or your year end push, so you can ride that wave instead of scrambling to catch up with it.

To build a solid calendar, keep these in mind:

- **Define clear goals for each campaign or stretch of time**, whether that’s raising a specific amount, bringing in new donors, or building buzz for an initiative.
- **Map out your campaign timelines**, breaking each one into phases with specific actions attached.
- **Flag your critical dates**, like [GivingTuesday](https://www.causevox.com/givingtuesday-fundraising/), relevant holidays, or awareness days tied to your cause.
- **Mix up your content types** by blending storytelling, visuals, [video](https://www.causevox.com/blog/10-powerful-nonprofit-videos-to-inspire-you/), testimonials, and calls to action so your feed doesn’t feel repetitive.
- **Set aside real time to engage**, not just post. Replying to comments and messages is where a lot of trust actually gets built.

### Start With a Year Overview

Zoom out first. [Map your whole year](https://www.causevox.com/blog/set-realistic-fundraising-goals/) before you get into the weeks and months. It can be helpful to plan themes throughout the year and mark important dates in the calendar. Tools like [Buffer](https://buffer.com/) and [Hootsuite](https://www.hootsuite.com/) make it easy to jot down ideas as you think through your content.

Once your year is mapped, break it down by month, and then by post: subject, summary, and call to action for each one.

### Organize With Content Buckets

Group your content into a few clear buckets so your feed stays balanced:

- **Entertain:** Lighter content, like a fun video or a quiz, that still connects back to your mission.
- **Inspire:** Success stories, powerful quotes, or moments that show real impact.
- **Introduce:** A staff member, a volunteer, or a behind the scenes look at your work.
- **Inform:** Share something educational, like a simple infographic or an update on your field.
- **Interact:** Ask a question, run a poll, invite comments. Give people a reason to respond.
- **Update:** Keep supporters in the loop on campaign progress or upcoming events.
- **Call to action:** Give people a clear next step, whether that’s giving, sharing, or signing up.

### Make Batching Your Friend

Set aside dedicated time to write and schedule a batch of posts at once instead of writing something new every single day. It’s a small shift that saves a surprising amount of time and keeps your calendar feeling manageable instead of constant.

A social media planning tool (like Buffer, Hootsuite, or Meta) allows you to schedule content across multiple platforms that will be posted automatically. You can also see exactly what it will look like on your feed.

## Developing Your Content Strategy

Before you post anything, get clear on your buckets: impact stories, behind-the-scenes, stats, program highlights. These are your raw materials. But raw materials alone don’t move people. What turns a good post into a great one is a story arc: introduce a problem, let people feel it, show what changes, and end on hope.

### The Marketing Rule of One

Whatever you’re creating, whether it’s a single post or a whole campaign, try to stick to:

- One audience member in mind
- One message
- One story
- One call to action

Trying to say everything to everyone usually means saying nothing clearly. Keep it simple, keep it focused, and always give people something specific to do next.

### Use a Mix of Content

Compelling content usually pulls from a few different angles:

- **Impact stories** that show donors exactly what their support makes possible.
- **Strong visuals** that capture your cause and the people behind it.
- **Video** that tells a story, shows your work in action, or features a real testimonial.
- **Social proof**, like a quote from a donor or volunteer, that builds credibility.

### Video Is Your Most Powerful Tool

If you take away one thing from, make it this: video outperforms almost everything else. It stops the scroll. It builds trust faster than a caption ever could. And it consistently gets the most engagement across every platform.

You don’t need a production team or perfect lighting. What you need is authenticity. People aren’t looking for perfection. They’re looking for real.

So when you’re pulling from your content buckets, ask yourself: can this be a video? Behind-the-scenes moments, program highlights, even stats, almost anything can come to life on camera. Show the hands doing the work. Show the real reactions. Let people see the story, not just read about it.

## Ethical and Trauma-Informed Storytelling

Telling real stories means handling real people’s experiences with care. A few things worth keeping in mind: People don’t owe you their story just because your organization has supported them.

Consent has to be full, informed, and enthusiastic, and people should be able to withdraw their story at any time, even after it’s been shared. It’s also important to give space after someone’s story reaches a hopeful place before asking them to share it publicly, rather than rushing to capture it.

Involve the staff who work directly with the people you serve, and build an actual process for gathering stories rather than winging it. And throughout the process, give people real control: the option to reschedule, pause, or stop a conversation without any pressure.

You can be honest about the challenges your organization exists to solve without exploiting someone’s pain to make the point. Respecting the person behind the story is what makes the story trustworthy in the first place.
