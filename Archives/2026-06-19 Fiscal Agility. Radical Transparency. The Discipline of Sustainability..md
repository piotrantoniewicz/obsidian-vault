### TEAMS! Newsletter | Stewardship Edition | June 19, 2026

### The Pillar Nobody Wants to Talk About.

Five days. Five pillars. We've covered who you hire, how you engage them, how you govern technology, and what keeps every decision anchored to purpose. Today we close the series where all of it ultimately has to land.

Stewardship is perhaps the least glamorous pillar of the TEAMS! framework. It is also perhaps the most existential. Because here is the unvarnished truth that mission-driven leaders sometimes resist saying out loud: the most purpose-driven organization in the world is zero net good if it runs out of funding.

Passion doesn't make payroll. Clarity of mission doesn't cover a funding gap. The organizations that endure, that are still serving people a decade from now, two decades from now, are the ones that treat financial discipline not as a constraint on mission but as the infrastructure that makes the mission possible.

### Transparency as The Foundation of Trust

Your culture is completely visible to the outside world, to the individuals and families you serve, to your volunteers, donors, and government funders. That isn't a vulnerability. It's an asset. Clearly communicating your impact and maintaining transparency in how you steward resources are what inspire supporters to engage, contribute, and advocate over the long term.

> ***Openly sharing bad news is the single most effective way to build mutual respect and trust. That openness creates a powerful partnership that sustains the organization through economic downturns and operational disruptions.***

CEOs naturally want to lead with wins. I understand the instinct; you want your board energized, your donors confident, your staff motivated. But the boards that become true thought partners, that show up resourced and ready when a crisis hits, are boards that have been given an honest, unvarnished view of the organization's real challenges. Not just the highlights reel.

Embedding real problems into board agendas is not a weakness. It is governance excellence. It is the difference between a board that rubber-stamps and a board that co-owns.

### Speak Once, Listen Twice

My governance philosophy is simple enough to fit on a Post-it: speak once, listen twice. When you bring genuine operational complexity into the boardroom, not sanitized summaries, but real challenges with real stakes, you invite board members to become strategic thought partners.

When the board, the executive team, and senior staff share an unvarnished understanding of what isn't working, they can bring their collective expertise to bear on solutions, risk mitigation, and new paths forward. That alignment doesn't happen by accident. It happens because the leader chose transparency over optics and trusted the room to handle the truth.

### The Governance Standard

> ***By embedding real, complex operational challenges into your board agendas, you invite board members to move beyond perfunctory oversight and become true strategic thought partners. The board that knows the real picture is the board that shows up when it matters most.***

### Funding is Royalty

When revenues are strong, control your spending and build a cash reserve covering 6 months of operating costs. When the economic climate shifts, and it will shift, your organization will be positioned to meet urgent needs without missing a beat.

I want to be clear about what this is and isn't. This is not cautious or conservative financial management. This is strategic financial agility, the capacity to show up for your community as a force of stability in a crisis rather than a casualty of it. The organizations that serve people in the hardest moments are the ones that built their reserves in the good ones.

> ***Cash is royalty. Raise it when you can. Don't wait until you desperately need it.***

I've watched organizations with exceptional programs and strong cultures falter, not because of mission failure, but because they never built the financial cushion that would enable them absorb a funding disruption, a leadership transition, or an unexpected regulatory shift. Reserves aren't a luxury, they are a solid fiacal obligation.

### Unrestricted Giving: The Real Engine of Sustainability

Fundraising volatility is a structural risk that most nonprofits manage reactively rather than strategically. And one of the biggest contributors to that volatility is an over-reliance on restricted gifts, funding tied to specific programs or outcomes that doesn't move when organizational needs do.

The shift we've pushed for consistently is moving from a transactional donor relationships to transformational one. That means cultivating donors who share your broader vision and are willing to provide unrestricted, multi-year general operating support. Not because it's easier, it's actually harder. It requires a level of trust that takes years to build. But it is what truly drives sustainable, long-term impact.

> ***Show donors a transparent, multi-year strategic plan and budget, and have honest conversations about how unrestricted funding is what truly drives sustainable, long-term impact.***

Leaders who can articulate with confidence exactly how general operating funds translate into mission outcomes, and donors who trust the organization deeply enough to invest in its capacity rather than just its programs, that partnership is the financial model that endures.

### "Shipping The Gold Master"

At Electronic Arts, other teams I worked with lived by a milestone called the "gold master", the final, definitive version of a game that would be sent to production. Many times hitting that milestone required a fierce willingness to pivot mulitple times along the way. Technology advanced mid-development. Market trends shifted overnight. Consumer expectations changed. We had to build an adaptable framework that could be adjusted immediately without compromising the quality of what the teams were making.

The same discipline applies to nonprofit financial management. Stay tightly attuned to macro forces, funding shifts, policy changes, demographic trends, and regulatory evolution so you can adjust your strategy as the landscape evolves. The leaders who get caught flat-footed aren't the ones who lacked information. They're the ones who weren't watching, or who waited too long to act on what they saw.

By pairing an unshakeable ethical compass with rigorous fiscal discipline, we ensure that as the landscape changes, we are ready. Mission secure. Still standing. Still serving.

### 150 Editions. Still Here. Still Building.

When I wrote the first TEAMS! newsletter, I had a simple conviction: the disciplines that make organizations excellent, the ones that determine whether the work actually reaches the people who need it, deserve to be taken as seriously as any strategic initiative, any capital campaign, any board presentation.

Talent. Engagement. AI and Technology. Mission. Stewardship. Five pillars. One framework. And the same question underneath all of it, edition after edition: are we building something that lasts?

The answer isn't in the strategy document. It's in the daily choices of who you hire, how you show up, what tools you trust with your people's work, what you're willing to say out loud in the boardroom, and whether you're building the financial foundation that lets you be there for the next person who needs you.

That's what Scale with Soul means to me. And that's why, 150 editions in, I'm still writing.
