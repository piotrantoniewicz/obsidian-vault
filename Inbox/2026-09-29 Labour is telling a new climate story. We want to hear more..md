Prime Minister Andy Burnham says tackling climate change can help us [“believe in ourselves again.”](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/sep/17/andy-burnham-climate-crisis-funding-peoples-power-projects) Climate Minister Katie White’s social media [content](https://www.tiktok.com/@katiewhiteobemp) brims with stories of action, progress and potential.

Labour are – rightly – abandoning the narratives we know don’t work in favour of a story [people want to hear and be part of.](https://climateoutreach.org/btc/2025/) Amid the Labour Party Conference, here’s our take on the emerging Labour climate story – and our suggestions for how MPs and policymakers can land it with maximum impact.

## 1\. Connection beats convincing

Burnham is leading with “I know people are worried about climate change”, signalling that he’s listening, not lecturing.

The subtle shift from ‘you *should* care’ to ‘I know you care’ is all important for climate communicators. People tell us that they’re worried about climate change, but they’re sick of feeling preached at. When it comes to [earning trust](https://climateoutreach.org/reports/beyond-trusted-messengers-climate-communications/), communicators who empathise will always beat those who exhort.

## 2\. A shared sense that we can do it

When announcing new funding for community power, Burnham says “we can pursue a different route: help people make the change by creating jobs, lowering bills and bringing power back to them.”

“We’re halfway there!” [says White](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/katie-white-obe-mp-30966438_climatechange-climatechangeact-activity-7501196843838640129-4paO?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAAwhLvkBlj4qYFa492gZio9GqSYXnQhvXdI) of our emissions reduction progress. Decades of research into climate narratives have emphasised the need for agency, not just urgency. With climate science’s dire warnings now baked into everyday life, there’s even more need for communicators to show what’s possible. It’s a story that starts with ‘we’re on the case and it’s working’, not ‘we’re starting from scratch.’

## 4\. Being real

None of the above framings are about sugarcoating reality or downplaying the scale of the challenge climate change poses. It’s about showing that change is possible – and that it’s positive to act on the biggest challenge humanity faces.

As the Prime Minister says: “The climate crisis presents us with an enormous challenge and possibility. In the coming years, we need to change the way we travel, build homes and produce energy. I have long seen the green transition as the best chance we will ever have to reindustrialise Britain.”

## 5\. Match words with action

People in the UK are disillusioned with politics. They particularly struggle to trust politicians to talk about climate change. For people in the UK to back Labour on climate, compelling communication [must be matched](https://climateoutreach.org/burnham-government-climate-change/) by bold policy action.
