### TEAMS! Newsletter | AI & Technology Edition | July 15, 2026

Every week, someone is likely asking you how fast you’re moving on AI. A colleague or supporter likely forwards you an article about “agentic AI.” A vendor pitches an auto-updating feature that quietly weaves artificial intelligence into the tools you already use and trust. And every week, the subtext is the same: move now, move faster, or get left behind.

I've spent more than two decades in and around the technology industry, watching wave after wave of change arrive with the exact same promise and the exact same pressure. And for nearly a decade now, I've led an organization that shows up every week for 1,500 people with developmental differences across five counties. Those two vantage points taught me the same lesson, but from entirely opposite directions, and that is:

> ***Speed without stewardship isn't leadership. It's abdication.***

In social services and healthcare, blind speed isn't innovation, certainly not as it's understood in Silicon Valley. It's exposure. When your organization holds health-related records for the people who trust you most, an unvetted AI deployment isn't a shortcut. It's a risk with your name and your board's charter attached. Over the past year, AbilityPath took what we believe was the most appropriate and thoughtful approach while continuing to explore how AI can improve our services: we slowed down on purpose. We started early by piloting AI-only where **Protected Health Information** (“PHI”) couldn't be accessed. We built an evaluation process before any new platform could get near our systems. We conducted a targeted deployment involving PHI, and we trained our people on exactly where the walls are and why they matter.

### Executive Summary

Strategic readiness in today’s environment means building an operational “shield” before you fire up the AI engine. At AbilityPath, we've held off on ad hoc adoption for a more rigorous upfront evaluation process, one that starts by being model-agnostic and takes a governance-first posture. This approach is measured against the main question: “Will this tool allow us to better serve our community and grow human connection in the process?” Safety isn't the bottleneck. It's the foundation that makes everything else possible.

### Key Takeaways

- **Validate in the Safe Zone First:** Prove AI works in non-HIPAA administrative settings before it ever touches a case file.
- **Never Sign What You Can't Prove:** Build the evidence file before signing AI use attestations or certifications, not after.
- **Automate the Task, Never the Relationship:** AI carries the paperwork. People must always carry *cura personalis*, care for the whole person.
- **Build for Swappability, Not Loyalty:** No single vendor gets to hold your mission hostage. Take an AI model-agnostic approach to adoption.
- **Grow the Learning Quotient:** The muscle to learn, unlearn, and relearn will outlast any single model.

### Lessons from the Frontlines

**Validate in the Safe Zone First.** Rushing patient records or sensitive case data into an unvetted AI ecosystem isn't bold; it's reckless. Technological maturity starts with a “risk-isolation” framework. Over a yearlong deep dive into AI use cases, our most effective move was simple: deploy pilots strictly where **Protected Health Information** (“PHI”) and personally identifiable information would not be used or exposed.

Take talent acquisition. By running automated tools on a newly deployed, secure HR platform, we cut open-to-hire time by 50%, filling critical frontline roles faster without ever putting a single client record at risk. Proving the technology in neutral territory is how you learn what a system can do before you trust it anywhere near the people you serve. The pilot proved incredibly successful and gave us important lessons ahead of the next system deployment.

> ***Validating in neutral territory isn't caution for its own sake. It's how you earn the right to go further.***

**Never Sign What You Can't Prove.** The regulatory landscape for human services providers has shifted fundamentally. Oversight bodies across our sector now demand attestation architectures that require executives to certify their entire digital footprint, including silent, automated AI updates embedded in legacy office suites and communications platforms. Sign one of those blindly, and 100% of the compliance liability lands on you personally.

Fiduciary responsibility means never attesting without an “evidence file.” You should map your data to the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** and pressure-test technical safeguards, including **Single Sign-On** (" SSO”), **Multi-Factor Authentication** (“MFA”), and granular **Role-Based Access Control** (“RBAC”), before a signature is applied to a compliance guarantee or funding agreement.

- Tool Inventory & PHI Classification Mapping
- Active, Version-Specific BAA Contracts

- Board-Level AI Governance Risk Policies

**Automate the Task, Never the Relationship.** When frontline staff and clinicians hear the word “AI,” their first unspoken fears are displacement or a loss of connection with the people they serve. I reassure our team that from day one, the goal has been to automate the mundane so we can elevate our time and connection with the people we serve. When we bring automated documentation into an **Electronic Medical Records** (“EMR”) or case-note system, it undergoes the same rigorous evaluation and is built strictly as a supportive drafting tool, never a decision-maker. With human-in-the-loop safeguards in place, automated notes have reduced administrative friction, resulting in a 20% increase in weekly capacity to serve children and families. AI handles the paperwork. Our people deliver the life-changing connection; that's the Human Dividend at work.

> ***AI can carry the paperwork. It will never carry our relationships.***

**Build for Swappability, Not Loyalty.** The commercial AI landscape is volatile by design. Today's market leader could face a regulatory roadblock, a punishing price increase, or an adverse privacy shift by next quarter. Betting your mission on a single proprietary vendor is a bet I'm not willing to make. Simply toggling a privacy setting in a consumer-grade app doesn't create a safe, HIPAA-compliant environment.

Long-term systems sustainability requires a model-agnostic infrastructure: a centralized hub that routes data to specific, enterprise-secured platforms via private **APIs** (Application Programming Interfaces) or **MPCs** (Model Context Protocol), covered by explicitly defined **Business Associate Agreements** (“BAAs”). Non-sensitive marketing text can flow through different, likely more cost-effective tools. In addition, anything touching operational data stays within environments that contractually guarantee that our prompts never train someone else's public model. If a vendor's compliance posture changes, a swappable architecture allows your organization to easily swap engines without rebuilding workflows. Most platforms today are designed to easily swap out the LLMs (Large Language Models) they use. Make sure the ones you choose have taken this design approach.

### Grow the Learning Quotient

Technology moves faster than policy. Policy moves faster than human habit. In an environment governed by HIPAA and state-level confidentiality mandates, a single untracked AI error, such as an employee inadvertently pasting sensitive text into a public chatbot, can trigger a compliance issue.

---

**The Learning Quotient**

- ***Learn:*** Modern Prompt Engineering
- ***Unlearn:***Legacy Manual Data Logging
- ***Relearn:*** Agentic & Dynamic Systems

---

Building a high Learning Quotient means investing in the cognitive capacity of our social workers, clinicians, and administrative staff to continuously learn, unlearn, and relearn how to use digital tools. That includes rigorous training on the dos and don'ts of AI use, paired with a Culture of Blameless Reporting, so hallucinations or accidental exposures are flagged early and fixed quickly, without fear.

### Five Practical Steps to Putting This into Action:

If you want to move your organization from ad hoc and exposed AI uses to measured and safer uses, consider starting here:

- **Issue a Consumer Chatbot Policy:** Set a clear policy banning staff from entering identifiable client records, internal operations, or PII into free, consumer versions of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity or other LLMs.
- **Initiate a Data Lineage Assessment:** Task your operations and IT teams with mapping how data flows across your ecosystem and where AI features may already be embedded in legacy systems and recent updates.
- **Establish an Evaluation Protocol for Core Systems:** Before adopting any new platform, require a signed BAA, a guarantee against model training on your data, and administrative controls like SSO and MFA
- **Design a Tiered AI Decision-Rights Matrix:** Specify exactly which tasks staff can perform independently using approved tools, and which clinical or high-impact actions require escalated review.
- **Launch a Dedicated Board AI Oversight Group:** Build an operationally focused working group on your board that learns by doing alongside leadership, so fiduciary oversight keeps pace with your technical roadmap and AI adoption.

### Final Thoughts:

The point of bringing artificial intelligence into the social impact ecosystem was never to create distance between the communities we serve and us. It's the opposite. It can and should be a tool that enables us to get closer, faster, and for longer with the people we serve. A measured, governance-focused approach insulates our organizations from regulatory risk, and it is the foundation that supports and increases our capacity to deliver deeply human, relationship-driven care.

> ***“We don’t get to choose whether AI will change our sector, because it will. We only get to choose whether we lead that change with our values intact. I choose intact. Every time.”***
