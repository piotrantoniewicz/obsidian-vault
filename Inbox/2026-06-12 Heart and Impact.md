### TEAMS! Newsletter | Special Kick-Off Edition

***Why culture, not strategy, not funding, not even technology, is the heartbeat that keeps your mission going.***

This edition kicks off a special weeklong TEAMS! series. Starting Monday, June 15, I'll spend the week walking through the five pillars one at a time: **T** alent, **E** ngagement, **A** I & Technology, **M** ission, and **S** tewardship, each with insights and lessons culled from 150 editions of TEAMS! Consider today’s edition a roadmap for what follows.

Every leader I talk to in the social sector is running the same gauntlet right now: unpredictable government funding that shifts under your feet, a hiring market that won't hold still, community needs climbing faster than capacity, and generative AI rewriting the rules mid-game. The instinct is to firefight each day, waking up and triaging the day's crisis, repeating until the day is gone, and your actual strategy is buried somewhere under the ash.

### I get it. I've lived a version of it.

But here's the part that is often skipped because it implicates us: **you can’t fundraise your way out of a fragile culture.** You can't hire your way out of it, restructure your way out of it, or, as plenty are trying, buy a piece of software that fixes it. When the outside world gets loud, your internal culture is either the anchor that holds your people steady or the weight that drags them under. There is no third option.

I spent over 25 years at Electronic Arts, watching the company grow from $63 million to north of $5 billion. You learn a few things in a run like that, and the most durable lesson is this: the winning teams were almost never the ones with the best tools or the biggest budgets. They were the ones with the strongest culture: clarity about who they were, how they treated each other, and what they refused to compromise on. With technology changing every 18 months, culture was the constant that led to success.

AbilityPath turns 106 this year. We didn't last more than a century because we were lucky, and certainly not because the funding was generous; it has rarely been either. We lasted because, across five counties and roughly 1,500 people we serve every week, there is a *way we do things* that outlives any single leader, any one grant cycle, any one piece of technology.

### That's the heartbeat. Let me explain what I mean.

**Culture is the heartbeat that endures.** Culture is not a consultant's deliverable. It is not a slogan on a mug or the theme of a one-day offsite that everyone has forgotten by Thursday. Culture is the raw, unglamorous reality of how your teams treat each other when no one is watching. It’s how a supervisor handles a mistake, or how leadership communicates when things are tough.

Think of it the way you'd think of a heart. It doesn't get applause. It beats roughly 100,000 times a day, whether or not anyone is paying attention, and its only job is to push life out to every extremity, every site, every program, every team interaction across every county. When the culture is strong, that circulation reaches the edges; people three steps removed from leadership still feel the pulse. When it's neglected, the extremities go cold first: quiet turnover, disengagement, a supervisor cutting a corner no one sees. The dangerous part is that a neglected heart doesn't warn you politely. It doesn't send a memo. It just stops one day, and by then you're in trouble.

A healthy heart does some of the recruiting for you, too. Aligned people feel the pulse and stay; the misaligned feel it and drift off. But that's a side effect, not the main event. The main event is circulation. Keep the blood moving to the edges, and the whole body holds.

### The TEAMS! you build on

Over the years, I've come to believe a culture built to last isn't one big thing. It's five disciplines, consistently followed, each held with equal seriousness. I call them TEAMS!.

- **Talent:** Hire deliberately, develop relentlessly, and retain as it matters. Because it does.
- **Engagement:** Human-centered, walk-around leadership. Get out from behind the screen and stay connected.
- **AI & Technology:** Adopt it humanistically. Let the machines carry the administrative load so your people can spend more time with the people they're here to serve.
- **Mission:** Keep the purpose of your mission boldly front and center for all you do. Always.
- **Stewardship:** Financial agility, radical transparency, and the willingness to do the whole job, not just the fun part. The Jesuits I learned from at Santa Clara called it *cura personalis,* care for the whole person. Same idea, older words.

Starting Monday, June 15, I'll take these one at a time, a full edition on each. This piece is the overview. The depth comes next.

### Legacy is not the same as standing still

Here's a misconception worth dispelling early: staying true to a 106-year mission does not mean being frozen in time. It is the opposite. Stewardship of a legacy sometimes demands bold and uncomfortable change, from strategic mergers to new programs to hard pivots, precisely so the mission survives and thrives.

This July, we're integrating Stroke Awareness & Recovery into AbilityPath, expanding services for a severely underserved community. This is a new capability layered on our century-old foundation. That's evolution without drift. The tools and the services can change. The heart beneath them shouldn't.

### None of this is theory. Here are five things you can do now:

- **Sit in on a huddle.** Watch one team meeting and ask one question: Are your core values showing up in how people solve real problems, or are they invisible?
- **Walk around.** Leave the desk and the Zoom. Talk to your team, community members, donors, board members, or volunteers, and just listen to what's important to them right now.
- **Run an AI gut-check.** Block an hour to ask where generative AI and technology could lift administrative weight off your people, not to replace them, to free them.
- **Catch someone doing it right.** Find one person who lived a core value this week and publicly recognize them. You reinforce the behavior you reward.
- **Reread your onboarding.** Do your hiring materials make the culture and mission obvious? They should be your first filter, not an afterthought.

### The close

The question in the social sector has never been whether needs will keep coming. They always will. The real question is whether we'll build the cultural infrastructure, talent systems, technological fluency, and financial discipline to keep the heart strong enough to meet the needs of the people our mission is chartered to serve, decade after decade.

> ***Build that, and you're running an organization that can scale, with heart. And you're handing the organization a culture strong enough to thrive for decades to come.***
