### Grown, Not Hired

*What happens when an organization invests in its own people first, and why it lets us serve more of the people who count on us*

### The Idea in Brief

- ***The idea.***Leaders who grow up within a mission carry it in ways that are harder to import into the organization, so we invest in developing them internally.
- ***In practice,***S.C.A.L.E., a leadership program we’ll kick off at the end of the month, develops our leadership team and equips them with the tools to manage into the future; You As A Leader reaches our managers and staff to support growth into future leadership roles; and targeted training, including the safe and ethical use of AI, keeps everyone’s skills current.
- ***The payoff.***A team that lives a shared set of practices and values improves our services and helps us serve more people.

### The Cairns: Marking the Trail

Above the tree line, where trails fade into rock, hikers sometimes come across a small stack of stones. Someone who passed this way earlier placed them there to help you find your way. When you arrive, you add a stone of your own for whoever comes next. No single stone matters much. The cairn matters, and it exists only because everyone who walked by chose to give something back.

I think about that stack of stones whenever I think about leadership at AbilityPath. Our work depends on people who deeply understand why we do it, and I’ve come to believe the surest way to develop them is to grow them. A coordinator becomes a manager. A manager becomes a director, and a director becomes a vice president. At each step, someone builds on what the person before them left behind. Good people join us from everywhere, and I’m grateful for each of them. What I care deeply about is what we do together after they arrive.

### A path people can see

People stretch when they can see where the next step leads. So we try to make the path visible. A coordinator who becomes a manager and then a director is a real path through our organization. We want every new team member to picture themselves a few steps ahead and know we’re investing in getting them there. There’s a second benefit that’s easy to miss. Leaders who grow up within an organization’s mission arrive at every new role already fluent in who we serve and why. Nobody has to teach them the mission from scratch because they’ve been living it. They carry that understanding into every decision, big and small.

A visible path also changes how everyday work feels. When people can see a route ahead of them, the tasks in front of them read as the first chapter of a longer story, making it much more than just a job. We want every team member to have that sense of direction.

### Two programs, one culture

We built two programs to support that path. **S.C.A.L.E.** is for our leadership team; organizational and leadership expert Diane Flynn developed it. It examines how we work together across five dimensions: **Self-Awareness, Communication, Alignment, Learning Mindset, and Efficiency.** It gathers candid team feedback and pairs it with a coaching and development plan, so growth rests on evidence and practice rather than good intentions. What I appreciate most is that it asks leaders to look at themselves first. If we want an organization that reflects, learns, and adjusts, the people at the leadership table need to go first and be seen doing so.

**You As A Leader** is designed for our staff. It begins with a three-day in-person workshop and continues with five to six months of coaching and peer learning. Cohorts are small, typically about a dozen people, so colleagues from different departments get to know each other well. Participants report stronger interdepartmental relationships, greater confidence in leading, peer coaching and reflection habits that outlast the workshop, and a deeper understanding of our mission and programs.

### Five practices worth living

Underneath both programs sits a short list of practices I chose deliberately:

**1\. Lifelong learning,**

**2\. Dedication to the mission,**

**3\. First Principles thinking,**

**4\. The Four Agreements, and**

**5\. A Beginner’s Mindset.**

Lifelong learning and a Beginner’s Mindset keep us curious at every level, mine included. First Principles thinking invites us to determine what is actually true about a challenge before we reach for the familiar answer. The Four Agreements (be impeccable with your word, don’t take anything personally, don’t make assumptions, and always do your best) provide a shared language for how we treat one another. Dedication to the mission is what the others are for; it is why we do this work.

I don’t think of these as five separate initiatives. They’re closer to a single way of working: stay curious, tell the truth, start with what’s real, treat one another well, and keep the people we serve in view. Each practice strengthens the others.

> ***The goal is not a team that can recite these practices. It is a team that lives them.***

When people live these practices, they foster and expand them in ways we could never design on our own. That’s when our services improve, and better services allow us to welcome more people.

### Judgment before tools

Alongside the programs, we run targeted trainings as the world changes. Safe and ethical use of AI is one of them, and I think of it as teaching two skills at once.

- **The first is leadership:** Using judgment and critical thinking, and always asking, “How does this benefit the people we serve?”
- **The second is literacy:** Knowing how to use the tools, understanding which guardrails apply, and protecting data and privacy.
- **One principle ties them together:** People make decisions about people, not AI. This training belongs to everyone, and our leaders model it every day.

We want both skills because each depends on the other. A tool used without judgment can produce a confident answer to the wrong question, and judgment without tool fluency leaves good ideas stranded. Together, they let people move quickly while staying accountable to the person on the other side of every decision.

### The trail ahead

Every cairn begins with a single stone, and it grows as each traveler adds another. The leaders who will carry AbilityPath forward are already on the trail: coordinators learning to manage, managers stepping into leadership, and directors shaping what comes next. Our responsibility is to ensure each of them finds a marker to follow and feels welcome to leave one behind.

That is the heart of Building to Belong, our 2030 vision. People First and Collaboration become real only when we invest in the people who do the work. Lifelong learning honors every journey, whether it belongs to someone we serve or to someone who serves alongside them. It moves us toward our north star of a world where everyone feels accepted, respected, and included.
