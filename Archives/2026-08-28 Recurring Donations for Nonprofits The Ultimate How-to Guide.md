Monthly giving has become one of the most reliable ways for nonprofits to fund their work, but building a programme that actually grows takes more than turning on a “make this monthly” checkbox and hoping for the best. It requires the right platform, a deliberate strategy, and communications that treat recurring donors as the long-term partners they are, not just a transaction that happens to repeat.

This guide walks through how to set up, grow, and automate a recurring donations programme that gives your organisation dependable, predictable revenue year after year.

## What Are Recurring Donations for Nonprofits?

A recurring donation is a gift a supporter agrees to give on a set schedule, most often monthly, rather than as a single one-time transaction. The donor sets it up once, and the payment processes automatically going forward until they choose to change or cancel it.

For nonprofits, this is different from a pledge or a multi-year commitment in one important way: **The donor is opting into an ongoing relationship, not a fixed total**. A monthly donor giving $25 a month hasn’t committed to $300 for the year. They’ve committed to being a recurring part of your organisation’s work and mission. That commitment is why so many nonprofits treat monthly giving programmes as a distinct [donor segment](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/how-to-leverage-key-donor-segments-for-year-end-fundraising-success/) with its own messaging, cadence, and stewardship plan.

Whatever your organisation calls the programme internally — monthly giving, sustainer programme, subscriptions, or something else entirely — the mechanics are the same: A donor authorizes automatic, recurring payments, and your organisation builds a programme around retaining and growing that base.

## Benefits of Recurring Donations

Recurring donations solve a problem every nonprofit finance and development team knows well: unpredictable revenue. A single large gift is wonderful, but it’s hard to plan around. A base of monthly donors is a foundation you can build a budget on.

A few of the reasons recurring giving is worth prioritising:

- **Predictable, budgetable revenue:** Knowing that a certain dollar amount will come in each month makes forecasting and planning meaningfully easier, especially for smaller organisations without large reserve funds.
- **Higher donor lifetime value:** A donor who gives $20 per month for three years has contributed far more than most one-time gifts, often without ever feeling like they made a “big” donation.
- **Lower cost per dollar raised over time:** Once a recurring donor is set up, the acquisition cost is already spent. Retaining them costs far less than [acquiring a new one-time donor](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/digital-donor-acquisition-strategies/) of equivalent value.
- **Deeper donor relationships:** Recurring donors tend to be your most engaged supporters. They’ve already said yes to an ongoing relationship, which makes them a natural audience for volunteering, event participation, and [advocacy](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/solutions/advocacy/).
- **Resilience during unpredictable years:** A diversified base of monthly gifts can help cushion the impact when major gifts or grant funding fluctuate.

None of this happens automatically just because a donor cheques a recurring box. It happens because the organisation builds a programme designed to earn and keep that ongoing trust.

## How to Set Up Recurring Donations

Setting up a recurring giving programme requires several distinct pieces: the platform, the donor-facing tools, the payment logic, and the follow-up experience. Getting each one right before launch will help you avoid a lot of cleanup later.

### Choose Your Donation Platform

The platform behind your donation form is the foundation everything else in this guide depends on. It needs to handle recurring billing cleanly, integrate with your CRM, support flexible ask arrays and upgrade prompts, include mobile-friendly donation forms, and give your team visibility into performance without a developer on standby.

We’ll go deeper on what to look for in a platform later in this guide, but it’s worth choosing this piece first. Strategy and stewardship tactics only work if the underlying system can execute on them.

### Create a Supporter Hub

Once a donor commits to giving monthly, they need a simple way to manage that commitment without emailing your team every time they want to update a card or change an amount. A supporter hub gives donors self-service access to their own giving history, payment details, and recurring gift settings.

This matters more than it might seem. Every time a donor has to e-mail or call your organisation to make a simple update, there’s a chance the request gets delayed, lost, or simply never acted on, and a lapsed recurring gift because of an expired card is a completely avoidable loss. A well-built supporter hub keeps donors in control of their own giving relationship, which reduces friction for them and reduces manual work for your team.

Cheque out our [step-by-step instructions on building a Supporter Hub](https://knowledge.engagingnetworks.net/pages/supporter-hub-step-by-step) in Engaging Networks.

### Configure Payment Options

Recurring donors should have flexibility in how and when they give, which comes down to a few configuration decisions.

- **Payment methods:** Support credit and debit cards, ACH/bank transfer, and digital wallets, since not every donor wants their card charged automatically.
- **Billing date and frequency:** Where possible, let donors choose when their gift processes each month. Someone who gets paid on the 15th may not want to process their payment on the 1st.
- **Failed payment planning:** Cards expire, get replaced, or get flagged for fraud, and a recurring programme without a plan for failed payments will quietly let donors slip through the cracks. We’ll cover automated recovery in more detail later in this guide.

### Create a Welcome Journey

The first few touchpoints after someone becomes a recurring donor set the tone for the entire relationship. A welcome journey — typically a short, automated series of emails — should confirm the gift, thank the donor genuinely, explain what their monthly support makes possible, and set expectations for what kind of communication they’ll receive going forward.

This is also a natural place to point donors toward their new supporter hub account so they know exactly where to go if they ever need to make a change.

### Test Before Launch

Before a recurring programme goes live to real donors, walk through the entire donor experience yourself. A few things worth confirming before launch:

- The donation form works cleanly on both desktop and mobile.
- A test gift processes correctly and shows up where it should.
- The recurring charge bills correctly on the next cycle, not just the first one.
- The welcome journey triggers as expected, with the right timing and content.
- A cancelled or failed payment is handled the way you intended, not just however the default settings happen to handle it.

## 7 Effective Strategies for Recurring Donations

Setting up the logistics is only part of the equation. The strategies below shape how donors perceive your recurring donation programme, and whether it feels worth joining and staying in.

### 1\. Set Clear Goals

Before promoting a monthly giving programme, define what success looks like. That might mean a target number of new monthly donors this year, a retention rate you want to hit annually, or a specific revenue goal the programme needs to reach to fund a particular initiative. Clear goals give your team something concrete to measure against and make it easier to justify the time investment a strong recurring programme requires.

### 2\. Define Your Value Proposition

Donors need a clear answer to “why give monthly instead of once?” The most effective value propositions tend to focus on the ongoing impact recurring gifts make possible, rather than framing recurring giving purely around convenience for the donor.

Take an animal shelter that relies heavily on foster placements, for example. A one-time gift might cover a round of vaccinations — useful, but disconnected from anything ongoing. A monthly gift, framed as “keeping our foster kennel ready for the next animal in need, every month,” ties the donor to the shelter’s larger mission rather than a single transaction.

### 3\. Simplify the Sign-Up Experience

Every additional field, click, or step on your recurring giving sign-up is a chance for a donor to abandon the process. Keep the form focused, make the recurring option visually clear and appealing, and remove any unnecessary steps between a donor’s decision to give monthly and their gift actually processing.

### 4\. Give Your Programme a Memorable Identity

Picture two donors setting up a monthly gift. One sees a form labelled “Recurring Donation.” The other sees “Join the Inner Circle.” Same transaction, same amount, but the second donor is joining something with a name, an identity, a sense of belonging.

A bit of branding and consistent visual identity across your donation page, emails, and donor communications all reinforce that a donor has joined a community, not just set up a recurring charge.

### 5\. Offer Meaningful Incentives

Incentives for monthly sustainers can help, but they should reinforce the mission rather than distract from it. A branded welcome gift that’s tied to impact, exclusive updates from the field, or invitations to donor-only gatherings all tend to work better than generic swag.

### 6\. Promote the Programme on All Channels

A monthly giving programme shouldn’t live only on a dedicated landing page, or as a checkbox on your donation form. It should show up on your homepage, in e-mail signatures, in appeal emails and newsletters, in social posts, at events, and in direct mail. Consistency across channels reinforces that this is a core part of how your organisation is funded, not a side offer.

### 7\. Show Your Appreciation

Recurring donors are easy to take for granted precisely because their gift doesn’t require a new decision each month. Building in regular, [genuine appreciation](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/the-power-of-thanks-and-gratitude/) in the form of handwritten notes, milestone acknowledgments (a donor’s one-year anniversary of giving, for example), or a simple personal thank-you call helps keep sustainers engaged.

## How to Increase Recurring Donations

Once the fundamentals are in place, growing the programme becomes a matter of where you look for new donors and how much friction stands between a supporter and a recurring gift.

### Tap Into Existing One-Time Donors in Your CRM

Your most promising pool of new monthly donors is very likely already in your database. Previous donors — especially those who’ve given multiple times, or who’ve engaged with your organisation in other ways (volunteering, attending events, or opening your emails consistently) — are often good candidates for a monthly ask.

This is where segmentation matters. A platform like Engaging Networks lets you segment your audience by giving history, engagement level, and behaviour so you can build a targeted upgrade campaign instead of sending a generic message to your entire list. **The more relevant the ask, the better it tends to perform.**

### Promote Your Programme Consistently

Increasing recurring donations rarely comes from a single campaign. It comes from consistently keeping the monthly giving option visible across every touchpoint — appeals, thank-you pages, newsletters, social media, etc. — so that supporters see it repeatedly and eventually act when the timing is right for them.

That consistency works best alongside the occasional dedicated push. A campaign focused on growing your monthly donor base, timed to a natural moment like [summer fundraising](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/summer-fundraising-ideas/) or [year-end giving](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/year-end-giving-guide/), gives supporters a clear reason to act now.

### Optimise Your Donation Form

#### Add a One-Click Recurring Checkbox to All Your Forms

Every donation form across your site and campaigns, not just your dedicated monthly giving page, should include a simple checkbox or toggle that lets a donor turn any gift into a recurring one with a single click. This removes the friction of navigating to a separate page entirely.

#### Make Recurring Giving the Default

Consider making a monthly gift the pre-selected choice, with one-time still clearly available for donors who prefer it. Beyond the donation form itself, consider how default framing shows up elsewhere: in phone scripts, direct mail asks, and event pledge cards. If your team’s default suggestion in every giving conversation is a monthly amount rather than a one-time ask, more supporters will end up giving that way simply because it was the first option presented to them.

#### Consider an Upsell Lightbox

An [upsell lightbox](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/upselling-donors-monthly-giving-lightbox/) intercepts a donor before they complete a one-time donation. Coupled with an enticing offer — like a lower suggested donation amount or a membership to an elite supporter group — upsell lightboxes can convert one-time donors into recurring supporters. ### Demonstrate Monthly Impact

Donors who understand exactly what their monthly gift funds tend to stay engaged longer. Concrete framing, like “$15 a month provides X” or “Your gift covers Y each quarter,” makes the recurring gift tangible. Sharing this kind of impact regularly, not just at sign-up, reinforces the value of an ongoing gift.

## How to Ask a Recurring Donor to Upgrade Their Gift

Growing a programme isn’t only about new donors. Existing monthly donors are often willing to give more, provided the ask is timed well and framed around impact rather than need.

### Know When to Ask

Timing matters as much as the message itself, and a few key moments tend to work better than a cold ask out of nowhere:

- The anniversary of a donor’s first gift
- After a major programme success or milestone
- At year-end
- Following a period of sustained engagement, like event attendance or an advocacy action

What doesn’t work as well? Asking too soon after someone signs up, before they’ve had a chance to feel invested in the relationship.

### Personalise the Request

A generic “consider increasing your gift” e-mail rarely performs as well as a message that references the donor’s specific giving history or the causes they’ve engaged with most. Personalising the request signals that you value donor relationships, not just their funds.

### Suggest a Small Monthly Increase

A modest, specific increase, such as an extra $5 or $10 a month, tends to feel more approachable than a large jump or an open-ended ask. Suggesting a specific new total, rather than leaving it entirely open, also makes the decision easier for the donor to say yes to quickly.

### Focus on Increased Impact

What does an extra $10 a month actually pay for? Frame the ask around specific outcomes — an additional month of medication for a patient in your care programme, one more family served each quarter, or launching a new programme, for example. Those are outcomes that a donor can feel good about funding.

### Automate Upgrade Campaigns

Automated upgrade campaigns can trigger based on a donor’s anniversary and engagement milestones, giving level, or engagement data. This ensures upgrade asks go out consistently and at the right moment, without depending on someone remembering to cheque a spreadsheet.

## How Automation Improves Recurring Donations

Much of the strategy above comes down to consistency, and consistency at scale is hard to maintain by hand. [Automation](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/marketing-automations-for-nonprofits/) handles the repeatable parts of honoring a donor relationship, freeing up time for the personal outreach that automation can’t replace.

### Automate Welcome and Stewardship Emails

Beyond the initial welcome journey, ongoing stewardship can be automated through regular impact updates, milestone acknowledgments, and seasonal touchpoints. That means every recurring donor gets consistent, timely communication without your team having to manually send each one.

### Recover Failed Payments Automatically

Why would a loyal monthly donor stop giving? Often, they don’t decide to. Their card expires, gets replaced after a fraud alert, or simply hits an issuing bank glitch, and the charge fails with no one on either side aware it happened.

Automated dunning management closes that gap by retrying a failed charge on a set schedule and notifying the donor if action is needed, recovering gifts that would otherwise lapse silently.

### Send Card Expiration Reminders

Rather than waiting for a payment to fail, automated reminders can reach donors before their card expires, prompting them to update their payment details proactively through their supporter hub account. This prevents the lapse from happening in the first place rather than trying to recover after the fact.

### Personalise Donor Communications

Automation makes it possible to personalise communications at scale, referencing a donor’s specific giving history or interests with no extra work. This kind of relevant, tailored communication tends to perform better than generic blasts sent to an entire list.

This can happen at two levels. Segmented e-mail blasts let you group donors by shared traits and send each group a version of the message built around what they actually care about. Conditional content goes a step further, showing different text, images, or asks to different donors within the same e-mail message.

### Identify Upgrade Opportunities

Nonprofit automation tools can flag donors who show signals of being ready for an upgrade ask based on engagement and giving history, so your team can focus outreach on the donors most likely to say yes.

### Measure Programme Performance

Automation isn’t only about donor-facing communication. [Unified reporting](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/solutions/nonprofit-reporting/) that tracks retention rate, average gift size, upgrade rate, and failed payment recovery gives your team the visibility needed to know what’s working and where the programme needs attention.

## Choosing the Right Recurring Donation Software

Every strategy in this guide depends on choosing the right donation platform that can support the features you need. Here’s what to look for.

### Flexible Giving Options

Your platform should support multiple gift frequencies, payment methods, and easy upgrade or downgrade paths, so donors have flexibility in how they give. A Supporter Hub also gives donors control over their giving, so they can change their payment method or monthly gift amount seamlessly.

### CRM Integration

Your donation platform and CRM need to work together seamlessly. Segmentation, donor history, and engagement data all need to flow between systems so you can build targeted campaigns instead of working from incomplete or outdated donor information.

### Marketing Automation

Look for a platform that can trigger welcome journeys, stewardship sequences, and upgrade campaigns automatically based on donor behaviour and milestones. The more your nonprofit tools are unified in a platform like Engaging Networks, the more you can automate.

### Payment Processing

Reliable, [secure payment processing](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/integrations/#Payment) with support for multiple payment methods and automated failed payment recovery is foundational. This is the piece that keeps recurring gifts actually recurring.

### Reporting and Analytics

Unified reporting that brings together fundraising, event, and campaign data in one place makes it possible to see the full picture of your recurring giving programme’s performance, rather than piecing together numbers from disconnected systems.

### Security and Compliance

Any platform processing recurring payments should meet [Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) compliance standards](https://www.engagingnetworks.net/blog/pci-compliance-nonprofit-guide/) and follow strong data security practices. Donor trust depends on their financial information being handled securely, and a breach can undo years of relationship-building instantly.

### Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Recurring Donation Platform

Before committing to a recurring donation platform, it’s worth asking:

- Does it support flexible giving options and one-click recurring gift upgrades on every form?
- How well does it integrate with our existing CRM and reporting tools?
- Can it automate stewardship, welcome journeys, and upgrade campaigns based on donor behaviour?
- What happens when a payment fails, and how much of that recovery is automated versus manual?
- What reports are available? Does reporting cover fundraising, events, e-mail, and advocacy in one place?
- Is it PCI compliant, and what additional security features are available?
