Three years ago, I was in Vegas for Clari's annual sales kick off.

I ended up chatting with a sales rep who had been working on her LinkedIn content for a while, but getting frustrated because she wasn’t seeing the results she wanted.

After some back and forth, she asked me a question that really stood out:

*How do I get people to care about my content?*

I didn't want to give her the cliche *"You just need to know your audience better."* Because she clearly already did based on our conversation.

What she's really struggling with is how to get people to *notice* her content.

Because after reviewing some of her posts I could clearly see that her ideas and experience are great.

**But the ways she's presenting that information isn't very enticing.**

And that can be easily fixed by using certain writing techniques.

### Couple more things:

- **I was intentionally very bold in my claims** because my research had uncovered something incredibly insightful, actionable, and relevant. That's not always the case though, so always keep your social contract. Don't over promise then under deliver (or risk losing credibility). *That's called click bait.*
- Because I spent so much time writing this hook copy, and because I was confident in it**, I also used this exact copy when sending to the email list too** - which also performed really well. Two birds one stone.
- My last advice: **fall in love with this type of writing**. You don't have to be this bold if that isn't your style, but learning how to convert attention to action is **extremely valuable** in your content journey.
