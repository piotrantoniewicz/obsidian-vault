Experiment ID: #203187

### Word of Life Fellowship

#### Experiment Summary

Timeframe: 04/09/2026 - 04/14/2026

In an effort to increase the landing page conversion rate for Word of Life’s Galatians Course funnel, we tested a layout inspired by other high-performing course landing pages. We changed the headline from a value-driven statement to a straightforward description headline. We added a strong call to action and value proposition reinforcement to combat the simplification of the headline. We updated the CTA button to reinforce that the course is free.

#### Research Question

We believe a short, informative, simple headline will increase conversions on an online course sign-up page compared to a longer, value-driven, narrative-led headline.

#### Flux Metrics Affected

The Flux Metrics analyze the three primary metrics that affect revenue (traffic, conversion rate, and average gift). This experiment produced the following results:

0% increase in traffic  
× 48.6% increase in conversion rate  
× 0% increase in average gift

#### Key Learnings

The key learnings from this experiment are that a clear, headline-driven, and more scannable layout for the Word of Life's Galatians Course funnel significantly increased course enrollments compared to a longer, narrative-led introduction. The treatment resulted in a 46.2% increase in email acquisition for all traffic with a 97.8% statistical level of confidence, proving that the straight-to-the-point approach was more effective in driving conversions. In future experimentation, we should prioritize simplicity and clarity in our landing page layouts. By making the value proposition easy to understand and act on, we can increase engagement and conversion rates. It is also important to continue testing different elements of the landing page, such as headlines, CTAs, and overall layout, to further optimize for the best results. Additionally, it would be beneficial to apply similar principles to other funnels and campaigns within Word of Life Fellowship to see if the success of this experiment can be replicated across different areas of the organization.
