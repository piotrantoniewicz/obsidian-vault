### I have a joke I make every election season. I go up to peers of mine, people who work in politics, and ask brightly whether they're excited for the elections. Then I watch their faces contort with stress.

These are the most political people I know.

Something has gone wrong when the people most drawn to this work are apologizing for how it makes them feel.

Think about what it looks like when politics comes to find you. An email saying “URGENT” and asking you for $10.. A text asking whether you saw the email. A deadline that isn't real, attached to a match that isn't a match. Open it, give something, pass it on, and once a year go and vote against someone.

You can absorb an enormous amount of politics that way and never once do any politics.

Here is what I find strange. Nobody in this industry is trying to fix the joylessness. It's not that we handle it badly. It just simply doesn't appear at all. The human component is not anywhere. It’s not built into our thinking, our tools, or how we connect with people.

Srđa Popović, who helped bring down Milošević partly by making him ridiculous, argues that humor isn't the relief from the struggle: it's a weapon inside it. Fear is the mortar holding bad systems together, and fun? Fun is what dissolves fear.

**It is through this human connection that people take part. And in the doing, beliefs form. The belonging and the doing come first.** Which means a politics that gives people nothing real to do has stopped producing the very thing it runs on.

The disengagement we all lament isn't a mystery, and it isn't a character flaw in the public. It's exactly what we built.
