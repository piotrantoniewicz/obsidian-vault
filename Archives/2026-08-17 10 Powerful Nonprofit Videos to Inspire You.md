Your appeal letter might be beautifully written. Your case for support might be airtight. But a donor who watches a real person talk about what your organization meant to them will feel something no paragraph can replicate.

We’re not just saying that. 80% of the highest-grossing campaigns on CauseVox have used video as part of their fundraising strategy. And [57% of people who watch a nonprofit video](https://nonprofitssource.com/online-giving-statistics/social-media/) go on to donate. That’s not a coincidence, that’s the power of seeing a real face, hearing a real voice, and feeling a real story.

The most effective nonprofit videos aren’t the most expensive ones. They’re the most honest ones. You don’t need a film crew or a big production budget. You need a story worth telling, and if you’re doing this work, you already have one.

To prove it, we rounded up 10 nonprofit videos that absolutely nail it, and pulled out the lessons you can steal for your own campaigns. Let’s dig in.

## 10 Nonprofit Videos That Get It Right

### 1\. Inspire Your Audience

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d64fyNi0UWg)

[*Amigos de las Americas*](https://amigosinternational.org/)

Amigos has been sending young people on life-changing volunteer programs across Latin America and the U.S. since 1965. Their video captures what that experience actually looks like: young volunteers working side by side with local peers, learning Spanish, building friendships across borders, and doing real community service work in the places they’re placed.

What makes it inspiring isn’t a polished script, it’s the faces of the volunteers themselves. You can see the moment a 16-year-old realizes they’re capable of more than they thought. That’s the story. More than 30,000 alumni have come back from these programs with sharper leadership skills, deeper social awareness, and a genuine drive to make change, and the video makes you feel exactly why.

**What to steal:** Let the experience speak for itself. If your program transforms the people who participate in it, put a camera on them in the middle of that transformation. Audiences don’t need to be told the impact is real, they need to see it on someone’s face.

### 2\. Show Your Mission In Action

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KAEtqQblWJk)

[*Volo Kids*](https://volokids.org/)

In 2015, Volo Kids launched their first sports program at Herring Run Park in Baltimore, 78 kids, one rec center, a city in the middle of healing. Their “Then and Now” video marks ten years since that first program, and the contrast does all the talking: 78 kids then. More than 80,000 kids across eight cities now.

That’s the power of a then-and-now video done right. You don’t need dramatic music or a celebrity voiceover. You just need to show where you started and where you are, and let the distance between those two points land.

**What to steal:** Numbers alone don’t move people, but numbers with context do. Anchor your growth in a specific moment, a first program, a first class, a first event, and show how far you’ve come from there. The before makes the after meaningful.

### 3\. Tell One Person’s Story

<iframe title="Not I But We | Cape Town, South Africa" src="https://player.vimeo.com/video/1064409462?dnt=1&amp;app_id=122963" width="1140" height="641" frameborder="0" allow="autoplay; fullscreen; picture-in-picture; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; web-share"></iframe>

[*Not I But We*](https://notibutwe.com/)

Not I But We offers survivors of human trafficking and domestic violence the opportunity to rebuild their lives through dignified work. Their video follows a survivor drafting patterns and sewing while she shares, in her own words, what the organization means to her.

It’s a simple but effective combination: the work visible on screen, the impact audible in her voice. Together, they say more than either could alone.

**What to steal:** Let someone talk about your organization while they’re doing something. Action grounds a testimonial in reality, it shows the work, not just the words. And sometimes the most powerful stories aren’t dramatic, they’re just someone, at a table, for the first time feeling like they belong there.

### 4\. Motivate A Specific Action

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RA2sn3cMiFc)

[*Orangewood Foundation*](https://orangewoodfoundation.org/)

Orangewood Foundation supports young people in Orange County who are transitioning out of foster care, experiencing homelessness, or surviving trafficking, helping them find housing, education, job skills, and stability. The cause is serious, but their event video is anything but heavy.

The Orangewood Challenge is a multi-sport, team-based obstacle course and their promo video leans all the way into the fun. Teams of four running, navigating, competing with music, food, and community. The video makes it look like exactly what it is: a great day that also happens to change lives. The ask is singular and impossible to miss: register your team.

That’s the lesson. When you’re promoting an [event](https://www.causevox.com/blog/connect-event-giving-auctions-follow-up/), your video’s only job is to make someone think I want to be there*.* Orangewood doesn’t try to explain every program or share every statistic. They show you a good time with a good purpose, and they tell you exactly how to sign up.

**What to steal:** One video, one call to action. The more fun and tangible you make that action feel, the easier it is for someone to say yes. Save the impact stats for your [donation page](https://www.causevox.com/blog/6-donation-page-kpis-you-should-be-tracking/), your event promo just needs to sell the experience.

### 5\. Celebrate What You’ve Built Together

<iframe title="Thistle Farms_ How Love Heals" src="https://player.vimeo.com/video/415741213?dnt=1&amp;app_id=122963" width="1140" height="641" frameborder="0" allow="autoplay; fullscreen; picture-in-picture; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; web-share"></iframe>

[*Thistle Farms*](https://thistlefarms.org/)

Thistle Farms is a nonprofit social enterprise dedicated to helping women survivors recover and heal from prostitution, trafficking, and addiction, providing a safe place to live, meaningful work, and a lifelong sisterhood of support. Their impact video doesn’t just report on the year. It takes you inside it.

You see the founder speaking to the heart of the mission. You see women at work, and woven throughout, you hear from them share how their lives have changed. It’s part thank-you, part proof, part invitation, and it works because it shows every dimension of the organization in one cohesive piece. By the end, you don’t just understand what Thistle Farms does. You understand what it *is*.

**What to steal:** An impact video is one of the most versatile assets you can create. It retains [existing donors](https://www.causevox.com/blog/major-gifts-through-donor-stewardship/), [attracts new ones](https://www.causevox.com/blog/6-ways-to-find-new-donors/), and tells your full story in a way a [donation page](https://www.causevox.com/donation-forms/) never could. Show the people, the place, and the work, not just the numbers. Let your community see exactly what they’re part of.

### 6\. Showcase Dollar-to-Impact

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aFzWfl8Q3F8)

[*Matthew 10 International*](https://www.matthew10.com/)

Over 4.4 million people are trapped in bonded labor at brick kilns across Pakistan, many enslaved for generations over debts they can never repay. Matthew 10 International works to purchase families’ freedom and establish them in a new life. Their fundraising video brings donors face to face with that reality in a way a statistic never could.

The video follows the stories of those enslaved in the brick kilns. They describe a man whose family has been enslaved for 14 years, all because his eight-year-old daughter needed a $500 surgery. She’s now 22. The family is still there. No words, no inflation of drama needed. The situation speaks for itself.

But here’s what makes this a dollar-to-impact masterpiece: $3,750 purchases a family’s debt, buys them a loader cart to start their own business, covers first month’s rent, new clothing, a freedom celebration, and ongoing pastoral support. Every dollar has a destination. Donors aren’t giving to a cause, they’re setting a specific family free. The campaign has already freed 71 people across four families, and every name has a face.

**What to steal:** The more precisely you can answer “what does my gift actually do,” the easier it is for someone to give. Break your budget into [donor-sized units](https://www.causevox.com/blog/best-practices-for-converting-donors-through-donation-tiers/), put a face on each one (with [beneficiary permission](https://www.causevox.com/blog/storytelling-in-fundraising-campaigns/) of course), and show donors the exact chain of events their gift sets in motion.

### 7\. Keep It Raw and Real

<iframe title="Sona's Story" src="https://player.vimeo.com/video/842241613?dnt=1&amp;app_id=122963" width="1140" height="641" frameborder="0" allow="autoplay; fullscreen; picture-in-picture; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; web-share"></iframe>

*[Ally Global](https://vimeo.com/842241613)*

Ally Global Foundation works in Nepal and Cambodia to rescue children from sex trafficking and walk survivors toward independent futures. Their video doesn’t open with a statistic. It opens with Sona’s voice: *“They said, if you don’t do this work, we will kill you.”*

**What to steal:** Raw and real means getting out of the way. If someone’s willing to speak, let them. One person, their story, the pauses and weight that come with it, that moves a donor more than anything you could produce around them.

### 8\. Let Your Founder Tell the Story, And Make Your Community the Hero

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Kddd-qY-Jg)

[*Empower*](https://weareempower.org/)

Cara Belvin lost her mother at nine years old. She didn’t know anyone her age who could relate. “I may as well have lived on planet Mars,” she says in Empower’s founder video. That loneliness, and the absence of anyone who could say you will survive this, is exactly what led her to build the only mentor program in the world specifically for kids in grief.

But here’s what makes this video land as a community-as-heroes piece: Cara doesn’t position herself as the hero. She points directly to her volunteers. “When I see these men and women who are ready to mentor and really be in service, that inspires me every day. That’s probably the reason I keep doing it.” The mentor community *is* the mission. The founder’s job is simply to show up, say thank you, and invite more people in.

**What to steal:** Your founder’s personal story can be your most powerful [fundraising](https://www.causevox.com/blog/connected-fundraising/) asset, but only if it leads somewhere bigger than themselves. Share the why behind the work, then hand the credit to the people making it happen. Donors don’t want to fund an organization. They want to join a community of people doing something that matters.

### 9\. Raise Awareness with Creativity

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1e8xgF0JtVg)

[*Girl Effect*](https://www.girleffect.org/)

This animated video uses only text and music to make its case for investing in adolescent girls in developing countries. No interviews. No narrator. No footage from the field. Just smart writing, compelling animation, and a building sense of urgency that lands like a punch.

It proves something important: you don’t need cameras on the ground to tell a powerful story. Sometimes a whiteboard and a great script is enough.

**What to steal:** If you can’t film in the field, get creative with how you represent your work. Animation, data visualization, illustrated storytelling, all of these can work beautifully on a small budget.

### 10\. Use Humor (Really)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qSElmEmEjb4)

[*Mama Hope*](https://www.mamahope.org/)

This video from Mama Hope features Kenyan men doing their best impressions of Hollywood action heroes, and it’s hilarious. But the backstory makes it even better.

After watching action films, Gabriel, Benard, Brian, and Derrik came to Mama Hope with an idea: they wanted to make a video that pokes fun at the way African men are portrayed in Hollywood. “If people believed only what they saw in movies, they would think we are all warlords who love violence.” They were tired of the over-sensationalized, one-dimensional depictions, and the white savior messaging that runs through so much nonprofit media. They wanted to tell their own story. So Mama Hope handed them the mic.

The result is a video that got shared millions of times because it’s genuinely fun to watch and the deeper message hits harder because you’re already smiling. They didn’t lecture anyone about stereotypes. They just laughed at them, on camera, on their own terms.

**What to steal:** Humor is underused in nonprofit video. If your mission and audience allow for it, don’t be afraid to make people laugh. Laughter builds trust. Trust drives donations.

### 1\. A Clear Goal Before You Press Record

Are you raising money for a specific project? Recruiting volunteers? Welcoming new donors? Know this before you write a single word of script. Your goal shapes everything.

### 2\. A Script, Even A Loose One

“We’ll just talk naturally” rarely works on camera. Write it out. Read it aloud. Have someone else read it. Then revise. Even if you end up improvising, the prep work shows.

### 3\. Music That Does Emotional Work

Silence is rarely your friend in video. The right music tells your viewer how to feel before your subject says a word. Take this seriously, and make sure you have the rights to whatever you use.

### 4\. One Real Story

You serve hundreds of people. We know. But your video should probably follow one of them. Specificity is what makes stories stick.

### 5\. A Single, Clear Call To Action

Donate. Sign. Share. Attend. Pick one. Your video’s job is to move someone toward that one thing, not everything at once.

### 6\. The Right Length (Hint: Shorter Than You Think)

Under 3 minutes for most fundraising videos. Under 90 seconds if you’re posting to social media. The goal isn’t to tell your whole story, it’s to make someone want to learn more.

### 7\. A Realistic Budget

Decide upfront what you can spend, what you can do in-house, and what you’ll outsource. A $500 video made with intention beats a $5,000 video made without a plan.

### 8\. A Distribution Plan

Your video isn’t done when you export it. Where will you post it? How will you [email](https://www.causevox.com/crm/) it? Will it live on your donation page? Plan this before you film, not after.

### 9\. Enough Courage To Hit Publish

Perfect is the enemy of good, and this is especially true in nonprofit video. The organizations whose videos move people are the ones who weren’t afraid to be a little vulnerable, a little unpolished, a little real. Be one of those organizations.
