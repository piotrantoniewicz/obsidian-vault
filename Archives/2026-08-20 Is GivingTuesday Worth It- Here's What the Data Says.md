It’s August, and that means it’s time for one of the fundraising world’s most reliable annual traditions: debating whether GivingTuesday is worth the effort.

Nonprofits skip GivingTuesday for all kinds of reasons. Some objectors say the day is too crowded, that participation in a fundraising campaign ahead of the year-end season feels risky, or that their efforts would be better spent elsewhere.

You don’t *need* a reason not to participate in GivingTuesday. If you and your team decide that doing so isn’t the wisest decision, that’s okay. But many of the common objections cited by GivingTuesday critics are based on myths, not data.

And, if you’re going to make the strategic decision not to run a GivingTuesday campaign, you deserve the realinformation that will help you make the best choice.

Here are four common myths that can discourage nonprofits from engaging their communities on GivingTuesday … and then what the data *actually* has to say.

## Myth #1: GivingTuesday Is Oversaturated

### The Reality

If you work for a nonprofit, you’re probably inundated with information about GivingTuesday. Industry thought leaders, tech companies, consultants, and random contacts on LinkedIn all talk about it. The internet is awhirl with webinars, blog posts, toolkits, templates, and planning worksheets.

When you’re immersed in something like that, it’s easy to assume your donors are just as aware of GivingTuesday as you are. In reality, they’re probably not.

### The Data

[Donation data from December of 2024](https://neonone.com/resources/guide/the-generosity-data-backed-insights-for-givingtuesday-and-year-end/) signals that your donors aren’t as swamped with GivingTuesday messaging as you might think.

We looked at the giving behavior of 37,499 people who donated during that month. Of that group, 1,178 of them (that’s 3.14%) made a gift on GivingTuesday.

If the objection that donors are inundated with GivingTuesday appeals was true, we’d expect that number to be a lot higher. Instead, this trend implies that only a small slice of donors are being invited to participate in a GivingTuesday campaign at all.

## Myth #2: Fundraising on GivingTuesday Will Hurt Your Year-End Campaign

### The Reality

There’s lots of room for you to connect with your donors on the big day! But will doing so harm your year-end fundraising?

No. This is one of the oldest myths floating around the sector, and it’s one GivingTuesday itself has already debunked. Their own analysis of donor behavior found that organizations participating in GivingTuesday saw [an overall increase in end-of-year giving](https://www.givingtuesday.org/blog/making-the-case-for-givingtuesday/), not a decrease.

### The Data

Neon One’s own data backs this up.

Of the 1,178 people in our donor panel who gave on GivingTuesday 2024, 562 of them (47.71%) made at least one additional gift before the end of December.

Nearly half of GivingTuesday donors give again in the same month. And, when you combine this with the finding that participating organizations see an overall lift in end-of-year fundraising, the story emerges that GivingTuesday doesn’t steal from your year-end campaign. It may even enhance it.

This holds true for recurring gifts, too. Our [2024 Recurring Giving Report](https://neonone.com/resources/recurring-giving-report/) found that 80.33% of people who initiated a recurring donation on GivingTuesday made at least one additional donation to the same nonprofit before the end of the year.

## Myth #3: Inboxes Are Too Crowded to Stand Out

### The Reality

This is a common concern for nonprofits and the consultants that support them. And it’s understandable! If you’re deeply involved in supporting multiple nonprofits—even if you’re just subscribed to their emails without donating—you’re going to see a lot of GivingTuesday emails in your inbox.

But most people are not getting dozens of GivingTuesday appeals, and they actually *open* the ones they do receive.

### The Data

In [our research into nearly 100,000 donors and their giving behavior](https://neonone.com/resources/the-generosity-report/) over a five-year period of time, we found that 29.75% of them supported more than one nonprofit. Most people who supported multiple organizations gave to just two organizations.

Even if you feel like you’re “competing” for a spot in someone’s inbox, the competition probably isn’t as intense as you think.

We used quotation marks around “competing” for a reason. One, it’s important to remember that it’s not a competition. And, two, you don’t have to compete—people will read your emails.

We know this because of some research we did into email engagement on GivingTuesday itself. When those emails do land in inboxes, people engage with them.

In 2023, we found that [GivingTuesday email performance](https://neonone.com/resources/blog/giving-tuesday-emails/) actually outpaced the year before it, even though nonprofits sent more emails than ever. Open rates jumped from 28.71% in 2022 to 35.92% in 2023—a 25% increase. Click-through rates jumped even further, from 1.63% to 3.08%, an 89% increase.

When we compared those engagement rates to the performance of emails sent during the rest of the year, there was no significant difference between messages sent on GivingTuesday and those sent on other days.

## Myth #4: GivingTuesday Isn’t Especially Useful for Building Your Donor Base

### The Reality

GivingTuesday is one of the biggest [donor acquisition days](https://neonone.com/resources/blog/donor-acquisition-tips/) of the entire year, and the donors it brings in tend to stick around longer than most.

### The Data

In 2022, GivingTuesday became [the single leading day for new donor acquisition](https://www.givingtuesday.org/blog/making-the-case-for-givingtuesday/), surpassing December 31st for the first time.

And these aren’t donors who give once and disappear. [A 2025 report](https://www.givingtuesday.org/blog/blackbaud-givingtuesday/) from the Blackbaud Institute found that GivingTuesday donors have a 65% repeat giving rate, 13 percentage points higher than the 52% repeat rate across all donors.

When you run a GivingTuesday campaign, you’re participating in one of the best days for new donor acquisition *and* reaching a group of people who are more likely to stay engaged with you for a long period of time.

## So, Is GivingTuesday Worth It?

The answer to this question is going to depend entirely on your goals and team dynamic. But we can tell you that a lot of the reasons commonly cited by detractors aren’t backed up by data.

Years of research signal that GivingTuesday isn’t too crowded, your donors aren’t tired of hearing about it, and participating won’t cannibalize your December fundraising. If anything, it will achieve the opposite.

You don’t have to treat GivingTuesday like a make-or-break, all-hands-on-deck event that is independent of all the other activities you have planned for the end of the year. Think of it less like a sprint and more like the first mile of a much longer race.

It’s the unofficial start of the year-end giving season, and it’s an opportunity to reach new and existing donors alike on one of the biggest giving days of the year. Show up, invite people to support a cause they love, and let the momentum carry you into the rest of December.
